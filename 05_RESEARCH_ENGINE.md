## Document Control

**Document Type:** Permanent Research Intelligence System  
**Governing Specifications:** `PROJECT_PRD.md`, `01_BRAND_BIBLE.md`, `02_BUDDHIST_GUIDE.md`, `03_CONTENT_ARCHITECTURE.md`, and `04_CONTENT_ENGINE.md`

**Required Output of Research:** A traceable Knowledge Packet accepted by the Content Engine  
**Supported Domains:** Buddhist scriptures, commentaries, doctrine, history, traditions, rituals, practices, figures, ethics, psychology, filial piety, karma, compassion, wisdom, meditation, modern application, and future related domains  
**Excluded Outputs:** Scripts, episode ideas, thumbnails, prompts, unsupported content claims, and speculative conclusions  
**Permanent Standard:** No Buddhist content should ever bypass this Research Engine

## Operating Premise

The Research Engine is the knowledge foundation of the Buddhist AI Studio. It exists because Buddhist content cannot be produced responsibly from memory, intuition, online fragments, or AI confidence. Buddhism is a vast family of traditions, languages, scriptures, commentaries, rituals, practices, histories, symbols, and living communities. It includes canonical teachings, devotional narratives, historical uncertainties, philosophical debates, regional variations, and modern interpretations. A studio that publishes for many years must therefore research with humility, traceability, and discipline.

This document defines the complete research operating system. It tells future AI agents what sources to prefer, how to classify evidence, how to compare translations, how to handle disagreement, how to prevent fake quotations, how to assign confidence levels, how to build Knowledge Packets, how to maintain a knowledge graph, and when to stop, escalate, or remain silent.

The Research Engine sits before the Content Engine. The Content Engine may transform knowledge into meaningful experiences only after research has produced a verified Knowledge Packet. If a Knowledge Packet does not exist, the content is not ready. If the Knowledge Packet is incomplete, uncertain, or high-risk, the content must be narrowed, escalated, or delayed.

The most important rule is simple:

**AI output is never evidence.**

AI may organize, compare, summarize, and draft research notes, but it cannot serve as a source. Every substantive claim must be traceable to an appropriate evidence basis or clearly marked as editorial reflection.

# PART 1 - Research Philosophy

## Why Research Exists

Research exists to protect Buddhist truth, viewer trust, and editorial integrity. The channel's mission requires more than beautiful narration and cinematic form. It requires that teachings be represented accurately, humbly, and with respect for tradition. Research is the discipline that prevents the channel from becoming vague spirituality, superstition, false certainty, or AI-generated religious fiction.

Research also protects viewers. A mistaken statement about karma can shame someone in pain. A false quote attributed to the Buddha can mislead thousands of viewers. A careless claim about ritual can create superstition. A simplified historical claim can erase tradition-specific complexity. A medical or psychological overclaim can harm vulnerable people. Research is therefore not only intellectual. It is ethical.

Research exists before content because content amplifies knowledge. A private misunderstanding is small. A published misunderstanding becomes part of the viewer's spiritual environment. The studio must therefore treat research as a form of stewardship.

Research should produce three outcomes:

**Clarity:** What can be responsibly said?

**Boundary:** What cannot be responsibly said?

**Confidence:** How certain is the evidence?

Without these outcomes, content should not proceed.

## Truth Before Speed

The studio must prefer slower verified knowledge over fast plausible output. Speed is useful only after the research system is stable. In Buddhist content, a rushed claim can become a long-term error embedded in scripts, subtitles, Shorts, quotes, articles, and community posts.

Truth before speed means:

**Do not draft from memory when sources are needed.**

**Do not use AI fluency as confidence.**

**Do not use a single weak web page for doctrinal claims.**

**Do not publish high-risk topics without review.**

**Do not simplify uncertainty into certainty for production convenience.**

If a source cannot be verified, the research record must say so. If a claim requires more time, take more time. If a topic cannot be responsibly researched within available resources, narrow the scope.

Speed may help with retrieval, formatting, and comparison, but it must never outrank evidence.

## Humility Before Certainty

Buddhist research often encounters uncertainty. Texts have multiple translations. Traditions interpret teachings differently. Historical records may be limited. Devotional narratives may not be historical biographies. Modern psychological interpretations may be useful but not canonical. Scholarship may disagree.

Humility before certainty means the researcher must not force closure when the evidence does not support closure. A responsible Knowledge Packet can say:

**The tradition presents this as...**

**Scholars differ on...**

**This appears in later devotional tradition rather than early historical records...**

**The available sources do not support a stronger claim...**

**This should be framed as editorial reflection, not doctrine...**

Humility is not weakness. It is a mark of trustworthiness. Viewers can handle nuance when it is explained clearly. Future AI agents must be trained to treat uncertainty as information, not as a defect to hide.

## Evidence Before Opinion

Opinion may appear only after evidence is understood and classified. The studio may produce editorial reflections, modern applications, symbolic interpretations, and psychological bridges, but these must not be confused with scripture, commentary, history, or scientific research.

Evidence before opinion means:

**Find the source before forming the claim.**

**Classify the source before interpreting it.**

**Compare relevant sources before resolving conflict.**

**Assign confidence before content adaptation.**

**Label editorial reflection internally and, when needed, publicly.**

An AI agent may suggest an interpretation, but it must identify the evidence basis. If no evidence supports the interpretation, it remains a creative reflection and must be bounded accordingly.

## Knowledge Stewardship

The studio is not merely consuming information. It is stewarding Buddhist knowledge for viewers across years. Stewardship requires care, memory, and responsibility.

Knowledge stewardship includes:

**Preserving source trails**

**Maintaining glossary consistency**

**Recording translation choices**

**Documenting uncertainty**

**Correcting errors**

**Avoiding source contamination**

**Protecting sacred material from sensationalism**

**Respecting living traditions**

**Preventing AI hallucination from entering the archive**

Stewardship also means not using every available piece of information. Some teachings require context. Some practices require teachers. Some traditions have restrictions. Some claims are too uncertain. Some stories are meaningful but easily distorted. The researcher must know when preservation means restraint.

## Long-term Knowledge Preservation

The Research Engine must support thousands of assets over a decade. This requires knowledge preservation beyond individual projects.

Every research cycle should leave behind structured knowledge:

**Source records**

**Citation notes**

**Knowledge Packets**

**Concept definitions**

**Translation decisions**

**Confidence scores**

**Conflict notes**

**Reviewer notes**

**Deprecation records**

**Update triggers**

The studio should assume future AI agents will inherit this knowledge. Therefore, notes must be written for retrieval, not only for the current writer. A source note that only says "found online" is useless. A note that identifies title, translator, passage, source category, supported claim, confidence, and limitations becomes infrastructure.

Long-term preservation also requires correction memory. When an error is found, the correction must be recorded so future assets do not repeat it. A mature research system learns.

# PART 2 - Source Hierarchy

## Purpose of the Source Hierarchy

The source hierarchy ranks sources according to reliability, authority, and appropriate use. It does not mean lower tiers are never useful. It means different claims require different evidence. A canonical claim needs canonical support. A historical claim needs historical evidence. A modern psychological claim needs psychological or scientific sources. A cultural practice claim may require ethnographic, temple, or community sources. A devotional interpretation may require traditional sources.

Future AI agents must never treat all sources as equal. A blog post, social media quote, or unsourced website cannot carry the same weight as a sutra translation, traditional commentary, academic monograph, or peer-reviewed article.

## Tier 1: Canonical Buddhist Scriptures

**Priority:** Highest for claims about what a recognized Buddhist scripture teaches, narrates, names, or presents.

**Includes:** Pāli Canon texts, Chinese Āgamas, Mahāyāna sutras, Pure Land sutras, Kinh Địa Tạng, Kinh Kim Cang, Kinh Pháp Hoa, Kinh A Di Đà, Kinh Dược Sư, Heart Sutra, Dhammapada, Jātaka materials where relevant, and other recognized scriptural collections.

**Use cases:** Scriptural explanation, doctrine in textual context, sacred narratives, vows, parables, dialogues, practices presented in scripture, symbolic imagery, and devotional language rooted in text.

**Reliability:** High for what the text says in a given translation or recension. Not automatically high for modern historical authorship, universal Buddhist consensus, or scientific claims.

**Limitations:** Scriptures exist in different languages, recensions, translations, and traditions. A text canonical in one tradition may not function the same way in another. A translated word may carry interpretive choices. A scriptural narrative may be sacred without being modern historical evidence.

**Rules:** Quote only from verified translations or editions. Identify text and translation. Compare translations when wording matters. Do not fabricate passages. Do not attribute a teaching to all Buddhism when the source is tradition-specific.

## Tier 2: Traditional Commentaries

**Priority:** Highest for how a tradition interprets scripture or doctrine.

**Includes:** Classical commentaries, subcommentaries, lineage explanations, traditional exegesis, scholastic treatises, and respected doctrinal manuals within Buddhist traditions.

**Use cases:** Explaining interpretive traditions, doctrinal nuance, practice meaning, ritual context, symbolic reading, philosophical debate, and lineage-specific understanding.

**Reliability:** High for representing a tradition's interpretation when the commentary is authentic and properly contextualized.

**Limitations:** Commentaries may differ across schools. They are not always identical with scripture. They may require specialized knowledge. A commentary from one tradition should not be presented as universal Buddhist interpretation.

**Rules:** Label commentary as commentary. Do not present commentary as direct scriptural text unless quoting scripture within it. Identify tradition and, where possible, author, text, and historical context.

## Tier 3: Recognized Buddhist Scholars

**Priority:** High for doctrinal explanation, translation insight, historical interpretation, and comparative understanding.

**Includes:** Recognized Buddhist studies scholars, translators, monastic scholars, lineage teachers with documented expertise, and established authors whose work is respected in Buddhist studies or communities.

**Use cases:** Explaining complex doctrine, translation variants, historical development, Buddhist philosophy, practice context, comparative traditions, and modern interpretation.

**Reliability:** High when the scholar is working within their expertise and sources are cited.

**Limitations:** Scholars may disagree. Some are stronger in history than practice; others are stronger in practice than historical method. A respected teacher's Dharma talk may be spiritually valuable but not a primary historical source.

**Rules:** Identify whether the source is academic, monastic, translation, commentary, or contemporary teaching. Do not treat one scholar as final authority when the field has disagreement.

## Tier 4: Academic Publications

**Priority:** High for historical, textual, linguistic, sociological, archaeological, comparative religion, and psychological research.

**Includes:** Peer-reviewed articles, university press books, academic monographs, critical editions, dissertations with caution, conference proceedings with caution, and research by recognized institutions.

**Use cases:** Historical timelines, textual dating, transmission history, archaeology, social context, comparative analysis, psychology of meditation, and cultural studies.

**Reliability:** High when peer-reviewed or published by reputable academic presses, especially when claims are specific and evidence-based.

**Limitations:** Academic research may not reflect devotional truth as lived by practitioners. Scholarship may be contested or revised. Academic framing can be reductive if used without sensitivity to tradition.

**Rules:** Use academic sources for academic claims. Do not use academic uncertainty to mock devotion. Do not use preliminary studies to make broad claims.

## Tier 5: Historical Records

**Priority:** High for historical claims when records are authentic, contextualized, and corroborated.

**Includes:** Inscriptions, travel records, temple records, biographies, dynastic histories, archaeological reports, transmission documents, dated manuscripts, and primary historical sources.

**Use cases:** Historical Buddha research, spread of Buddhism, regional transmission, temple history, monastic institutions, translation history, and cultural practice development.

**Reliability:** Variable. A historical record may be primary but still biased, legendary, incomplete, or written long after events.

**Limitations:** Historical records require interpretation. Hagiography is not the same as neutral biography. Official records may reflect political interests. Later accounts may preserve devotion rather than verifiable fact.

**Rules:** Classify record type. Check date, authorship, context, and corroboration. Avoid false precision. Distinguish historical evidence from traditional memory.

## Tier 6: Educational Organizations

**Priority:** Moderate to high depending on the organization.

**Includes:** Universities, Buddhist educational institutions, museum education pages, reputable monasteries, Buddhist associations, translation foundations, digital canon projects, and established non-profit educational resources.

**Use cases:** Introductory context, translations, glossaries, historical overviews, practice explanations, museum object context, and source access.

**Reliability:** Good when transparent, sourced, and institutionally credible.

**Limitations:** Some organizations may simplify heavily, represent one tradition, or present devotional material without distinguishing source categories. Institutional credibility varies.

**Rules:** Use as supporting sources, not sole authority for high-risk claims unless the organization is directly authoritative for that text or tradition. Check whether content has citations.

## Tier 7: General Reference Sources

**Priority:** Low to moderate. Useful for orientation, not final authority.

**Includes:** Encyclopedias, general reference works, reputable overview websites, library guides, and broad educational summaries.

**Use cases:** Initial orientation, finding keywords, identifying sources, checking broad dates, locating terminology, and building a research plan.

**Reliability:** Variable. General references often simplify, omit controversies, or include unsourced statements.

**Limitations:** Not sufficient for direct scriptural, doctrinal, historical, or sensitive claims. Wikipedia dependency is prohibited as a final research basis.

**Rules:** Use only as a starting point. Trace claims to stronger sources. Never cite general reference as the only support for high-risk content.

## Tier 8: Unacceptable Sources

**Priority:** Not acceptable as evidence.

**Includes:** Unsourced social media posts, quote graphics, AI-generated summaries, anonymous blogs, sensational spiritual websites, miracle marketing pages, fortune-telling content, sectarian attack sites, conspiracy sites, commercial ritual guarantee pages, fake quote collections, and content with no traceable sources.

**Use cases:** None for evidence. Such sources may be used only as examples of misinformation to avoid, and only internally.

**Reliability:** Unacceptable.

**Limitations:** High risk of fabricated quotes, superstition, sectarian bias, medical misinformation, fear bait, and source contamination.

**Rules:** Do not use for claims. Do not import their wording. Do not treat popularity as credibility. If an idea appears only in Tier 8 sources, reject it or seek stronger evidence.

## Source Contamination Rules

Source contamination occurs when weak source claims enter research notes and become treated as credible because they sound familiar. To prevent this:

**Separate orientation notes from verified evidence.**

**Label weak sources clearly.**

**Do not copy language from unacceptable sources.**

**Trace every claim upward to stronger tiers.**

**Remove claims that cannot be traced.**

**Record rejected claims when they are likely to recur.**

The research archive should not become a mixture of verified and unverified material. Clear labeling is mandatory.

# PART 3 - Research Workflow

## Overview

The research workflow converts a question into a Knowledge Packet. It must be completed before the Content Engine begins content development. The workflow is:

**Question**

**Research Planning**

**Source Collection**

**Source Classification**

**Cross-checking**

**Conflict Detection**

**Evidence Evaluation**

**Confidence Scoring**

**Knowledge Extraction**

**Knowledge Packet**

**Content Engine**

Each step has a distinct purpose. Skipping steps creates hallucination risk.

## Step 1: Question

Research begins with a question, not a topic label. A topic label such as karma, Kinh Địa Tạng, or meditation is too broad. The research question defines the scope.

Good research questions identify:

**The teaching or issue being examined**

**The tradition or text involved**

**The viewer need**

**The claim type**

**The intended depth**

For example, a research question might ask what a specific sutra says about a vow, how a tradition interprets merit dedication, or how modern research cautiously describes mindfulness and attention. The question must not be so broad that research becomes unfocused.

If the question involves sensitive topics, the risk flag must be added immediately.

## Step 2: Research Planning

Research planning defines what evidence is needed before source collection. The plan should identify likely source tiers, key terms, languages, traditions, and escalation needs.

The plan must answer:

**What kind of claim is being researched?**

**Which source tiers are required?**

**Which traditions may differ?**

**Which languages or translations matter?**

**What would count as sufficient evidence?**

**What would require human review?**

The plan prevents random searching. It also prevents AI from collecting sources that are easy to find rather than sources that are appropriate.

## Step 3: Source Collection

Source collection gathers evidence according to the plan. Collect stronger sources first whenever possible. Use lower-tier sources only for orientation or when the claim type is appropriate.

For each source, record:

**Title**

**Author or translator**

**Tradition or institution**

**Publication or edition**

**Date if available**

**Source tier**

**Relevant passage or section**

**Claim supported**

**Limitations**

Do not collect sources without notes. A pile of links is not research.

## Step 4: Source Classification

Classify every source by tier and claim type. A source may be strong for one purpose and weak for another. A sutra is strong for what the sutra says but not automatically strong for historical dating. A scholar may be strong for history but not for lived ritual practice. A temple page may be useful for that temple's practice but not for universal doctrine.

Classification should include:

**Canonical**

**Commentarial**

**Scholarly**

**Historical**

**Devotional**

**Cultural**

**Psychological**

**Scientific**

**Editorial**

This classification must be preserved in the Knowledge Packet.

## Step 5: Cross-checking

Cross-checking verifies claims across sources. The stronger the claim, the more cross-checking is required.

Cross-check:

**Exact quotations against the primary text**

**Translation wording against alternate translations**

**Historical claims against academic or primary historical sources**

**Doctrinal interpretations against commentary or recognized teachers**

**Modern psychological claims against research literature**

**Cultural practice claims against tradition-specific sources**

If only one source supports a claim, confidence should be limited unless the source is definitive for that claim. For example, a canonical passage can support that the text says something, but interpretation may still require additional sources.

## Step 6: Conflict Detection

Conflict detection identifies disagreement. Conflict may arise between translations, traditions, scholars, histories, devotional narratives, and modern interpretations.

Types of conflict include:

**Textual conflict**

**Translation conflict**

**Doctrinal conflict**

**Historical conflict**

**Ritual practice conflict**

**Interpretive conflict**

**Scientific evidence conflict**

Conflict is not a problem to hide. It is data. The Knowledge Packet must record conflict and recommend how content should present it.

## Step 7: Evidence Evaluation

Evidence evaluation judges the strength of support for each claim.

Evaluate:

**Source tier**

**Source relevance**

**Author expertise**

**Tradition context**

**Date and proximity**

**Corroboration**

**Translation reliability**

**Possible bias**

**Scope limits**

**Risk level**

A high-tier source used outside its scope may not support the claim. A strong quote may still be misleading if removed from context. Evaluation must consider not only whether a source exists, but whether it supports the exact claim being made.

## Step 8: Confidence Scoring

Assign confidence to each major statement: Very High, High, Medium, Low, or Unknown. Confidence must be based on evidence, not writer preference.

Confidence scoring determines how content may phrase the claim. Very High and High claims may be stated clearly. Medium claims need caution or context. Low claims should usually be omitted or framed as uncertain. Unknown claims should not be presented as knowledge.

## Step 9: Knowledge Extraction

Knowledge extraction turns evidence into reusable units:

**Definitions**

**Concepts**

**Relationships**

**Stories**

**Symbols**

**Practices**

**Ethical principles**

**Psychological insights**

**Historical context**

**Common misunderstandings**

**Modern relevance**

Extraction must preserve source links and confidence. Do not separate a claim from its evidence.

## Step 10: Knowledge Packet

The Knowledge Packet is the required output of the Research Engine. It organizes the research into a structured form the Content Engine can use. It includes topic, question, definitions, sources, contexts, confidence levels, citations, editorial notes, and risk flags.

A content asset may not proceed without a Knowledge Packet. If the packet is incomplete, the asset is not ready.

## Step 11: Content Engine

Only after the Knowledge Packet is complete may the Content Engine transform the research into a content experience. The Content Engine may simplify, sequence, narrate, adapt, and reflect, but it may not invent knowledge outside the packet without returning to research.

# PART 4 - Canonical Scripture Research

## General Scripture Rules

Canonical scripture research requires the highest discipline. Scriptures are sacred sources and must not be used casually, misquoted, or blended without context.

Rules:

**Identify the text by recognized title.**

**Identify the tradition or canon family.**

**Identify the translation or edition.**

**Distinguish quotation from paraphrase.**

**Compare translations when wording matters.**

**Do not quote from unsourced websites.**

**Do not attribute modern sayings to scriptures.**

**Do not universalize tradition-specific texts.**

**Preserve uncertainty about dating or authorship.**

Scriptural research must be both reverent and precise.

## Kinh Địa Tạng Research

Kinh Địa Tạng research should focus on filial piety, vows, karmic responsibility, hell realms, merit dedication, compassion for beings in suffering, and the relationship between the living and deceased. Because this text contains emotionally sensitive material, research must include safety framing.

Research requirements:

**Use reliable Vietnamese, Chinese, or English translations where available.**

**Identify passage context before interpretation.**

**Distinguish the sutra's narrative from modern claims.**

**Handle hell realm descriptions with compassion-first framing.**

**Avoid using the text to frighten viewers.**

**Avoid claims about specific deceased persons.**

**Cross-link filial piety to boundaries and family complexity.**

For content about Kinh Địa Tạng, the Knowledge Packet must include risk flags for karma, hell realms, death, grief, merit dedication, and filial piety when relevant.

## Kinh Kim Cang Research

Kinh Kim Cang research should focus on emptiness, non-attachment, non-abiding mind, generosity without clinging, non-self, and the limits of conceptual identity. Translation precision matters because terms can be philosophically subtle.

Research requirements:

**Compare translations when discussing key phrases.**

**Avoid explaining emptiness as nothingness.**

**Contextualize Prajñāpāramitā tradition.**

**Distinguish philosophical explanation from modern self-help.**

**Avoid overconfident simplification.**

If a content asset uses a famous phrase, exact wording must be verified. If translation variants differ meaningfully, the packet must record them.

## Kinh Pháp Hoa Research

Kinh Pháp Hoa research should focus on skillful means, one vehicle, Buddha nature or universal awakening potential where tradition-appropriate, parables, Bodhisattva path, faith, devotion, and cosmic narrative. The text has rich symbolic and doctrinal layers.

Research requirements:

**Identify parable context.**

**Distinguish narrative, doctrine, and devotional interpretation.**

**Avoid flattening the sutra into one slogan.**

**Use commentary or scholarship for complex doctrinal claims.**

**Handle cosmic imagery symbolically and devotionally without spectacle.**

Because Kinh Pháp Hoa is interpreted differently across East Asian traditions, research should record tradition-specific readings when relevant.

## Kinh A Di Đà Research

Kinh A Di Đà research should focus on Amitābha Buddha, Sukhāvatī, Pure Land aspiration, faith, vows, recitation, remembrance, and hope at death. It must avoid guarantees about rebirth.

Research requirements:

**Identify Pure Land tradition context.**

**Compare translations of key terms related to faith, vows, and practice.**

**Avoid presenting nianfo or niệm Phật as a worldly success formula.**

**Avoid guaranteeing the viewer's rebirth or the deceased's state.**

**Connect Pure Land practice to humility, aspiration, ethics, and devotion.**

Pure Land research should be devotional without becoming transactional.

## Kinh Dược Sư Research

Kinh Dược Sư research should focus on healing, vows, light, ethical restoration, compassion, illness, protection language, and Medicine Buddha devotional practice. It requires strong medical boundary controls.

Research requirements:

**Distinguish devotional healing language from medical claims.**

**Do not claim cure.**

**Identify vows and their textual context.**

**Use careful language around illness and suffering.**

**Include medical and mental health boundary notes where relevant.**

The Knowledge Packet for Medicine Buddha content must flag medical overclaim risk.

## Nikāya Research

Nikāya research should use reliable translations and identify the specific collection and discourse where possible. It is especially relevant for Four Noble Truths, Noble Eightfold Path, dependent arising, mindfulness, ethics, meditation, impermanence, non-self, and early Buddhist teachings.

Research requirements:

**Identify discourse title and collection.**

**Avoid quoting without exact reference.**

**Compare translations for key Pāli terms when necessary.**

**Avoid presenting all Buddhist traditions as identical to one early source.**

**Use Nikāya sources carefully in Mahāyāna contexts.**

Nikāya research can provide strong foundations, but must be integrated with tradition-aware framing.

## Āgama Research

Āgama research is important for comparative early Buddhist material preserved in Chinese and other traditions. It can help broaden beyond Pāli-only framing.

Research requirements:

**Identify the Āgama collection and translation context.**

**Use reliable translations or scholarly sources.**

**Compare with Nikāya parallels when relevant.**

**Avoid overclaiming if translation access is limited.**

Āgama research often requires higher expertise. Escalate when necessary.

## Mahāyāna Sutra Research

Mahāyāna sutra research must identify tradition context, translation history when relevant, and doctrinal themes. Mahāyāna texts often include symbolic, devotional, cosmic, and philosophical layers.

Research requirements:

**Identify the specific sutra and translation.**

**Respect Mahāyāna internal logic.**

**Avoid treating cosmic narrative as spectacle.**

**Use commentary or scholarship for complex claims.**

**Distinguish historical scholarship from devotional authority.**

Mahāyāna research should preserve reverence and clarity together.

## Translation Comparison Rules

When comparing translations:

**Identify each translation.**

**Compare key terms, not every word.**

**Ask whether wording differences affect meaning.**

**Record significant variants.**

**Avoid building major claims on one ambiguous word.**

**Escalate if the original language matters and expertise is lacking.**

Translation comparison should help content become clearer, not burden viewers with unnecessary detail.

## Fake Quotation Prevention

To avoid fake quotations:

**Never quote from memory.**

**Never use quote graphics as sources.**

**Never trust AI-generated citations.**

**Verify exact wording against a reliable source.**

**Record source, translator, and location.**

**If uncertain, paraphrase cautiously or omit.**

**Do not attribute editorial reflections to scripture.**

Fake scripture attribution is a critical failure. It must be treated as a publication-blocking issue.

# PART 5 - Historical Research

## Historical Research Principles

Historical research must distinguish evidence, tradition, legend, devotion, and interpretation. Buddhism's history spans India, Central Asia, Sri Lanka, Southeast Asia, China, Vietnam, Korea, Japan, Tibet, and global modernity. No single source or tradition captures the whole.

Historical claims require source support. The more specific the claim, the stronger the evidence required. Exact dates, conversations, motives, and biographies are often uncertain. The research packet must not create false precision.

## Historical Buddha

Research on the historical Buddha must distinguish between historically plausible reconstruction and canonical or later traditional narratives.

Research requirements:

**Use recognized scholarship for historical claims.**

**Use canonical sources for traditional life narratives.**

**Avoid claiming every detail is historically verified.**

**Avoid reducing the Buddha to a modern philosopher or therapist.**

**Respect devotional significance while preserving historical honesty.**

The Knowledge Packet should classify each life detail: historically supported, canonical narrative, later tradition, devotional story, or uncertain.

## Bodhisattvas

Bodhisattva research must distinguish devotional figures, scriptural roles, symbolic meanings, and historical persons where applicable. Many Bodhisattvas are not historical biographies in the modern sense.

Research requirements:

**Identify the figure's primary tradition context.**

**Identify associated sutras, vows, virtues, and practices.**

**Avoid inventing biography.**

**Avoid superhero framing.**

**Preserve devotional respect.**

The packet should identify whether the content treats the Bodhisattva devotionally, symbolically, doctrinally, or historically.

## Indian Buddhism

Indian Buddhist history research should cover early Buddhism, councils with caution, monastic communities, development of schools, Mahāyāna emergence, Buddhist philosophy, universities, art, transmission, and decline in India where relevant.

Research requirements:

**Use academic and historical sources.**

**Avoid simplistic timelines.**

**Acknowledge uncertainty and scholarly debate.**

**Do not present later sectarian narratives as neutral history.**

**Connect history to teaching only when evidence supports it.**

## Chinese Buddhism

Chinese Buddhism research should include translation history, schools, sutra reception, Pure Land, Chan, Tiantai, Huayan, monastic culture, ritual, and interaction with Confucian and Daoist contexts when relevant.

Research requirements:

**Use sources knowledgeable about Chinese Buddhist history.**

**Distinguish Chinese adaptation from Indian origins.**

**Respect regional development as legitimate, not merely derivative.**

**Avoid flattening Chinese Buddhism into one tradition.**

## Vietnamese Buddhism

Vietnamese Buddhism research is essential for the channel's audience. It may involve Thiền, Pure Land, devotional practice, Kinh Địa Tạng, Vu Lan, ancestor remembrance, temple culture, monastic history, lay practice, and modern Vietnamese Buddhist life.

Research requirements:

**Preserve Vietnamese terminology and diacritics.**

**Use Vietnamese Buddhist sources where appropriate.**

**Distinguish doctrine, culture, and local practice.**

**Avoid treating all Vietnamese practices as superstition.**

**Avoid universalizing Vietnamese forms as all Buddhism.**

Vietnamese Buddhist research should honor cultural memory while maintaining doctrinal clarity.

## Japanese Buddhism

Japanese Buddhist research may include Zen, Pure Land, Nichiren, Tendai, Shingon, monastic and lay developments, ritual, art, and modern global influence.

Research requirements:

**Identify specific school.**

**Avoid using "Zen" as a generic synonym for calm.**

**Avoid flattening Japanese Buddhism into aesthetics.**

**Use appropriate scholarship or tradition-specific sources.**

## Tibetan Buddhism

Tibetan Buddhist research requires special care because of Vajrayāna, tantric practice, lineage, initiation, and restricted teachings.

Research requirements:

**Avoid instructing restricted practices.**

**Use qualified sources.**

**Identify school or lineage where relevant.**

**Avoid exoticizing ritual, art, or symbolism.**

**Escalate for advanced or esoteric topics.**

If the studio lacks appropriate expertise, Tibetan Vajrayāna content should remain introductory and respectful.

## Historical Timelines

Timelines must be evidence-based and cautious. Use approximate dates when exact dates are uncertain. Distinguish traditional dating from scholarly dating. Record source basis for each date.

Timelines should avoid implying linear superiority. Buddhist history is not a simple progression from primitive to advanced or pure to corrupted. It is a complex transmission of teachings, practices, institutions, and cultures.

## Archaeological Evidence

Archaeological evidence may support claims about sites, inscriptions, art, manuscripts, and material culture. It must be interpreted carefully.

Rules:

**Use reputable archaeological or museum sources.**

**Do not overstate what artifacts prove.**

**Distinguish object evidence from doctrinal interpretation.**

**Avoid using archaeology to dismiss devotional meaning.**

## Legend vs History

Legend and history must be classified. Legendary narratives may be spiritually meaningful without being historically verified. Historical uncertainty should not be presented as debunking.

The Knowledge Packet must label stories as:

**Historically attested**

**Canonical narrative**

**Traditional legend**

**Devotional story**

**Symbolic teaching**

**Unverified**

Content can use legendary material only when it is framed appropriately.

# PART 6 - Comparative Research

## Purpose of Comparative Research

Comparative research helps the studio explain differences among traditions, translations, commentaries, scholars, and interpretations without creating confusion or false equivalence. It is essential for a channel that covers Mahāyāna sutras, early Buddhist teachings, Pure Land, Thiền, Buddhist psychology, ritual, and modern reflection.

Comparative research must be respectful. The goal is not to decide which tradition is superior. The goal is to clarify context.

## Comparing Traditions

When comparing traditions, identify:

**Shared ground**

**Distinctive teachings**

**Different source bases**

**Practice differences**

**Historical contexts**

**Terminology differences**

**Viewer relevance**

Avoid ranking traditions. Avoid saying one tradition is more rational, more pure, more compassionate, or more advanced unless quoting and contextualizing a tradition's own claim with caution.

## Comparing Translations

Translation comparison should focus on meaning. Do not overwhelm content teams with every minor wording difference.

Compare:

**Key doctrinal terms**

**Repeated phrases**

**Ambiguous words**

**Terms linked to practice**

**Terms linked to sensitive claims**

Record whether differences are minor, meaningful, or major. If major, escalate.

## Comparing Commentaries

Commentaries may interpret the same text differently. Compare them by tradition, date, purpose, doctrinal school, and intended audience.

Do not merge commentaries into a single artificial interpretation. If a content asset cannot explain multiple views responsibly, it should choose one tradition and label it.

## Comparing Scholars

Scholars may disagree about dating, authorship, transmission, or interpretation. Compare:

**Evidence used**

**Method**

**Publication quality**

**Field expertise**

**Consensus or minority status**

**Date of scholarship**

Do not present a single scholar's view as consensus unless supported.

## Comparing Historical Interpretations

Historical interpretations often differ because evidence is fragmentary. The Knowledge Packet should record whether a claim is consensus, debated, speculative, or unknown.

Historical uncertainty should be presented as uncertainty, not as content weakness.

## Comparing Psychological Interpretations

Psychological interpretations of Buddhism can be useful but must not replace doctrine. Compare Buddhist frameworks with modern psychology only when the relationship is clear.

Avoid false equivalence such as "Buddhism and neuroscience say the same thing." Instead, say that a modern lens may help some viewers understand a related pattern, while keeping Buddhist teaching distinct.

## Comparing Modern Applications

Modern applications differ by audience, culture, and context. A teaching applied to work stress may differ from the same teaching applied to grief or family duty. Comparative research should identify the core doctrine and the application boundary.

Modern application is never stronger than the doctrine it interprets. If the application changes the teaching, reject it.

## Avoiding False Equivalence

False equivalence occurs when different claims are treated as equally supported merely because they exist. A canonical passage, a traditional commentary, a scholarly hypothesis, and a social media opinion are not equal.

To avoid false equivalence:

**Rank sources.**

**Classify claim types.**

**Identify scope.**

**State confidence.**

**Do not balance strong evidence with weak speculation.**

Neutrality does not mean pretending all claims have the same weight.

# PART 7 - Knowledge Extraction

## Purpose of Knowledge Extraction

Knowledge extraction turns research into reusable knowledge units. The goal is not to collect information but to prepare accurate, structured knowledge for content production and long-term preservation.

Extraction must preserve evidence links. A concept without source context becomes unstable. A story without classification becomes risky. A practice without tradition context can become misleading.

## Extracting Concepts

A concept is a reusable unit of meaning such as karma, impermanence, compassion, emptiness, merit, rebirth, mindfulness, vow, or filial piety.

For each concept, extract:

**Definition**

**Source basis**

**Tradition context**

**Related terms**

**Common misunderstandings**

**Practice relevance**

**Ethical relevance**

**Sensitive usage risks**

**Confidence level**

Concept extraction should align with glossary standards.

## Extracting Definitions

Definitions should be clear, concise, and expandable. A beginner definition should not be false at advanced levels. If a term has multiple meanings, define the context.

Definitions should include:

**Plain-language meaning**

**Technical nuance**

**Tradition-specific note if needed**

**Forbidden simplification**

For example, emptiness must not be defined as nothingness. Karma must not be defined as fate or punishment. Merit must not be defined as spiritual currency.

## Extracting Relationships

Relationships connect concepts. Extract relationship type:

**Prerequisite**

**Expansion**

**Contrast**

**Application**

**Source relationship**

**Practice relationship**

**Ethical relationship**

**Seasonal relationship**

**Safety relationship**

These relationships feed the Knowledge Graph and Content Architecture.

## Extracting Stories

Stories must be classified:

**Historical**

**Canonical**

**Commentarial**

**Legendary**

**Devotional**

**Symbolic**

**Modern composite**

For each story, extract:

**Source**

**Characters or figures**

**Narrative function**

**Teaching point**

**Doctrinal concepts**

**Sensitive risks**

**What may not be invented**

Stories should never be extracted as free-floating anecdotes.

## Extracting Symbols

Buddhist symbols may include lotus, light, jewels, robes, incense, bowls, paths, realms, vows, medicine, water, mountains, and Bodhisattva attributes.

For each symbol, extract:

**Traditional meaning**

**Textual or cultural source**

**Doctrinal connection**

**Devotional function**

**Possible symbolic interpretation**

**Misuse risks**

Symbolic extraction should preserve devotional respect.

## Extracting Practices

Practices include meditation, chanting, bowing, repentance, offerings, merit dedication, mindful speech, loving-kindness, and study.

For each practice, extract:

**Tradition context**

**Purpose**

**Basic description**

**Required caution**

**What not to promise**

**Relationship to ethics**

**Relationship to doctrine**

Do not extract advanced practice instructions without qualified sources and review.

## Extracting Ethics

Ethical extraction identifies intention, action, consequence, precepts, non-harming, generosity, speech, responsibility, repentance, and repair.

For each ethical teaching, extract:

**Principle**

**Source basis**

**Common distortion**

**Modern application boundary**

**Compassion-first framing**

Ethical extraction must prevent blame and shame.

## Extracting Psychology

Psychological extraction should identify Buddhist mental processes: craving, aversion, ignorance, perception, feeling, habit, attention, compassion, shame, fear, grief, and anger.

For each psychological insight, extract:

**Buddhist framework**

**Modern psychological parallel if appropriate**

**Evidence boundary**

**Clinical boundary**

**Practice relevance**

Do not turn Buddhist psychology into diagnosis.

## Extracting Filial Piety

Filial piety extraction must include gratitude, interdependence, parents, ancestors, caregiving, family complexity, boundaries, merit dedication, and cultural context.

For each filial piety claim, extract:

**Source basis**

**Cultural context**

**Buddhist ethical meaning**

**Boundary note**

**Guilt manipulation risk**

Filial piety content must never imply obedience in abusive or harmful conditions.

## Extracting Karma

Karma extraction must preserve intention, action, conditions, consequence, habit, and responsibility without cruelty.

For each karma claim, extract:

**Exact claim**

**Source basis**

**Scope**

**Misunderstanding risk**

**Victim-blaming risk**

**Rebirth relation if any**

**Confidence level**

Karma claims require especially careful confidence scoring.

## Extracting Meditation

Meditation extraction should include practice type, tradition context, purpose, ethical relationship, safety limitations, and viewer level.

Do not extract meditation as generic relaxation. Do not claim clinical results without evidence.

## Extracting Compassion and Wisdom

Compassion and wisdom are core cross-pillar concepts. Extract them with relationships:

**Compassion to Bodhisattva vows**

**Compassion to ethics**

**Compassion to grief**

**Wisdom to emptiness**

**Wisdom to non-self**

**Wisdom to practice**

These concepts should become major Knowledge Graph hubs.

## Purpose

The Buddhist Knowledge Graph is the studio's structured map of teachings, texts, figures, practices, traditions, emotions, and applications. It allows future AI agents to retrieve relevant knowledge, avoid duplication, recommend related assets, and maintain consistency.

The graph is not a decorative diagram. It is the operational memory of the studio.

## Concept Nodes

Concept nodes represent reusable Buddhist ideas. Each node must include:

**Canonical label**

**Sanskrit or Pāli term if relevant**

**Definition**

## Relationship Types

Relationship types must be controlled:

**Is defined by**

**Appears in**

**Contrasts with**

**Is symbolized by**

## Dependency Rules

Dependency rules determine learning order.

Examples:

**Emptiness should usually depend on dependent arising or non-self for beginner audiences.**

**Karma should depend on intention and causes and conditions before rebirth.**

**Hell realms should depend on compassion-first framing and karma safety rules.**

**Pure Land should depend on devotional context and non-guarantee rules.**

**Filial piety should depend on gratitude and boundaries.**

Dependencies prevent advanced or sensitive topics from appearing without support.

## Cross References

Cross references connect nodes across pillars. A sutra node should link to concepts, figures, practices, symbols, and traditions. A practice node should link to doctrine, ethical purpose, and safety boundaries. A figure node should link to stories, virtues, sutras, and devotional practices.

Cross references must be meaningful. Do not link everything to everything. Excessive linking weakens retrieval.

## Knowledge Hierarchy

The graph should distinguish:

**Root teachings**

**Major doctrines**

**Scriptural contexts**

**Tradition interpretations**

**Practice forms**

**Ethical applications**

**Modern reflections**

This hierarchy prevents modern application from being mistaken for doctrine.

## Semantic Connections

Semantic connections capture meaning. For example, compassion is semantically related to Bodhisattva vows, grief support, ethics, loving-kindness, and non-harming. It is not automatically the same as attachment, enabling, or sentimentality.

Semantic notes should include common false connections. For example:

**Non-attachment is not indifference.**

**Emptiness is not nothingness.**

**Karma is not fate.**

**Merit is not currency.**

These notes help AI avoid errors.

## Ontology Rules

The ontology must maintain clean categories:

**Text is not the same as doctrine.**

**Doctrine is not the same as interpretation.**

**Interpretation is not the same as modern application.**

**Practice is not the same as guaranteed result.**

**Legend is not the same as history.**

**Devotion is not the same as empirical proof.**

**Psychological framing is not the same as clinical treatment.**

AI agents must preserve these distinctions in every Knowledge Packet.

# PART 9 - Confidence System

## Purpose

Every major statement must receive a confidence level: Very High, High, Medium, Low, or Unknown. Confidence determines how content may phrase the claim and whether escalation is required.

Confidence is assigned per statement, not per topic. A topic may contain very high confidence definitions and low confidence historical details.

## Very High Confidence

Very High confidence applies when a claim is directly supported by strong sources and is not meaningfully disputed in the relevant context.

Examples of evidence basis:

**A verified statement about what a specific text says.**

**A widely accepted basic definition supported by multiple strong sources.**

**A historical fact supported by strong scholarly consensus and evidence.**

**A glossary term already approved by the studio.**

Use clear language. Still avoid overextending beyond the exact claim.

## High Confidence

High confidence applies when strong sources support the claim, but minor translation, interpretation, or contextual limitations exist.

Use clear but bounded language. Identify tradition context where needed.

## Medium Confidence

Medium confidence applies when evidence is credible but limited, contested, interpretive, or dependent on a particular tradition or scholar.

Use cautious language:

**Many traditions understand...**

**One common interpretation is...**

**Scholars often suggest...**

**Within this context...**

Medium confidence claims should not carry major content promises without support.

## Low Confidence

Low confidence applies when evidence is weak, isolated, uncertain, or not directly relevant. Low confidence claims should usually be omitted unless the content is explicitly about uncertainty.

If included, clearly mark uncertainty and avoid building conclusions on the claim.

## Unknown

Unknown applies when no reliable evidence is available or research has not been completed. Unknown claims must not be presented as knowledge.

Unknown should trigger one of three actions:

**Research more.**

**Escalate.**

**Remain silent.**

## Determining Confidence

Confidence is determined by:

**Source tier**

**Number of sources**

**Directness of evidence**

**Translation reliability**

**Tradition relevance**

**Scholarly consensus**

**Conflict level**

**Risk level**

**Reviewer expertise**

High-risk claims require stricter confidence. A Medium confidence claim about a harmless historical detail may be usable with caution. A Medium confidence claim about suicide, karma, rebirth, or ritual efficacy may require omission or escalation.

## When to Acknowledge Uncertainty

AI must explicitly acknowledge uncertainty when:

**Sources disagree.**

**Historical evidence is limited.**

**A text's dating or authorship is debated.**

**A translation affects meaning.**

**A practice varies by tradition.**

**A claim is devotional rather than empirical.**

**A modern psychological interpretation is suggestive, not proven.**

Uncertainty should be written calmly. It should not undermine reverence.

# PART 10 - Conflict Resolution

## Detecting Conflict

Conflicts appear when sources make different claims or frame the same material differently. AI must actively search for conflict rather than collecting only sources that confirm the first answer.

Conflict signals:

**Different translations use different key terms.**

**Traditions explain a practice differently.**

**Scholars disagree on dates or authorship.**

**A devotional source presents a story as sacred while academic sources classify it as later tradition.**

**Modern psychology interprets a practice differently from traditional sources.**

Conflict must be recorded.

## Comparing Evidence

Compare evidence by:

**Source tier**

**Claim type**

**Relevance**

**Expertise**

**Context**

**Corroboration**

**Bias**

**Date**

Do not resolve conflict by choosing the most convenient source. Do not average incompatible claims. Do not present weak speculation as equal to strong evidence.

## Presenting Multiple Interpretations

When multiple interpretations are legitimate, present them with context:

**Identify the tradition or scholar.**

**Explain the difference briefly.**

**State the shared ground if any.**

**Avoid declaring superiority.**

**Guide the viewer toward the interpretation relevant to the asset.**

Multiple interpretations should clarify, not overload. If a content asset cannot handle the complexity, narrow its scope.

## Remaining Neutral

Neutrality means representing evidence fairly. It does not mean pretending every claim is equally supported. The studio may choose a Mahāyāna frame for a Mahāyāna sutra while acknowledging other traditions.

Neutral language should be accurate and respectful. Avoid "some people believe" when referring to established traditions in a dismissive way. Prefer "in this tradition" or "within this interpretation."

## When Not to Conclude

Do not conclude when:

**Evidence is insufficient.**

**Sources conflict and no responsible resolution is available.**

**The claim is outside studio expertise.**

**The topic requires restricted or specialized knowledge.**

**A conclusion would harm vulnerable viewers.**

**A conclusion would require speculation.**

Not concluding is often the most truthful research outcome.

# PART 11 - Citation Standards

## Purpose

Citation standards make knowledge traceable. The channel may not always display formal citations in videos, but internal citation records must be strong enough for future review.

## Direct Quotations

Direct quotations require exact verification.

Record:

**Source title**

**Translator or author**

**Edition or publication**

**Chapter, section, page, or canonical reference where available**

**Exact wording**

**Language of source**

**Date accessed for web sources**

**Confidence level**

Do not modify a direct quote silently. If adapting wording for clarity, label it as paraphrase.

## Paraphrasing

Paraphrasing must preserve meaning and source context. It should not add claims the source does not make.

Record:

**Source used**

**Original claim**

**Paraphrased claim**

**Reason for paraphrase**

**Confidence level**

Paraphrase is not a way to avoid citation. It still requires traceability.

## Historical Claims

Historical claims require historical sources. Record whether the claim is:

**Consensus**

**Common scholarly view**

**Debated**

**Traditional account**

**Legendary**

**Uncertain**

Use approximate language when needed. Do not create false precision.

## Doctrinal Claims

Doctrinal claims require scripture, commentary, recognized teachers, or scholarly support. Record tradition context. Do not use a modern blog as the only source for doctrine.

## Editorial Reflections

Editorial reflections must be labeled internally. They may be used when they arise from researched understanding, but they must not be attributed to scripture or tradition.

Record:

**What evidence inspired the reflection**

**Why it is editorial**

**Risk level**

**Approved boundaries**

## Modern Examples

Modern examples generally do not need external citation if they are generic and not factual claims about real people. However, they must be ethically safe and not presented as evidence.

If a modern example uses research, statistics, historical events, or real cases, cite appropriately.

## Translation Attribution

Translation attribution is mandatory for direct scripture quotes. If multiple translations are compared, record each. If the content uses a studio translation, identify who produced it and what source it is based on.

Do not mix translations in one quote.

# PART 12 - Hallucination Prevention

## System Principle

Hallucination prevention is the Research Engine's central AI safety function. Buddhist content is especially vulnerable because AI can generate plausible sutra titles, fake quotes, invented stories, false Sanskrit, and confident doctrinal summaries.

The rule is:

**No source, no claim.**

## Invented Sutras

AI must never invent sutra titles, chapters, passages, or textual locations. If a sutra title is unfamiliar, verify it before use. If a text exists only in weak sources, do not treat it as canonical.

## Invented Buddha Quotes

Fake Buddha quotes are common. AI must verify every quote. If exact sourcing is unavailable, do not attribute it to the Buddha. Use "a modern reflection" only if the line is editorial.

## Invented Bodhisattva Stories

AI must not create new Bodhisattva narratives. Traditional stories must be sourced. If adapting symbolically, the adaptation must be labeled as interpretation, not tradition.

## Invented History

AI must not invent dates, meetings, teachers, lineages, travel routes, temple histories, or biographical details. Historical plausibility is not evidence.

## Invented Rituals

AI must not invent ritual instructions, numbers, chants, offerings, or guarantees. Ritual research requires tradition-specific sources. If a practice varies, say so.

## Invented Doctrines

AI must not blend Buddhism with law-of-attraction ideas, energy healing, fortune-telling, deterministic destiny, or generic spirituality and present the blend as Buddhist doctrine.

## Pseudo-science

AI must not claim science proves karma, rebirth, enlightenment, merit, chanting power, or ritual efficacy. Scientific claims must be sourced and modest.

## False Certainty

AI must not convert uncertainty into certainty for smoother writing. If confidence is Medium, Low, or Unknown, the content must reflect that. False certainty is a research failure.

## Hallucination Audit

Every Knowledge Packet should include a hallucination audit:

**Are all quotes verified?**

**Are all named texts real and relevant?**

**Are historical details sourced?**

**Are translations identified?**

**Are Sanskrit, Pāli, Chinese, or Vietnamese terms checked?**

**Are rituals sourced?**

**Are modern claims supported?**

**Has AI invented connective tissue beyond evidence?**

Any failure blocks content.

# PART 13 - Knowledge Packet Standard

## Purpose

The Knowledge Packet is the only acceptable input into the Content Engine. It is a structured research artifact that contains enough verified knowledge, context, confidence, and risk guidance for writers to create content without inventing or drifting.

## Canonical Knowledge Packet Structure

### Topic

State the topic precisely. Avoid broad labels unless the packet is a concept anchor.

### Core Question

Define the research question the packet answers. The question should guide scope.

### Definitions

Provide canonical definitions for key terms. Include Vietnamese, Sanskrit, Pāli, Chinese, or English forms where relevant. Record translation notes.

### Canonical Sources

List relevant scriptures or canonical texts. Include translation, edition, passage, and supported claims.

### Historical Context

Provide historical background only where relevant. Classify confidence and uncertainty.

### Doctrinal Context

Explain the doctrinal frame, tradition context, and major interpretations.

### Psychological Context

Include Buddhist psychological interpretation and modern psychology only when appropriate. Maintain clinical boundaries.

### Ethical Context

Identify ethical implications, karma concerns, responsibility framing, and compassion-first rules.

### Common Misunderstandings

List misunderstandings future writers must avoid. Include forbidden simplifications.

### Modern Relevance

Identify responsible modern applications. Distinguish editorial application from doctrine.

### Supporting Stories

List stories with source classification: historical, canonical, legendary, devotional, symbolic, modern composite, or editorial analogy.

### Related Concepts

List concept nodes and relationship types.

### Confidence Levels

Assign confidence to each major statement. Include rationale.

### Citation Requirements

List which claims require direct citation, paraphrase support, translation attribution, or reviewer notes.

### Editorial Notes

Give content-facing guidance: tone, framing, caution, omission recommendations, and possible viewer misunderstandings.

### Risk Flags

Identify sensitive topics: death, grief, suicide, abuse, trauma, illness, karma, rebirth, hell realms, ritual efficacy, filial guilt, medical claims, children, interfaith, or sectarian comparison.

### Escalation Requirement

State whether human review, Buddhist scholar review, translator review, or safety review is required.

## Packet Completeness Rules

A Knowledge Packet is incomplete if:

**It lacks source records.**

**It has unscored claims.**

**It does not identify risk.**

**It does not classify source type.**

**It contains direct quotes without attribution.**

**It includes uncertainty without guidance.**

**It has no related concept mapping.**

Incomplete packets cannot enter the Content Engine.

# PART 14 - AI Research Decision Framework

## What to Search

AI should search according to claim type. For scripture, search canonical text and translations. For interpretation, search commentaries and recognized scholars. For history, search academic and primary historical sources. For practice, search tradition-specific sources. For psychology, search Buddhist frameworks and credible modern research only when needed.

Search should begin with stronger sources, not whatever appears first.

## When to Stop Searching

Stop searching when:

**The research question is answered at the required confidence level.**

**Claims are adequately sourced for the content risk level.**

**Major conflicts have been identified and handled.**

**Further searching is unlikely to change the conclusion.**

**The packet can state both evidence and limitations clearly.**

Do not search endlessly to avoid making an editorial decision. Research must be sufficient, not infinite.

## When More Evidence Is Required

More evidence is required when:

**A claim is high-risk.**

**Sources conflict.**

**Only weak sources support a claim.**

**A quote is unverified.**

**A translation affects meaning.**

**The claim involves history, medicine, psychology, ritual efficacy, rebirth, hell realms, or suicide.**

**The content will become an anchor asset.**

Anchor assets require stronger research than minor reinforcement assets.

## When to Escalate

Escalate when:

**The topic requires language expertise.**

**The doctrine is contested.**

**A high-risk sensitive topic is involved.**

**The source tradition is outside available expertise.**

**Vajrayāna or restricted practice appears.**

**Medical or crisis implications appear.**

**The AI cannot determine confidence.**

Escalation is a strength, not a failure.

## When to Remain Silent

Remain silent when:

**No reliable source exists.**

**The topic would require speculation.**

**The claim may harm viewers.**

**The content would imply authority the channel does not have.**

**The topic belongs to qualified teachers, clinicians, or emergency support.**

Not every question should become content.

## When to Reject Weak Sources

Reject weak sources when they:

**Provide no citations.**

**Use miracle guarantees.**

**Promote fear.**

**Sell rituals as transactions.**

**Contain fake quotes.**

**Attack other traditions.**

**Make medical claims.**

**Depend on AI summaries.**

Weak sources may be logged as rejected to prevent reuse.

# PART 17 - Source Registry and Research Library

## Purpose of the Source Registry

The Source Registry is the studio's controlled index of approved, provisional, deprecated, and rejected sources. It prevents future AI agents from rediscovering the same sources repeatedly, relying on weak sources, or losing track of which translations and scholars have already been evaluated.

The registry is not a bibliography alone. It is a decision system. It records what a source is good for, what it is not good for, its tier, tradition context, reliability notes, and usage restrictions. A source may be approved for one use and prohibited for another. For example, a temple website may be useful for explaining that temple's liturgical schedule but not for universal doctrinal claims. A general encyclopedia may be useful for orientation but not for final historical claims. A modern teacher's Dharma talk may be useful for practice reflection but not for textual dating.

No research system can scale without a source registry. Without it, AI agents will default to easy retrieval and plausible text. With it, the studio can build institutional memory.

## Source Record Fields

Every source record should include:

**Source ID:** A stable internal identifier.

**Title:** Full title of the source.

**Author, translator, editor, or institution:** Identify responsible parties.

**Source type:** Scripture, commentary, scholarly book, academic article, historical record, educational institution, translation project, temple resource, encyclopedia, web article, audio teaching, video teaching, or rejected source.

**Source tier:** Tier 1 through Tier 8.

**Tradition context:** Theravāda, Mahāyāna, Pure Land, Thiền, Zen, Tibetan, Chinese, Vietnamese, Japanese, academic, interfaith, or other context.

**Language:** Original and translation language.

**Publication details:** Publisher, journal, edition, date, URL, archive link, or collection reference where available.

**Reliability status:** Approved, approved with limits, provisional, deprecated, rejected, or requires expert review.

**Approved use cases:** What the source may support.

**Prohibited use cases:** What the source may not support.

**Known limitations:** Bias, simplification, sectarian scope, outdated scholarship, uncertain attribution, translation limitations, or missing citations.

**Related sources:** Stronger sources, alternate translations, commentaries, scholarly responses, or corrections.

**Last reviewed date:** Date of latest evaluation.

**Reviewer:** Person or role responsible for evaluation.

**Notes:** Research comments, usage warnings, and known issues.

Source records should be written for future retrieval. A future AI agent should know within seconds whether a source can support a claim.

# PART 20 - Risk-specific Research Protocols

## Purpose

Some topics require special research protocols because errors can harm viewers or distort Buddhism. These topics must not be researched with general workflow alone.

## Karma Protocol

Karma research must identify intention, action, conditions, consequence, habit, ethical responsibility, and tradition context.

Required checks:

**Does the source define karma as intentional action?**

**Does the claim risk victim blaming?**

**Does it imply fate?**

**Does it overexplain suffering?**

**Does it connect to rebirth, and if so, with what confidence?**

**Does it require compassion-first framing?**

Karma claims should be narrowed if they cannot be stated without blame.

## Rebirth Protocol

Rebirth research must distinguish doctrine, tradition, devotional belief, philosophical interpretation, and modern speculation.

Required checks:

**Which tradition is being represented?**

**Is the claim scriptural, commentarial, or interpretive?**

**Does the content imply knowledge of a person's rebirth?**

**Does it risk frightening grieving viewers?**

**Does it claim scientific proof?**

Rebirth claims about individuals are prohibited.

## Hell Realms Protocol

Hell realm research must be tied to compassion, moral seriousness, and source classification.

Required checks:

**Which text or tradition presents the hell realm imagery?**

**Is the content descriptive, symbolic, devotional, or doctrinal?**

**Does the asset risk horror framing?**

**Does it imply viewers are threatened?**

**Does it include the compassionate purpose of the teaching?**

Hell realm research must include safety notes.

## Merit and Ritual Protocol

Merit and ritual research must prevent transactional claims.

Required checks:

**What practice is described?**

**Which tradition supports it?**

**What intention is central?**

**Does the source promise specific results?**

**Is the result language devotional or literal guarantee?**

**Could viewers interpret this as spiritual purchase?**

Any guarantee must be rejected or reframed as traditional aspiration if supported.

## Death and Grief Protocol

Research for death and grief must include doctrinal sources and emotional safety.

Required checks:

**Does the claim concern the deceased?**

**Does it imply certainty about post-death state?**

**Does it support compassionate remembrance?**

**Does it avoid grief shaming?**

**Does it need crisis or mental health boundary language?**

Research should produce gentle, bounded language recommendations.

## Suicide and Crisis Protocol

Suicide and crisis research must include safety guidance. Buddhist doctrine alone is not sufficient.

Required checks:

**Could the content be heard by someone in acute crisis?**

**Does it avoid method details?**

**Does it avoid karmic threats?**

**Does it encourage immediate human support?**

**Does it require safety reviewer escalation?**

AI must not improvise crisis guidance beyond approved safety language.

## Abuse and Trauma Protocol

Abuse and trauma research must prevent spiritual bypassing.

Required checks:

**Does the claim pressure forgiveness?**

**Does it imply abuse is deserved karma?**

**Does it use compassion to erase boundaries?**

**Does it recommend qualified support?**

**Does it distinguish Buddhist reflection from therapy?**

Content must not instruct viewers to remain in harm.

## Medical Claims Protocol

Medical and healing claims require strict boundaries.

Required checks:

**Is the claim devotional, psychological, or medical?**

**Does it imply cure?**

**Does it discourage professional care?**

**Does it use Medicine Buddha or chanting responsibly?**

**Is scientific support being overstated?**

Medical claims require high evidence and usually should be avoided in favor of supportive, non-clinical language.

# PART 21 - Research Ethics

## Respect for Living Traditions

Research must treat Buddhist traditions as living communities, not merely content sources. A ritual, chant, robe, temple, icon, or story may carry meaning for practitioners beyond what academic description captures.

Respect means:

**Do not mock devotional practice.**

**Do not extract sacred material for spectacle.**

**Do not treat culture as decoration.**

**Do not flatten living traditions into textual data.**

**Do not use a tradition's materials against it unfairly.**

Academic clarity and devotional respect can coexist.

## Avoiding Sectarian Bias

Sectarian bias occurs when one tradition is treated as the default truth and others as deviations. The channel may have a Mahāyāna-compatible orientation, but research must still represent traditions fairly.

Avoid:

**Calling one tradition more authentic without evidence and context.**

**Treating devotional traditions as inferior.**

**Treating early sources as automatically complete for all Buddhist content.**

**Treating modern secular mindfulness as the standard for meditation.**

**Treating Vietnamese practice as universal Buddhism.**

Research should identify the frame rather than erase difference.

## Cultural Humility

Cultural humility is essential for Vietnamese, Chinese, Japanese, Tibetan, Indian, Sri Lankan, Southeast Asian, and global Buddhist contexts.

Researchers should ask:

**Whose tradition is being described?**

**Is this practice local, regional, sectarian, or broadly Buddhist?**

**Are we imposing modern Western categories?**

**Are we reducing ritual to superstition?**

**Are we using cultural imagery without context?**

Cultural humility protects the channel from extraction and oversimplification.

## Ethical Use of Modern Psychology

Modern psychology can clarify some viewer experiences but should not dominate Buddhist research. Do not force Buddhist teachings into psychological frameworks or use psychology to validate Buddhism superficially.

Research should distinguish:

**Buddhist analysis of mind**

**Clinical psychology**

**Popular psychology**

**Neuroscience**

**Editorial analogy**

Only clinical or scientific claims require scientific evidence. Buddhist psychological reflection requires Buddhist sources and clear boundaries.

## Researcher Conduct

Researchers and AI agents must practice epistemic humility:

**Say when you do not know.**

**Separate evidence from interpretation.**

**Do not hide weak sources.**

**Do not exaggerate confidence.**

**Do not chase novelty.**

**Do not use sacred material casually.**

Research conduct is part of the studio's moral culture.

# PART 24 - AI Research Failure Modes

## Purpose

AI research failure modes must be named so future agents can detect and prevent them.

## Failure Mode 1: Fluent Fabrication

Fluent fabrication occurs when AI generates elegant, plausible claims without evidence.

Prevention:

**Require source records.**

**Verify every quote.**

**Use confidence scoring.**

**Reject unsupported detail.**

## Failure Mode 2: Source Substitution

Source substitution occurs when AI uses an easy weak source instead of the appropriate strong source.

Prevention:

**Follow the source hierarchy.**

**Search approved registry first.**

**Use weak sources only for orientation.**

## Failure Mode 3: Tradition Collapse

Tradition collapse occurs when AI blends Theravāda, Mahāyāna, Pure Land, Thiền, Vajrayāna, and modern mindfulness into one voice.

Prevention:

**Classify tradition context.**

**Use tradition-specific qualifiers.**

**Avoid universal claims when differences matter.**

## Failure Mode 4: Translation Overconfidence

Translation overconfidence occurs when AI builds a major claim on one translated word.

Prevention:

**Compare translations for key terms.**

**Record original-language issues.**

**Escalate when wording matters.**

## Failure Mode 5: Devotional Literalism Without Context

This occurs when AI presents devotional claims as empirical certainty or universal doctrine.

Prevention:

**Classify devotional sources.**

**Use careful language.**

**Avoid guarantees.**

## Failure Mode 6: Academic Reductionism

Academic reductionism occurs when AI uses scholarship to strip devotional or symbolic meaning from practice.

Prevention:

**Respect source type.**

**Distinguish historical method from spiritual meaning.**

**Avoid contempt for devotion.**

## Failure Mode 7: Modernization Distortion

Modernization distortion occurs when AI rewrites Buddhist teachings as productivity, therapy, neuroscience, or self-help.

Prevention:

**Keep Buddhist doctrine primary.**

**Classify modern application as editorial.**

**Avoid clinical and scientific overclaims.**

## Failure Mode 8: Confidence Inflation

Confidence inflation occurs when Medium or Low evidence becomes written as High certainty.

Prevention:

**Assign confidence per statement.**

**Require qualifiers.**

**Review high-risk claims.**

## Failure Mode 9: Missing Negative Evidence

AI may collect confirming sources and miss disagreement.

Prevention:

**Search for conflicts deliberately.**

**Compare traditions and scholars.**

**Record unresolved disagreements.**

## Failure Mode 10: Citation Mirage

Citation mirage occurs when AI produces plausible citations, page numbers, or titles that have not been verified.

Prevention:

**Never trust AI-generated citations.**

**Open or inspect sources directly.**

**Record citation only after verification.**

Any failure mode incident should be logged in the research audit system.

# PART 26 - Multilingual Research Protocol

## Purpose

Buddhist research is multilingual by nature. Key materials may appear in Pāli, Sanskrit, Classical Chinese, Tibetan, Vietnamese, Japanese, Korean, English, French, German, or other modern languages. The studio must avoid treating English-language availability as the boundary of knowledge. At the same time, AI agents must not pretend language expertise they do not have.

The multilingual protocol defines how to handle language responsibly.

## Language Identification

Every source record should identify the source language and translation language. If the researcher cannot identify the language, the source should not be used for precise claims.

Record:

**Original language**

**Translation language**

**Translator**

**Translation date**

**Translation tradition**

**Known translation limitations**

Language identification is especially important for scriptural terms, names, mantras, titles, and philosophical vocabulary.

## Translation Dependency

A claim is translation-dependent when its meaning relies on a specific wording. Translation-dependent claims require comparison or escalation.

Examples of translation-dependent areas include:

**Emptiness**

**Non-self**

**Mindfulness**

**Karma**

**Merit**

**Faith**

**Vow**

**Rebirth**

**Liberation**

**Skillful means**

**Pure Land terms**

When a claim is translation-dependent, do not use one translation as if it settles the meaning. Record alternatives.

## Vietnamese Research Protocol

Vietnamese Buddhist research must include Vietnamese sources when the topic concerns Vietnamese practice, terminology, culture, or devotional life. English-only research can miss the emotional and cultural meaning of terms such as hiếu đạo, hồi hướng, công đức, nghiệp, and Vu Lan.

Rules:

**Use Vietnamese diacritics correctly.**

**Preserve Vietnamese titles and names.**

**Identify whether a Vietnamese source is temple-based, scholarly, devotional, educational, or popular.**

**Do not treat popular Vietnamese web content as automatically reliable.**

**Compare Vietnamese explanations with canonical or scholarly sources where doctrinal claims are made.**

**Record culturally specific usage.**

Vietnamese sources can be highly valuable, but they must still be classified by tier and claim type.

## Chinese Text Research

Many Mahāyāna sutras central to the channel are preserved in Chinese translation. Research involving Chinese Buddhist texts should identify the Chinese title or Taishō reference when available through reliable sources. AI agents should not invent Taishō numbers or Chinese titles.

Rules:

**Use reliable digital canon or scholarly references.**

**Do not translate Classical Chinese independently unless qualified.**

**Compare existing translations when available.**

**Escalate if a claim depends on Chinese wording.**

Chinese text research is especially relevant for Kinh Địa Tạng, Kinh Pháp Hoa, Kinh A Di Đà, Kinh Dược Sư, and East Asian commentarial traditions.

## Pāli and Sanskrit Research

Pāli and Sanskrit terms should be used carefully. AI agents must not fabricate diacritics, roots, or etymologies.

Rules:

**Use established dictionaries, translations, or scholarly sources.**

**Do not build doctrine from amateur etymology.**

**Record whether a term is Pāli, Sanskrit, or both.**

**Avoid mixing forms without purpose.**

**Use diacritics consistently in formal research records.**

Etymology can illuminate meaning but should not replace doctrinal context.

## Translation Escalation

Escalate to a translator or qualified reviewer when:

**A direct quote is central to content.**

**Translations disagree significantly.**

**A term carries doctrinal weight.**

**The original language affects interpretation.**

**A studio translation is proposed.**

**The source is in Classical Chinese, Tibetan, Sanskrit, or Pāli and no reliable translation is available.**

If escalation is not possible, narrow the claim.

**A karma claim risks victim blaming.**

**A rebirth claim implies knowledge of an individual's destiny.**

**A medical claim implies cure or replacement of care.**

**A suicide or crisis topic lacks safety review.**

**A story is presented as historical without evidence.**

**An AI-generated citation has not been verified.**

**A Knowledge Packet is incomplete.**

## Conditional Approval

Some packets may receive conditional approval. Conditions may include:

**Use only paraphrase, not direct quote.**

**State as tradition-specific.**

**Add uncertainty language.**

**Remove historical detail.**

**Avoid public use in Short format.**

**Require human narration note.**

**Add support language.**

Conditional approval must be visible in the handoff package.

## Rejection

Reject a research packet when the claim cannot be supported, the source base is contaminated, the topic is outside studio competence, or safe framing is not possible.

Rejected packets should be archived with reasons so future AI agents do not attempt the same weak path again.

# PART 30 - Knowledge Migration and Future Systems

## Purpose

Over ten years, the studio may change tools, databases, AI models, file formats, platforms, and workflows. Research knowledge must survive these changes. Knowledge migration rules ensure that source integrity and context are not lost.

## Portable Research Records

Research records should be stored in formats that can be exported, searched, and converted. Avoid systems that trap knowledge in inaccessible interfaces.

Each record should preserve:

**Source details**

**Claim links**

**Confidence scores**

**Reviewer notes**

**Version history**

**Risk flags**

**Related concept nodes**

## AI Model Migration

When new AI models are introduced, they must be tested against the Research Engine.

Test whether the model:

**Invents citations**

**Overstates confidence**

**Collapses traditions**

**Misuses Buddhist terms**

**Ignores risk flags**

**Can follow source hierarchy**

**Can use Knowledge Packets without adding unsupported claims**

No new model should be trusted automatically because it is more fluent.

## Database Migration

When moving research data to a new database, verify:

**No source records are lost.**

**Confidence fields remain attached to claims.**

**Deprecated sources remain marked.**

**Rejected sources remain blocked.**

**Concept relationships remain intact.**

**Version history is preserved.**

**Search retrieval still works.**

Migration should include sample audits.

## Future Research Domains

The studio may later expand into new areas: Buddhist art, architecture, pilgrimage, comparative ethics, language study, children's education, academic interviews, or interactive learning. Each new domain must be added through the same source hierarchy and packet system.

Expansion must not create a parallel research method. New domains extend the Research Engine; they do not bypass it.

## Preservation of Uncertainty

When migrating systems, uncertainty notes must be preserved. A common failure is that future systems retain the conclusion but lose the caveat. This is dangerous. Medium confidence must remain Medium. Unknown must remain Unknown. Tradition-specific must remain tradition-specific.

Knowledge migration must preserve humility.

# PART 31 - Research Performance Metrics

## Purpose

The Research Engine must be measurable. A studio cannot maintain research integrity for thousands of assets by relying only on intention. Research performance metrics show whether the system is actually preventing hallucination, improving traceability, strengthening source quality, and supporting content production.

Research metrics should not reward speed alone. Fast research with weak evidence is a liability. The best metrics balance efficiency with accuracy, humility, and maintainability.

## Core Metrics

**Packet completeness rate:** Percentage of Knowledge Packets that include all required fields before entering the Content Engine.

**Source traceability rate:** Percentage of major claims that can be traced to a source record or editorial note.

**Quote verification rate:** Percentage of direct quotations verified against reliable sources before content handoff. This should be 100 percent.

**Confidence scoring coverage:** Percentage of major claims assigned Very High, High, Medium, Low, or Unknown.

**High-risk review completion:** Percentage of high-risk packets reviewed by the appropriate human role before production.

**Deprecated source avoidance:** Number of times deprecated or rejected sources were caught before entering a packet.

**Correction recurrence rate:** Number of repeated errors after a correction has been logged. This should trend toward zero.

**Translation review compliance:** Percentage of translation-dependent claims reviewed or qualified appropriately.

**Knowledge Graph mapping rate:** Percentage of packets mapped to concept nodes and relationship types.

**Research-to-content overrun rate:** Number of times the Content Engine introduced claims not present in the Knowledge Packet.

These metrics should be reviewed quarterly.

## Quality Signals

Strong research quality is visible when:

**Writers rarely need to invent connective claims.**

**Reviewers can quickly trace statements.**

**AI agents retrieve prior packets before drafting.**

**Terminology remains stable across assets.**

**Sensitive topics contain clear risk notes.**

**Viewer confusion decreases over time.**

**Corrections are documented and not repeated.**

**Source quality improves across new packets.**

Weak research quality is visible when:

**Packets contain vague source notes.**

**Quotes lack exact attribution.**

**Confidence levels are missing.**

**AI agents repeatedly propose already-rejected claims.**

**Different assets define the same term differently.**

**High-risk topics move quickly without review.**

**Historical claims become more precise than evidence allows.**

**Community comments reveal doctrinal confusion caused by content.**

Weak signals should trigger audit and process repair.

## Research Improvement Loop

The research improvement loop has five steps:

**Measure:** Review metrics and identify weak points.

**Diagnose:** Determine whether the problem is source quality, AI retrieval, reviewer capacity, glossary gaps, packet structure, or content handoff.

**Correct:** Update packets, sources, templates, glossary entries, or workflow rules.

**Train:** Update AI instructions and human reviewer guidance.

**Audit:** Check whether the same problem recurs.

This loop keeps the Research Engine alive. It prevents the document from becoming static policy that no one follows.

## Minimum Research Health Standard

The studio should not scale output unless research health remains strong. If packet completeness falls, quote verification weakens, high-risk review is skipped, or correction recurrence rises, production volume should slow until research integrity is restored.

The minimum standard is:

**Every quote verified.**

**Every major claim traceable.**

**Every high-risk packet reviewed.**

**Every Knowledge Packet confidence-scored.**

**Every content handoff bounded by approved claims.**

Research health is production health. If research weakens, the entire channel weakens.

## Research Culture Standard

Metrics should shape culture, not fear. Researchers, editors, and AI agents should feel responsible for slowing down when evidence is weak. A delayed asset is not a failure if the delay prevents false teaching. A narrowed claim is not a loss if it preserves truth. A visible uncertainty note is not a weakness if it prevents false authority.

The studio should reward careful correction, honest escalation, and well-documented uncertainty. Over time, this culture makes research faster because the knowledge base becomes cleaner, not because standards become lower.

This culture must be taught to every future collaborator before production begins.

No exception should be made for urgent schedules.

## Permanent Research Standard

The permanent standard for the Buddhist AI Studio is:

**No claim without evidence.**

**No quote without verification.**

**No scripture without source.**

**No history without context.**

**No interpretation without classification.**

**No confidence without basis.**

**No content without a Knowledge Packet.**

**No AI output treated as authority.**

**No speed above truth.**

**No certainty where humility is required.**

This Research Engine is the foundation beneath every future content engine, writing engine, architecture system, and brand expression. It protects the channel from hallucination, superstition, false attribution, source contamination, and overconfidence. It allows Buddhist content to be beautiful because it is trustworthy, moving because it is responsible, and scalable because its knowledge is preserved.

The final research question is always:

**Can this claim be traced, trusted, and carried forward into the channel's permanent Buddhist library?**

If the answer is no, the claim must not enter content.
