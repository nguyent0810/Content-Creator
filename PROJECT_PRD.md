# Project PRD: Long-Term Buddhist Education, Reflection, and Cinematic Storytelling Channel

## Document Control

**Document Type:** Product Requirement Document  
**Primary Use:** Single source of truth for all future human and AI collaborators  
**Channel Domain:** Buddhist education, spiritual reflection, and cinematic storytelling  
**Content Scope:** Buddhism across canonical texts, philosophy, practice, history, ethics, psychology, and modern life  
**Output Governed By This PRD:** YouTube video concepts, series plans, editorial briefs, research briefs, script briefs, production notes, voice direction, visual direction, and quality reviews  
**Output Not Included In This PRD:** Finished YouTube scripts, finished episode narration, thumbnails, titles, or metadata  
**Longevity Target:** Multi-year channel system capable of producing hundreds of videos while preserving identity, ethical standards, and editorial consistency

## 1. Vision

The vision of the channel is to become a trusted, cinematic, and contemplative Buddhist education platform for modern viewers seeking wisdom, healing, ethical clarity, and spiritual depth. The channel should make Buddhist teachings feel alive without diluting them, accessible without trivializing them, and emotionally resonant without turning them into entertainment spectacle.

The channel exists to serve viewers across generations who want more than inspirational quotes, fragmented spiritual advice, or algorithm-driven religious sensationalism. It should offer a long-form, coherent, and carefully curated body of Buddhist learning that can remain valuable for years. Each video should feel like part of a larger library: one path among many toward understanding suffering, compassion, impermanence, karma, mindfulness, filial piety, wisdom, and liberation.

The channel should not be limited to one scripture, one school, one ritual tradition, or one mood. It must be structurally expandable across Kinh Địa Tạng, Kinh Pháp Hoa, Kinh Kim Cang, Kinh A Di Đà, Kinh Dược Sư, Thiền, nhân quả, hiếu đạo, the life of the Buddha, Bodhisattvas, Arahants, Buddhist psychology, healing, mindfulness, and modern Buddhist reflections. The long-term vision is a content ecosystem where different series support different needs: some viewers come for sutra education, others for moral reflection, others for peace during grief, others for cinematic stories of Buddhist figures, and others for practical guidance in daily life.

The channel should become known for sincerity, depth, restraint, beauty, and trust. It should not chase novelty at the expense of truth. It should not create anxiety around karmic punishment, death, spirits, or ritual efficacy. It should instead invite viewers into wise reflection: What causes suffering? What nourishes compassion? What does it mean to live with responsibility? How can ancient teachings illuminate modern emotional life? How can a person become gentler, clearer, and less dominated by greed, anger, and delusion?

The channel's highest aspiration is to become a long-term spiritual companion, not a short-term content machine. It should help viewers return repeatedly to practice, reflection, gratitude, and ethical action. It should preserve the dignity of Buddhist teachings while using the strengths of modern media: narrative pacing, cinematic atmosphere, clear structure, careful sound, evocative imagery, and emotionally intelligent storytelling.

## 2. Mission

The mission of the channel is to create high-quality Buddhist educational and reflective video content that helps modern viewers understand Buddhist teachings, apply them ethically, and experience a deeper sense of peace, compassion, and responsibility in everyday life.

The channel will translate complex Buddhist themes into clear, respectful, emotionally resonant narratives without claiming final authority over doctrine. It will explain scriptures and concepts with humility, distinguish interpretation from source material, and make room for historical complexity and differences among Buddhist traditions.

The channel will serve three connected missions:

1. **Educational Mission:** Explain Buddhist texts, ideas, figures, and practices in a way that is accurate, accessible, and structured. Viewers should leave with greater understanding, not only temporary emotion.
2. **Reflective Mission:** Invite viewers into inner examination. The channel should help people look at suffering, anger, attachment, guilt, grief, family duty, mortality, and compassion with steadiness.
3. **Cinematic Mission:** Use visual and narrative craft to make Buddhist learning memorable. Cinematic storytelling should support insight, not replace it.

Every future script, series, and production decision must support these missions. If a proposed video is visually impressive but spiritually shallow, it does not meet the mission. If it is doctrinally detailed but emotionally cold and inaccessible, it only partially meets the mission. If it is popular but relies on fear, superstition, or exaggerated promises, it violates the mission.

The mission requires disciplined editorial balance. The channel should be warm but not sentimental, wise but not arrogant, beautiful but not decorative, accessible but not simplified into slogans. Its videos should help viewers slow down in a media environment that trains them to react quickly. They should encourage practice, ethical reflection, gratitude, patience, compassion, and humility.

## 3. Brand Purpose

The brand purpose is to help people encounter Buddhist wisdom as a living source of clarity, healing, and ethical transformation.

The channel should exist because many people are spiritually hungry but overwhelmed by fragmented information. They may know Buddhist terms such as karma, mindfulness, emptiness, compassion, rebirth, or nirvana, but they often encounter them in partial, commercialized, or distorted forms. Some viewers may have inherited Buddhist practices from family but never received a clear explanation. Others may be new to Buddhism and drawn by meditation, psychology, or grief support. Others may feel guilt around parents, anxiety about death, or confusion about moral responsibility. The channel must meet these needs with patience and dignity.

The purpose is not to convert viewers, compete with temples, replace teachers, or establish an authoritative sectarian platform. The purpose is to create a respectful bridge: between scripture and daily life, between tradition and modern language, between intellectual understanding and emotional transformation, between cinematic media and contemplative attention.

The channel should also protect viewers from low-quality spiritual media. It should not exploit fear of bad karma, promise miracles, dramatize the unseen realms for shock value, or present ritual acts as transactional guarantees. It should model a healthier form of Buddhist communication: careful, compassionate, humble, and clear about uncertainty.

The brand should become associated with a specific promise: when viewers watch this channel, they can expect depth without confusion, beauty without manipulation, emotion without melodrama, and reverence without superstition.

## 4. Core Philosophy

The core philosophy of the channel is that Buddhist wisdom becomes most powerful when it is understood, contemplated, embodied, and expressed through compassionate action.

The channel is built on several foundational convictions:

**Suffering can be understood.** Buddhist teaching begins with an honest recognition of dukkha: dissatisfaction, instability, pain, stress, and the subtle unease that accompanies attachment. The channel should not rush past suffering with shallow comfort. It should help viewers see suffering clearly and gently.

**Causes matter.** Pain is not random content for dramatic storytelling. Buddhist reflection asks what conditions give rise to suffering and what conditions can reduce it. Videos should guide viewers toward causal understanding: habits, intentions, perceptions, craving, aversion, ignorance, family patterns, speech, attention, and moral choices.

**Compassion and wisdom belong together.** Compassion without wisdom may become sentimentality; wisdom without compassion may become cold abstraction. The channel must unite both. It should explain ideas while softening the heart.

**The viewer is not a passive consumer.** Every video should subtly invite practice. Practice may mean mindfulness, ethical restraint, gratitude toward parents, repentance, generosity, patience, forgiveness, chanting, study, or simply pausing before harmful speech. The viewer should feel called toward life, not merely informed.

**Tradition deserves respect and careful translation.** Buddhist scriptures, stories, and practices should not be treated as raw material for arbitrary reinvention. At the same time, the channel must make them understandable in modern language. The guiding principle is faithful adaptation: preserve meaning, clarify context, and avoid distortion.

**Mystery should be handled with humility.** Buddhist cosmology includes realms, Bodhisattvas, karmic consequences, vows, merits, and unseen dimensions of existence. The channel may discuss these topics, but it must avoid sensational certainty, fear marketing, or false empirical claims. Where traditions differ, that difference should be acknowledged.

**Cinematic form is a vehicle, not the destination.** Music, pacing, imagery, and narrative structure should deepen attention. They must not overwhelm the teaching or manipulate the viewer into emotional dependency.

## 5. Brand Values

The channel's brand values define the expected character of every future output.

**Reverence:** Buddhist teachings, figures, rituals, and communities must be approached with respect. Reverence does not require grandiosity. It appears through careful language, accurate framing, and avoidance of mockery, sensationalism, or careless simplification.

**Clarity:** Videos must explain. The channel should avoid vague spiritual language that sounds profound but teaches little. Every concept should be made understandable through structure, examples, and careful distinctions.

**Compassion:** The tone should be gentle toward human struggle. Viewers may arrive with grief, guilt, anxiety, family conflict, illness, or spiritual confusion. Content should not shame them. It should help them see more clearly while preserving dignity.

**Integrity:** Claims must be responsible. Sources must be treated honestly. Historical and doctrinal uncertainty must be acknowledged. The channel should never make promises it cannot justify.

**Depth:** The channel should resist the pressure to reduce Buddhism to productivity advice, wellness hacks, or generic positivity. It should be accessible but substantial.

**Humility:** The channel is not a final authority. It should use language that recognizes diversity among Buddhist traditions and the limits of any single interpretation.

**Beauty:** Production should be aesthetically refined. Beauty here means calm composition, thoughtful pacing, coherent sound, and imagery that supports contemplation. Beauty should never become decoration detached from meaning.

**Continuity:** Videos should feel like they belong to the same spiritual and editorial universe. Future AI agents must preserve consistent assumptions, tone, terminology policy, ethical boundaries, and viewer promise.

**Usefulness:** Every video should help the viewer in some real way: understanding a teaching, finding language for grief, reflecting on conduct, deepening practice, or seeing life with more patience.

**Non-exploitation:** The channel must not exploit fear, death, guilt, filial obligation, karmic anxiety, poverty, illness, or bereavement for engagement.

## 6. Editorial Mission

The editorial mission is to build a coherent, trustworthy, and emotionally resonant Buddhist learning library that can support long-term spiritual reflection across multiple viewer needs.

Editorially, the channel must bridge five dimensions:

1. **Text:** Sutras, commentarial traditions, Buddhist stories, doctrinal concepts, and historical materials.
2. **Practice:** Meditation, chanting, ethical conduct, repentance, mindfulness, compassion, gratitude, and daily-life application.
3. **Emotion:** Grief, guilt, anxiety, anger, loneliness, gratitude, devotion, awe, and hope.
4. **Life Stage:** Youth, adulthood, parenthood, aging, illness, bereavement, family duty, and preparation for death.
5. **Modern Context:** Work stress, consumer culture, digital distraction, family fragmentation, mental health language, and the search for meaning.

The editorial mission is not to create isolated videos. It is to create a durable system of meaning. Each video should be easy to understand on its own while also fitting into larger series, playlists, and viewer pathways. For example, a viewer interested in Kinh Địa Tạng should be guided toward filial piety, vows, karmic responsibility, hell realms, compassion for the deceased, and ethical repentance. A viewer interested in Thiền should be guided toward attention, direct experience, non-attachment, simplicity, and daily practice. A viewer interested in Buddhist psychology should be guided toward craving, aversion, perception, habit energy, emotions, and mindful transformation.

The channel should cultivate editorial trust through repetition of quality rather than repetition of formulas. Each video can vary in structure, topic, and mood, but the underlying principles should remain stable: accurate framing, human relevance, contemplative pacing, ethical caution, and a sense of spiritual seriousness.

## 7. Target Audience

### Demographics

The channel is designed for a broad but intentional audience. It should be accessible to viewers with different levels of Buddhist knowledge while maintaining depth for those who already practice.

Primary demographic segments include:

**Vietnamese-speaking Buddhist viewers and culturally Buddhist families:** This group may include people who grew up around temples, chanting, ancestor remembrance, filial piety, merit dedication, and Buddhist holidays. Some may understand rituals emotionally but want clearer explanations. Some may want content that honors family traditions without feeling old-fashioned or inaccessible.

**Adults seeking spiritual grounding:** Viewers in their twenties through sixties who are experiencing stress, loss, family conflict, moral uncertainty, or existential fatigue. They may be drawn to Buddhism for calm and wisdom but need structured guidance.

**Meditation and mindfulness audiences:** Viewers interested in awareness, emotional regulation, Buddhist psychology, and contemplative practice. Some may come from secular mindfulness and gradually become curious about Buddhist roots.

**Intergenerational family viewers:** Adult children reflecting on parents, elders reflecting on mortality, families seeking meaningful content for rituals, memorial periods, Vu Lan, or moments of illness and loss.

**Spiritual learners outside Buddhism:** Viewers who may not identify as Buddhist but are open to Buddhist philosophy, ethics, and contemplative practice.

**Diaspora viewers:** Vietnamese or Asian diaspora audiences who may seek reconnection with inherited spiritual culture in a modern production style.

The channel should avoid assuming high academic literacy or formal doctrinal training. It should also avoid assuming viewers are beginners in every episode. The system must support layered content: introductory, reflective, textual, cinematic, and deeper-dive formats.

### Psychographics

The audience is united less by age and more by inner need. Many viewers are reflective, emotionally sensitive, morally serious, or spiritually searching. They may feel that ordinary entertainment does not satisfy them. They may want beauty, calm, and meaning. They may be tired of aggressive media environments and drawn toward slower, deeper listening.

Common psychographic traits include:

**Search for peace:** Viewers want relief from mental noise, anxiety, and emotional turbulence. They are drawn to voices and visuals that help them breathe.

**Moral concern:** Viewers wonder how to live rightly. They may think about karma, speech, family duty, compassion, regret, and the consequences of choices.

**Family memory:** Many viewers carry strong emotions around parents, ancestors, children, debt of gratitude, unresolved conflict, or filial responsibility.

**Spiritual curiosity:** They want to understand Buddhist ideas beyond surface-level quotes. They may ask what emptiness means, how karma works, why Bodhisattvas make vows, or what meditation is for.

**Aesthetic receptivity:** They respond to cinematic pacing, atmospheric sound, and poetic but clear narration. They do not want noisy spectacle.

**Healing orientation:** They may be working through grief, guilt, trauma, burnout, illness, or loneliness. They need content that is gentle and responsible.

**Distrust of shallow spirituality:** Many are wary of exaggerated miracle claims, commercialized spirituality, or moralistic fear tactics. They want a voice that feels grounded.

### Pain Points

The channel should address pain points with care, never exploitation.

**Fragmented understanding:** Viewers may know many Buddhist terms but lack a coherent framework. They may hear about karma, merit, rebirth, emptiness, or compassion without understanding how these ideas relate.

**Emotional suffering:** Anxiety, grief, anger, regret, guilt toward parents, fear of death, loneliness, and uncertainty can drive viewers toward spiritual content.

**Fear-based religious messaging:** Some viewers have been exposed to content that uses hell realms, karmic punishment, ghosts, or ritual warnings in a frightening or manipulative way. This channel must provide a steadier alternative.

**Modern stress and distraction:** Viewers live with digital overload, economic pressure, family obligations, and emotional fatigue. They need teachings translated into modern conditions without being reduced to productivity advice.

**Intergenerational distance:** Younger viewers may feel disconnected from temple language; older viewers may feel younger generations are losing respect for tradition. The channel can become a bridge.

**Confusion between Buddhism and superstition:** Viewers may struggle to distinguish symbolic, devotional, karmic, cosmological, psychological, and historical claims. The channel must clarify without contempt.

**Lack of trustworthy narrative content:** Many Buddhist stories are either too dry, too fantastical, or too moralistic in modern media. The channel should provide cinematic storytelling with interpretive responsibility.

### Transformation Goals

The desired viewer transformation is not simply more views, longer watch time, or stronger emotional reaction. The transformation should be spiritual, ethical, and reflective.

After sustained engagement with the channel, viewers should:

**Understand Buddhist teachings more clearly.** They should be able to explain key ideas in simple terms and recognize how different teachings connect.

**Feel calmer and less alone.** Videos should provide emotional steadiness, especially around suffering, grief, family duty, and impermanence.

**Become more ethically attentive.** Viewers should reflect on speech, intention, consumption, anger, generosity, patience, and responsibility.

**Relate to family with more gratitude and wisdom.** The channel should deepen understanding of hiếu đạo without creating unhealthy guilt or enabling harmful family dynamics.

**Approach karma with responsibility rather than fear.** Karma should be understood as moral causality and intentional action, not as a simplistic punishment system.

**Practice more consistently.** Viewers may chant, meditate, reflect, study, repent, give, forgive, or pause before reacting.

**Hold mystery with humility.** They should neither mock traditional cosmology nor cling to sensational certainty.

**Develop trust in the channel as a spiritual library.** They should feel that returning to the channel is like returning to a calm and reliable learning space.

## 8. Channel Positioning

The channel is positioned as a cinematic Buddhist learning and reflection platform for modern life.

It should sit at the intersection of:

**Buddhist education:** Clear explanations of sutras, teachings, figures, and concepts.

**Spiritual reflection:** Emotional and ethical contemplation for daily life.

**Cinematic storytelling:** Narrative immersion, visual atmosphere, and sound design that support attention.

**Responsible modern interpretation:** Careful connection between ancient teachings and contemporary psychological, familial, and social realities.

The channel should not position itself as a temple, monastic authority, academic institution, therapy provider, fortune-telling platform, miracle channel, or generic mindfulness brand. It may learn from each of these domains, but it must remain distinct.

The channel's comparative space includes Buddhist Dharma talks, sutra reading channels, meditation channels, documentary channels, spiritual storytelling channels, and self-help channels. Its differentiation lies in combining reverence, clarity, cinematic production, and editorial integrity.

The channel should be recognizable by its calm seriousness. It should not feel overly casual, preachy, academic, mystical in a sensational way, or artificially dramatic. It should feel like a crafted contemplative experience: emotionally warm, intellectually clear, visually refined, and ethically grounded.

In audience language, the positioning may be summarized as: a place to understand Buddhist wisdom deeply and feel it gently in the heart.

## 9. Unique Selling Proposition

The unique selling proposition is:

**A cinematic Buddhist wisdom library that explains sacred teachings with clarity, emotional depth, and ethical integrity for modern viewers.**

This proposition has four essential components:

**Cinematic:** The channel uses narrative form, sound, imagery, pacing, and atmosphere to make teachings memorable. Cinematic does not mean overproduced spectacle. It means intentional emotional architecture.

**Buddhist Wisdom Library:** The channel is not dependent on one viral format. It is designed as a structured, expandable library across sutras, figures, practices, ethical themes, and modern reflections.

**Clarity and Emotional Depth:** The channel teaches and moves the viewer. It avoids the false choice between intellectual explanation and heartfelt resonance.

**Ethical Integrity:** The channel protects viewers from exaggeration, superstition, sectarian arrogance, historical carelessness, and manipulative fear.

No future content should weaken this proposition. A video that is merely relaxing but not meaningfully Buddhist is off-position. A video that is doctrinally dense but emotionally inaccessible is incomplete. A video that is cinematic but inaccurate is unacceptable. A video that relies on fear or miracle claims for attention directly contradicts the proposition.

## 10. Viewer Experience

The desired viewer experience is contemplative, trustworthy, and emotionally absorbing.

A viewer should feel, within the first minute, that the channel is different from ordinary spiritual content. The pacing should create room for attention. The opening should establish relevance without sensationalism. The voice should feel calm and grounded. The visual world should be intentional. The topic should be framed clearly enough that the viewer knows why it matters.

The viewer experience should contain these qualities:

**A sense of arrival:** The video should help the viewer transition from distraction to presence. Openings should not be loud, rushed, or gimmicky.

**A clear path:** The viewer should understand the structure of the video. Even contemplative content needs progression: question, context, teaching, reflection, application, closing insight.

**Emotional safety:** Sensitive topics such as death, hell realms, karma, illness, parental guilt, and grief must be handled with care. The viewer should feel guided, not threatened.

**Spiritual seriousness:** The channel should not flatten Buddhism into lifestyle aesthetics. It should communicate that the teachings matter.

**Human relevance:** Concepts should be connected to lived experience. Viewers should recognize themselves in the discussion without feeling exposed or judged.

**Aesthetic restraint:** Music, visuals, and narration should support the teaching. They should not compete with it.

**Afterglow:** A good video should leave the viewer quieter, clearer, and more reflective. The ending should not feel like a sales pitch or abrupt algorithmic handoff.

The channel should also design experience at the library level. Playlists, series naming, visual identity, and episode sequencing should help viewers navigate according to need: grief, study, meditation, sutra, family, moral reflection, or Buddhist history.

## 11. Viewer Emotional Journey

The emotional journey of a typical video should move from recognition to clarity to reflection to intention.

**Recognition:** The viewer first recognizes a human problem or spiritual question. This may be sorrow, anger, attachment, fear of death, guilt toward parents, confusion about karma, or curiosity about a sutra. Recognition should be gentle. It should not accuse the viewer.

**Opening of attention:** The video creates enough calm and interest for the viewer to stay. This may come through a question, image, story fragment, or concise framing of the teaching.

**Contextual grounding:** The video explains where the teaching comes from, what tradition or text it belongs to, and what problem it addresses. This prevents vague spirituality.

**Deepening:** The teaching becomes more layered. The viewer begins to see causes, conditions, ethical implications, and inner patterns.

**Emotional resonance:** The video connects the teaching to lived experience. This is where the viewer may feel grief, gratitude, humility, repentance, relief, or compassion.

**Integration:** The video offers a way to carry the insight into life. This may be a question to contemplate, a small practice, a shift in attitude, or a moral commitment.

**Quiet closure:** The ending should return the viewer to stillness. It should not overstate transformation. It should allow the teaching to continue inside the viewer.

Across the channel, the long-term emotional journey should evolve. A new viewer may arrive looking for comfort. Over time, the channel should invite deeper study, stronger ethical responsibility, more mature understanding of Buddhist teachings, and more consistent practice.

The emotional tone should vary by series:

**Sutra education:** Reverent, clear, luminous, careful.

**Karmic reflection:** Serious, responsible, compassionate, non-frightening.

**Hiếu đạo and filial piety:** Tender, honest, nuanced, grateful, not guilt-driven.

**Buddhist psychology:** Insightful, practical, calm, precise.

**Lives of Buddhist figures:** Cinematic, inspiring, historically careful, humanizing.

**Healing and grief:** Gentle, spacious, non-clinical, supportive.

**Meditation and mindfulness:** Simple, grounded, experiential, non-performative.

## 12. Long-term Growth Strategy

The long-term growth strategy is to build a durable Buddhist content library, not a short-lived trend channel. Growth should come from trust, consistency, quality, search value, playlist depth, and emotional return.

The strategy has several phases.

### Phase 1: Foundation

The channel should establish identity, tone, and editorial reliability. Initial series should include a balanced mix of accessible themes and core Buddhist texts. Kinh Địa Tạng may serve as an early anchor if existing source materials are available, but the channel must immediately signal broader Buddhist scope. Early content should prove the channel's ability to explain, reflect, and tell stories.

Foundation goals:

**Define the voice:** Calm, reverent, clear, emotionally intelligent.

**Create anchor pillars:** Sutra education, Buddhist life reflection, karma and ethics, filial piety, mindfulness, and Buddhist stories.

**Establish quality expectations:** Consistent audio, pacing, source handling, and visual mood.

**Build evergreen value:** Avoid over-reliance on current events or trend hooks.

### Phase 2: Library Expansion

Once the identity is stable, the channel should expand into structured playlists and recurring series. Each series should have a clear promise and predictable viewer need.

Expansion goals:

**Develop sutra pathways:** Kinh Địa Tạng, Kinh Pháp Hoa, Kinh Kim Cang, Kinh A Di Đà, Kinh Dược Sư.

**Develop practice pathways:** Thiền, mindfulness, chanting, repentance, compassion practice, gratitude practice.

**Develop life pathways:** Grief, family, parents, aging, illness, work stress, anger, forgiveness.

**Develop figure pathways:** Đức Phật, Quán Thế Âm, Địa Tạng, Văn Thù, Phổ Hiền, Dược Sư, A La Hán figures, major disciples.

**Develop concept pathways:** Karma, rebirth, emptiness, impermanence, non-self, compassion, wisdom, vows, merit, refuge.

### Phase 3: Authority Through Depth

The channel should earn authority by producing deeper, more carefully researched series that viewers can return to. This may include multi-part sutra explorations, thematic documentaries, and structured educational arcs.

Authority goals:

**Create definitive internal guides:** Each major topic should have a flagship video or series.

**Improve research systems:** Maintain source notes, doctrinal distinctions, glossary standards, and fact-check procedures.

**Build repeatable production templates:** Not rigid formulas, but stable workflows that preserve quality.

**Invite expert review when possible:** Monastics, scholars, translators, or experienced practitioners can review sensitive or complex topics.

### Phase 4: Ecosystem Growth

Long-term expansion may include multilingual versions, podcasts, community study guides, newsletters, audio-only meditation/reflection releases, seasonal series around Buddhist holidays, and companion reading paths. Expansion must not weaken editorial integrity.

Ecosystem goals:

**Create learning journeys:** Beginner Buddhism, sutra study, grief and healing, family and filial piety, meditation foundations.

**Create seasonal programming:** Vesak, Vu Lan, Ullambana, Lunar New Year reflections, year-end karma and repentance, memorial themes.

**Support multiple content lengths:** Short reflections, standard long-form videos, extended cinematic essays, and audio contemplations.

**Maintain brand coherence:** Every new format must obey this PRD.

Growth should never be pursued through fear thumbnails, miracle claims, sectarian conflict, exaggerated titles, or exploitative urgency. The channel should grow because viewers trust it.

## 13. Content Pillars

The channel's content pillars define the recurring domains of production. Every video must belong clearly to at least one pillar, and major videos may connect multiple pillars.

### Pillar 1: Sutra Education

This pillar explains Buddhist scriptures, their themes, narrative structures, key concepts, and relevance to practice. It includes but is not limited to Kinh Địa Tạng, Kinh Pháp Hoa, Kinh Kim Cang, Kinh A Di Đà, and Kinh Dược Sư.

Content under this pillar should:

**Provide context:** Identify the scripture, its broad tradition, major themes, and interpretive cautions.

**Explain meaning:** Clarify important terms, images, vows, dialogues, and doctrinal ideas.

**Avoid overclaiming:** Do not present one interpretation as the only possible reading when traditions vary.

**Connect to life:** Show how the teaching informs ethical conduct, devotion, meditation, compassion, and wisdom.

### Pillar 2: Buddhist Ethics and Karma

This pillar explores moral causality, intention, speech, action, consequences, repentance, generosity, patience, and responsibility.

Content under this pillar should:

**Teach karma responsibly:** Karma is not simplistic punishment, fatalism, or a tool for blaming victims.

**Emphasize agency:** Present ethical choices as meaningful conditions that shape experience.

**Avoid fear marketing:** Hell realms and karmic consequences may be discussed, but not exploited.

**Encourage repentance and repair:** When harm has been done, the channel should guide toward acknowledgment, restraint, compassion, and corrective action.

### Pillar 3: Hiếu Đạo and Family

This pillar explores filial piety, gratitude, ancestor memory, parent-child relationships, caregiving, grief, intergenerational conflict, and family duty.

Content under this pillar should:

**Honor parents without romanticizing all families:** Some viewers have painful family histories. The channel should teach gratitude and responsibility while recognizing boundaries and harm.

**Connect to Buddhist compassion:** Hiếu đạo should be framed as part of gratitude, interdependence, and ethical maturity.

**Avoid guilt exploitation:** Do not use parental debt as a weapon against viewers.

**Support healing:** Encourage wise speech, forgiveness where possible, care, remembrance, and merit dedication.

### Pillar 4: Meditation, Mindfulness, and Inner Practice

This pillar covers Thiền, mindfulness, attention, breathing, awareness of emotions, non-reactivity, concentration, loving-kindness, and daily-life practice.

Content under this pillar should:

**Be experiential:** Viewers should understand how teachings relate to direct observation of mind and body.

**Avoid clinical overreach:** Mindfulness may support well-being, but the channel must not present itself as medical or psychiatric treatment.

**Connect practice to ethics:** Meditation is not merely stress reduction. It supports wisdom, compassion, and liberation.

**Remain simple and grounded:** Avoid exoticizing meditation.

### Pillar 5: Buddhist Psychology and Healing

This pillar interprets emotions, suffering, attachment, craving, anger, grief, shame, fear, and habit through Buddhist frameworks, with careful reference to modern psychological language where helpful.

Content under this pillar should:

**Clarify mental processes:** Explain how perception, craving, aversion, clinging, and ignorance shape suffering.

**Use modern psychology carefully:** Do not claim scientific certainty where none exists. Do not diagnose viewers.

**Support self-compassion:** Encourage awareness without self-hatred.

**Promote practical reflection:** Offer contemplative questions and gentle practices.

### Pillar 6: Lives of the Buddha, Bodhisattvas, Arahants, and Buddhist Figures

This pillar uses cinematic storytelling to introduce sacred and historical figures, including Đức Phật, Bodhisattvas, Arahants, disciples, teachers, and exemplary practitioners.

Content under this pillar should:

**Distinguish sacred narrative and historical scholarship:** Where stories are devotional, legendary, or canonical, label them appropriately.

**Humanize without diminishing:** The figures should feel meaningful, not flattened into fictional characters.

**Focus on virtues:** Compassion, wisdom, vows, patience, courage, renunciation, and service.

**Avoid spectacle for spectacle's sake:** Miraculous elements must support meaning.

### Pillar 7: Modern Buddhist Reflections

This pillar applies Buddhist teachings to modern life: work, technology, consumption, loneliness, ambition, comparison, speech online, family pressure, burnout, and social conflict.

Content under this pillar should:

**Avoid trend dependency:** Modern topics should be evergreen where possible.

**Translate without diluting:** Buddhist ideas should remain Buddhist, not generic self-help.

**Offer realistic practice:** Recommendations should be modest and applicable.

**Preserve contemplative tone:** Do not become a productivity channel.

### Pillar 8: Devotion, Ritual, and Sacred Imagination

This pillar discusses chanting, vows, merit dedication, refuge, offerings, repentance, Pure Land aspiration, Medicine Buddha practice, and Bodhisattva devotion.

Content under this pillar should:

**Respect devotional life:** Do not dismiss ritual as superstition.

**Explain symbolic and ethical dimensions:** Ritual should be connected to intention, mindfulness, gratitude, compassion, and transformation.

**Avoid transactional guarantees:** Do not promise that a chant, offering, or ritual will automatically produce wealth, health, romance, protection, or rebirth.

**Acknowledge tradition-specific differences:** Practices may vary across schools and cultures.

## 14. Series Architecture

Series architecture allows the channel to scale without losing coherence. Each series must have a clear premise, audience need, topic boundary, tone, and production pattern. Future AI writers must classify every proposed episode under an existing series or justify a new series according to this architecture.

### Series Type A: Sutra Pathways

**Purpose:** Provide structured explorations of major Buddhist scriptures.

**Examples of supported series:**

**Kinh Địa Tạng Pathway:** Focus on filial piety, vows, karmic responsibility, hell realms, compassion for beings in suffering, merit dedication, and the relationship between the living and deceased.

**Kinh Pháp Hoa Pathway:** Focus on skillful means, Buddha nature, one vehicle, Bodhisattva path, faith, parables, and universal possibility of awakening.

**Kinh Kim Cang Pathway:** Focus on emptiness, non-attachment, non-abiding mind, generosity without clinging, and the limits of conceptual identity.

**Kinh A Di Đà Pathway:** Focus on Amitabha Buddha, Pure Land aspiration, faith, vows, practice, remembrance, and hope at the threshold of death.

**Kinh Dược Sư Pathway:** Focus on healing, vows of Medicine Buddha, suffering, illness, ethical restoration, light, and compassionate protection.

**Episode design:** Each episode should explain a theme, passage, image, vow, parable, or practice implication. Episodes should not become line-by-line commentary unless explicitly designed as a commentary subseries.

### Series Type B: Buddhist Concepts

**Purpose:** Explain foundational Buddhist ideas across traditions.

Supported topics include impermanence, non-self, emptiness, dependent origination, karma, rebirth, compassion, wisdom, refuge, precepts, merit, vows, mindfulness, concentration, and liberation.

**Episode design:** Start from a human question, define the concept, correct common misunderstandings, provide examples, connect to practice, and close with reflection.

### Series Type C: Lives and Legends

**Purpose:** Use cinematic storytelling to explore the lives, virtues, and teachings of Buddhist figures.

Supported figures include Đức Phật, Queen Māyā, King Śuddhodana, Yasodharā, Rāhula, Mahākāśyapa, Ānanda, Śāriputra, Maudgalyāyana, Mahāpajāpatī Gotamī, Kṣitigarbha, Avalokiteśvara, Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, Bhaiṣajyaguru, Amitābha, and culturally important teachers where appropriate.

**Episode design:** Establish historical or sacred context, tell the narrative with dignity, interpret the virtue or teaching, and connect to modern life.

### Series Type D: Daily Life Dharma

**Purpose:** Translate Buddhist wisdom into everyday situations.

Supported topics include anger in family life, envy, work stress, grief, caring for parents, mindful speech, money and generosity, loneliness, forgiveness, aging, illness, digital distraction, and death awareness.

**Episode design:** Begin with a recognizable situation, identify the Buddhist lens, deepen through teaching, and offer a practical contemplative shift.

### Series Type E: Healing and Grief

**Purpose:** Support viewers facing loss, illness, regret, emotional pain, or fear.

**Episode design:** Use spacious pacing, gentle language, and strong ethical boundaries. Do not diagnose, promise cure, or manipulate vulnerability. Connect healing to compassion, acceptance, repentance, merit dedication, mindfulness, and community support where relevant.

### Series Type F: Meditation and Practice

**Purpose:** Guide viewers into Buddhist practice foundations and reflective exercises.

**Episode design:** May include educational explanation, guided contemplation, practice principles, or common obstacles. Distinguish teaching content from guided practice content. Avoid presenting advanced practices without context.

### Series Type G: Buddhist Questions

**Purpose:** Answer common questions with humility and clarity.

Examples include: What is karma? Is Buddhism pessimistic? What does emptiness mean? Why chant sutras? What is merit? How should Buddhists understand death? What is the difference between compassion and attachment?

**Episode design:** State the question, answer in layers, identify misunderstandings, acknowledge tradition-specific differences, and close with practical reflection.

### Series Type H: Seasonal and Ritual Reflections

**Purpose:** Support Buddhist calendar moments, family rituals, memorial periods, and cultural observances.

Examples include Vu Lan, Vesak, Lunar New Year, Ullambana, ancestor remembrance, year-end repentance, and memorial reflections.

**Episode design:** Provide cultural and Buddhist context, avoid superstition, honor emotion, and offer ethical reflection.

### Rules for Creating New Series

New series may be created only when:

**The viewer need is distinct.** The proposed series must serve a recurring need not already covered.

**The topic can sustain at least ten episodes.** A series should not be created for a one-off idea.

**The tone is identifiable.** The series should have a clear emotional and production identity.

**The series strengthens the channel library.** It must connect to the broader content pillars.

**The editorial risks are manageable.** Sensitive topics require stronger fact-check and review.

## 15. Production Principles

Production principles govern how the channel should feel and function as audiovisual media.

**Contemplative pacing:** The channel should allow room for silence, breath, and reflection. Pacing should not be sluggish, but it must not feel rushed. The viewer should be led through thought, not pushed through information.

**Voice integrity:** Narration should sound calm, warm, mature, and sincere. It should avoid exaggerated drama, artificial holiness, comedic looseness, or sales-like energy. The voice should make difficult topics feel safe to approach.

**Cinematic restraint:** Visuals should be elegant and meaningful. Use temples, landscapes, candlelight, manuscripts, symbolic imagery, human gestures, historical scenes, or abstract contemplative imagery where appropriate. Avoid visual clutter, horror aesthetics, cheap supernatural effects, and excessive stock footage.

**Sound as atmosphere:** Music should support contemplation. It should not manipulate grief, overstate holiness, or overpower narration. Silence and near-silence are production tools.

**Coherent visual identity:** Across videos, color, typography, motion, and image selection should feel consistent. The visual system may vary by series but should remain recognizably part of the same channel.

**Accessibility:** Narration should be clear. Captions and on-screen text should be readable. Complex terms should be explained. Visual beauty should not reduce comprehension.

**Respect for sacred imagery:** Images of Buddhas, Bodhisattvas, monks, temples, and rituals must be used respectfully. Do not place sacred images in sensational or trivial contexts.

**No fear thumbnails:** Thumbnails and visuals must not use horror framing for hell realms, ghosts, death, karmic punishment, or illness. Serious topics can be visually strong without becoming exploitative.

**Library consistency:** Production templates should be documented for each series: intro length, narration tone, music palette, visual motifs, pacing, and ending style.

**Human review:** Videos involving doctrine, historical claims, mental health, ritual efficacy, or sensitive family themes should receive elevated editorial review before publication.

## 16. Editorial Principles

Editorial principles govern what the channel says, how it says it, and what it refuses to do.

**Teach before persuading:** The channel should prioritize understanding. It should not pressure viewers into belief, ritual, donation, or identity.

**Respect complexity:** Buddhist traditions are diverse. Terms and teachings may differ across Theravāda, Mahāyāna, Vajrayāna, Pure Land, Chan/Zen/Thiền, and regional practices. Avoid flattening differences.

**Distinguish source, interpretation, and reflection:** A script must make clear when it is presenting a canonical story, historical claim, traditional interpretation, modern analogy, or editorial reflection.

**Avoid false certainty:** Do not state uncertain historical details, miraculous claims, or doctrinal interpretations as uncontested fact.

**Avoid moral cruelty:** Teach responsibility without blaming victims, shaming grief, or weaponizing karma.

**Preserve spiritual seriousness:** Avoid clickbait language, casual jokes about sacred material, sensational titles, or shallow moral lessons.

**Use examples carefully:** Modern examples should illuminate teachings without reducing them to self-help clichés.

**Protect vulnerable viewers:** Content on death, suicide, grief, illness, abuse, trauma, guilt, or mental health must be written with restraint and include appropriate encouragement toward professional, community, or trusted support when needed.

**Avoid sectarian superiority:** The channel may explain differences among schools, but it must not mock or denigrate legitimate Buddhist traditions.

**Maintain continuity of terminology:** Key terms should be used consistently. If multiple translations are possible, scripts should define the chosen usage.

**Respect viewer intelligence:** Do not over-explain obvious points or use vague mystical language as a substitute for thought.

**Return to practice:** Editorial content should eventually connect insight to life, conduct, attention, or compassion.

## 17. Storytelling Philosophy

The storytelling philosophy is contemplative narrative in service of wisdom.

The channel should use story not merely to entertain but to open understanding. Buddhist teaching has always used stories: the Buddha's life, Jātaka tales, parables, encounters between teachers and disciples, Bodhisattva vows, karmic narratives, and accounts of transformation. These stories are powerful because they embody ideas. They make impermanence visible, compassion memorable, and moral causality emotionally intelligible.

Storytelling should follow these principles:

**Begin with a spiritual tension:** A good story begins with a question, conflict, or human condition: fear, grief, pride, longing, confusion, sacrifice, awakening, vow, or compassion.

**Honor the teaching inside the story:** The narrative should not become detached from its Dharma meaning. Every dramatic moment should serve insight.

**Avoid over-fictionalization:** When adapting traditional stories, do not invent major motivations, dialogue, or events that distort the meaning. Creative reconstruction must be restrained and clearly compatible with source material.

**Use cinematic specificity:** Concrete images help viewers feel the story: a quiet road, a lamp at night, a mother's hands, a monk's bowl, a storm before dawn. Specificity should be tasteful, not melodramatic.

**Make characters morally alive:** Buddhist figures should not be treated as flat icons. Their virtues, choices, and struggles should reveal something about the path.

**Let silence carry meaning:** Not every insight needs to be explained immediately. Pauses, images, and simple sentences can create contemplative depth.

**End with transformation, not triumph:** Buddhist storytelling is not about victory in the worldly sense. It often ends with release, humility, vow, insight, compassion, or a changed relationship to suffering.

The channel should avoid storytelling patterns that undermine Buddhist teaching: revenge arcs, miracle spectacle without ethical meaning, simplistic good-versus-evil framing, fear-driven karmic punishment, or emotional manipulation of parents, death, and suffering.

## 18. Teaching Philosophy

The teaching philosophy is layered clarity.

Each video should teach at multiple levels so that beginners can follow and experienced viewers still gain depth. A good teaching episode should include:

**Plain-language explanation:** Define the topic simply before adding complexity.

**Context:** Explain where the teaching appears, why it matters, and how it has been understood.

**Correction of misunderstanding:** Many Buddhist concepts are misunderstood. Karma is not fate; emptiness is not nihilism; non-attachment is not indifference; compassion is not enabling harm; mindfulness is not merely relaxation.

**Analogy:** Use analogies to clarify, but do not let analogies replace doctrine.

**Application:** Show how the teaching changes perception, speech, conduct, or practice.

**Contemplation:** Offer a reflective question or inner orientation that helps the viewer continue learning after the video.

The channel should teach with humility. It should avoid declaring that "Buddhism says" when the statement applies only to one school or interpretation. It should avoid unnecessary technicality but preserve key terms where they carry important meaning. When Sanskrit, Pāli, Chinese, or Vietnamese terms are used, they should be explained naturally.

Teaching should not be aggressive. The channel should not lecture viewers as if they are morally deficient. It should illuminate causes and invite responsibility. When addressing harmful conduct, the tone can be serious, but it should remain compassionate.

The ideal teaching style is like a wise guide walking beside the viewer: clear enough to trust, gentle enough to receive, deep enough to return to.

## 19. Buddhist Philosophy Alignment

The channel must align with broadly recognized Buddhist philosophical foundations while respecting differences among traditions.

Key alignments include:

**Four Noble Truths:** Content should recognize suffering, its causes, its cessation, and the path. Even when not explicitly stated, this framework should inform the channel's approach to human problems.

**Dependent Origination:** The channel should emphasize causes and conditions rather than isolated blame. Emotions, habits, relationships, and karmic patterns arise dependently.

**Karma as intentional action:** Karma should be framed primarily around intention, action, habit, and consequence. Avoid simplistic explanations that make every suffering appear deserved.

**Impermanence:** Content should help viewers understand change, loss, aging, death, and the instability of all conditioned things.

**Non-self:** When appropriate, explain that identity is conditioned, changing, and not an independent permanent essence. Avoid presenting non-self in a way that induces nihilism or despair.

**Emptiness:** For Mahāyāna content, emptiness should be explained as the lack of inherent independent existence and the openness of phenomena through dependent arising. It must not be reduced to "nothing exists."

**Compassion and wisdom:** The channel should consistently connect insight with care for beings.

**Bodhisattva ideal:** Mahāyāna content should honor vows, compassion, skillful means, and dedication to the liberation of beings.

**Precepts and ethical conduct:** Moral discipline should be treated as foundational, not optional decoration.

**Mind training:** Meditation, mindfulness, chanting, and contemplation should be presented as ways to transform the mind and conduct, not merely as relaxation tools.

**Liberation orientation:** Even when addressing everyday life, the channel should remember that Buddhism points beyond comfort toward freedom from ignorance, craving, and suffering.

The channel may include devotional, philosophical, psychological, historical, and practical dimensions. It should not force all traditions into one doctrinal frame. When discussing Pure Land, Chan/Thiền, Theravāda, or Vajrayāna-related themes, the script should respect their internal logic and avoid careless blending.

## 20. Topics Allowed

The channel may cover a wide range of Buddhist and Buddhist-adjacent topics when handled within this PRD's standards.

Allowed topics include:

**Major scriptures:** Kinh Địa Tạng, Kinh Pháp Hoa, Kinh Kim Cang, Kinh A Di Đà, Kinh Dược Sư, Heart Sutra, Dhammapada, selected Nikāya/Āgama teachings, Jātaka stories, and other Buddhist texts when properly contextualized.

**Buddhist doctrines:** Four Noble Truths, Noble Eightfold Path, dependent origination, karma, rebirth, impermanence, non-self, emptiness, compassion, wisdom, refuge, precepts, merit, vows, mindfulness, concentration, and liberation.

**Buddhist figures:** Đức Phật, Bodhisattvas, Arahants, disciples, monastic figures, lay practitioners, and culturally significant Buddhist teachers.

**Practice:** Meditation, chanting, bowing, repentance, offering, merit dedication, loving-kindness, mindful breathing, mindful speech, compassion practice, and study.

**Ethics:** Non-harming, generosity, truthful speech, sexual responsibility, sobriety, right livelihood, care for parents, care for the dying, forgiveness, and remorse.

**Family and filial piety:** Hiếu đạo, gratitude, parent-child relationships, ancestor remembrance, caregiving, family conflict, and Vu Lan.

**Death and grief:** Impermanence, dying, mourning, memorial practices, dedication of merit, Pure Land aspiration, fear of death, and compassionate presence.

**Buddhist psychology:** Craving, aversion, ignorance, attention, perception, emotion, anger, shame, fear, grief, habit energy, and transformation.

**Modern life:** Work stress, digital distraction, consumption, ambition, comparison, loneliness, burnout, speech online, and ethical living in contemporary society.

**Historical and cultural context:** Buddhist history, transmission, temple culture, ritual meaning, art, symbols, and festivals when researched responsibly.

**Comparative clarification:** Differences among Buddhist schools may be explained respectfully.

**Cinematic sacred storytelling:** Traditional stories, parables, lives of figures, karmic tales, and symbolic narratives may be adapted with restraint.

## 21. Topics Forbidden

The channel must avoid topics and treatments that violate its ethical, spiritual, or factual standards.

Forbidden topics and treatments include:

**Fortune-telling and deterministic prediction:** Do not produce content claiming to predict a viewer's fate, karma, wealth, marriage, death, rebirth, or spiritual status.

**Miracle guarantees:** Do not promise that chanting, offerings, donations, rituals, or watching a video will guarantee healing, money, protection, debt removal, romantic success, pregnancy, legal victory, or rebirth.

**Fear-based karmic threats:** Do not create content designed to frighten viewers into engagement through hell, ghosts, curses, karmic punishment, or parental debt.

**Victim blaming:** Do not imply that illness, poverty, abuse, disability, tragedy, or grief is simply deserved due to past karma.

**Sectarian attacks:** Do not mock, denounce, or demean Buddhist schools, monastics, temples, cultural practices, or other religions.

**Political propaganda:** Do not use Buddhist teachings to endorse political parties, candidates, nationalist agendas, or partisan hostility.

**Medical misinformation:** Do not claim Buddhist practice replaces medical care, psychotherapy, medication, emergency support, or professional treatment.

**Financial exploitation:** Do not imply donations, offerings, or purchases generate guaranteed merit or worldly success.

**Graphic horror:** Do not depict hell realms, death, illness, or spirits with exploitative gore or horror aesthetics.

**Fabricated scripture:** Do not invent sutra passages, attribute modern statements to the Buddha, or present fictional teachings as canonical.

**Unlabeled fiction:** Do not present invented stories as historical or scriptural fact.

**Conspiracy spirituality:** Do not connect Buddhist teachings to conspiracy theories, secret codes, apocalyptic predictions, or hidden elites.

**Cultic authority claims:** Do not claim the channel, creator, narrator, or AI system has special spiritual authority, supernatural access, or exclusive truth.

**Manipulative guilt content:** Do not use parents, ancestors, death, or filial duty to shame viewers into fear or obedience.

## 22. Superstition Policy

The channel must handle superstition with nuance. Buddhism includes devotional practices, cosmology, ritual, merit, vows, and unseen dimensions that many practitioners hold as meaningful. The channel should not dismiss these with contempt. At the same time, it must not promote superstition, magical thinking, or transactional spirituality.

The guiding principle is: **respect devotion, reject exploitation.**

Allowed approaches:

**Explain traditional belief respectfully:** The channel may describe how certain practices are understood in Buddhist traditions.

**Present symbolic and ethical meanings:** Rituals can be explained as cultivating gratitude, humility, mindfulness, repentance, generosity, aspiration, or compassion.

**Acknowledge faith language:** Devotional traditions may speak of protection, blessing, vows, merit, or rebirth. Such language should be contextualized rather than turned into guaranteed outcomes.

**Distinguish belief from proof:** The channel should not claim empirical certainty for supernatural matters.

**Encourage wholesome intention:** The focus should be on transformation of mind and conduct.

Forbidden approaches:

**No guaranteed results:** Never say that a practice definitely causes a specific worldly outcome.

**No ritual transactions:** Do not frame offerings, chanting, or donations as spiritual purchases.

**No fear hooks:** Do not say viewers must perform an action or suffer karmic disaster.

**No spiritual ranking:** Do not claim to identify viewers' karmic level, purity, past lives, or spiritual destiny.

**No contempt:** Do not ridicule traditional practitioners as ignorant or superstitious.

When addressing topics such as ghosts, hell realms, protective chanting, merit transfer, omens, dreams, or karmic signs, scripts should use careful language:

**Preferred language:** "In many Buddhist traditions, this is understood as..."  
**Preferred language:** "The deeper ethical meaning is..."  
**Preferred language:** "Whether one approaches this devotionally, symbolically, or philosophically..."  
**Avoided language:** "This will definitely..."  
**Avoided language:** "If you do not do this, disaster will happen..."  
**Avoided language:** "Only this method can save you..."

## 23. Scientific Integrity Policy

The channel may discuss mindfulness, meditation, compassion, stress, grief, attention, and emotional healing in relation to modern psychology or science, but it must maintain scientific integrity.

The guiding principle is: **use science to clarify, not to decorate or overclaim.**

Requirements:

**Do not exaggerate research:** If referencing scientific studies, avoid broad claims that meditation cures disease, eliminates trauma, or guarantees happiness.

**Distinguish Buddhist teaching from scientific evidence:** A Buddhist claim about suffering and craving is not the same kind of claim as a clinical trial result.

**Avoid medical advice:** The channel must not tell viewers to stop medication, avoid therapy, refuse medical treatment, or replace professional care with spiritual practice.

**Use mental health language carefully:** Do not diagnose viewers or claim that a video treats depression, anxiety disorders, PTSD, addiction, or suicidality.

**Encourage appropriate support:** For severe distress, illness, self-harm risk, abuse, or trauma, content should encourage seeking qualified professional help, trusted community support, or emergency resources where appropriate.

**Avoid neuroscience clichés:** Do not use vague claims such as "rewire your brain instantly" or "activate hidden spiritual neurons."

**Be cautious with healing claims:** Buddhist practices may support resilience, meaning, emotional regulation, and compassion, but claims must remain modest.

Acceptable framing:

**"Mindfulness practices are often used to support attention and emotional regulation."**

**"Many people find chanting calming, especially when practiced with steady breath and sincere intention."**

**"Buddhist psychology offers a way to observe craving, aversion, and habit patterns."**

Unacceptable framing:

**"This chant cures anxiety."**

**"Meditation heals trauma without therapy."**

**"Science proves karma."**

**"Listening to this video changes your brain permanently."**

Scientific integrity protects both the viewer and the Dharma. Overclaiming may gain attention briefly, but it damages trust.

## 24. Historical Integrity Policy

The channel must treat history responsibly. Buddhism spans more than two millennia, many regions, languages, schools, texts, legends, and devotional traditions. Simplifying this history is sometimes necessary for accessibility, but distortion is not acceptable.

The guiding principle is: **make history clear without pretending it is simpler than it is.**

Requirements:

**Distinguish historical, canonical, legendary, and devotional material:** The life of the Buddha includes historically studied elements and sacred narrative traditions. Bodhisattva stories may be devotional or scriptural rather than historically biographical.

**Avoid false precision:** Do not invent exact dates, locations, authorship, or events where scholarship is uncertain.

**Use tradition-aware language:** For example, "According to Mahāyāna tradition..." or "In the Pāli Canon..." or "Later Buddhist traditions often tell..."

**Respect regional variations:** Vietnamese, Chinese, Tibetan, Japanese, Theravāda, and other Buddhist cultures may preserve different practices and interpretations.

**Do not modernize motives carelessly:** Avoid projecting contemporary psychology, politics, or individualism onto ancient figures without caution.

**Avoid fabricated drama:** Historical storytelling may be vivid, but it must not create false conflicts or invented details that change meaning.

**Source major claims:** Scripts should maintain internal research notes even if not all sources are spoken in the final video.

**Handle translation differences:** Buddhist terms often have multiple translations. Scripts should avoid building major claims on one translation without context.

Historical integrity does not mean every video must become academic. It means the channel should be honest about what kind of material it is presenting. A devotional story can be powerful when named as devotional. A parable can be meaningful without being treated as literal reportage. A historical uncertainty can be acknowledged without weakening the teaching.

## 25. Fact-check Policy

Every video must pass a fact-check appropriate to its risk level. The channel's fact-check system should classify content into three levels.

### Level 1: Low Risk

Low-risk content includes general reflections on compassion, mindfulness, gratitude, patience, impermanence, and daily life where no specific historical, medical, or doctrinal claim is central.

Requirements:

**Check basic terminology.** Ensure key Buddhist terms are used correctly.

**Check for prohibited claims.** Confirm no miracle guarantees, medical overclaims, or karmic victim blaming.

**Check tone.** Ensure compassionate and non-manipulative framing.

### Level 2: Medium Risk

Medium-risk content includes explanations of Buddhist concepts, sutra themes, devotional practices, karmic stories, meditation guidance, and modern psychology connections.

Requirements:

**Verify doctrinal framing.** Confirm the teaching is not oversimplified or sectarian.

**Check source attribution.** Identify whether claims come from scripture, tradition, commentary, scholarship, or editorial reflection.

**Review translation choices.** Ensure terms are defined and not misleading.

**Check scientific language.** Confirm no clinical or research overclaims.

**Review emotional safety.** Sensitive topics must not shame or frighten viewers.

### Level 3: High Risk

High-risk content includes historical claims, scripture-specific commentary, hell realms, death and rebirth, suicide, trauma, abuse, mental illness, serious illness, ritual efficacy, controversial topics, or comparisons among Buddhist schools.

Requirements:

**Use multiple sources where possible.** Do not rely on one unverified source.

**Maintain research notes.** Record major sources and interpretive decisions.

**Expert review recommended.** Seek review from a knowledgeable practitioner, monastic, scholar, translator, or qualified professional depending on the topic.

**Check for harm.** Ensure the content cannot reasonably be interpreted as medical advice, victim blaming, fear coercion, or sectarian attack.

**Label uncertainty.** Explicitly identify tradition-specific or contested points.

### Fact-check Checklist

Before publication, every script must answer:

**What is the central teaching?**

**What sources support it?**

**Which claims are scriptural, historical, traditional, scientific, psychological, or editorial?**

**Are any claims overstated?**

**Could vulnerable viewers be harmed by this framing?**

**Does the script imply guaranteed worldly results?**

**Does the script blame suffering people for their pain?**

**Does the script distinguish belief from proof where necessary?**

**Does the script respect Buddhist diversity?**

**Does the ending guide toward wholesome reflection or practice?**

No script should move into production until fact-check concerns are resolved.

## 26. Script Lifecycle

The script lifecycle defines how future AI writers and human collaborators should move from idea to publishable script. This PRD does not generate scripts, but it governs the process.

### Stage 1: Topic Intake

Every topic must be documented with:

**Working title or topic statement**

**Primary content pillar**

**Series classification**

**Viewer need**

**Core teaching**

**Risk level**

**Primary sources or research direction**

**Expected emotional tone**

**Intended transformation**

A topic should not proceed if it lacks a clear viewer need or Buddhist teaching center.

### Stage 2: Editorial Brief

The editorial brief defines the video's purpose before writing begins. It should include:

**Audience segment:** Who is this for?

**Viewer state:** What emotional or intellectual state might bring them to this video?

**Promise:** What will the viewer understand or contemplate by the end?

**Boundaries:** What must the script avoid?

**Key terms:** Terms that require definition or consistent translation.

**Source notes:** Scriptures, traditions, historical references, or expert materials.

**Narrative approach:** Teaching essay, story-driven, guided reflection, concept explainer, or hybrid.

### Stage 3: Research and Source Mapping

Research should gather relevant source material before drafting. AI writers must not invent citations, passages, or historical details. If sources are unavailable, the script must use cautious language and request human research support.

Source mapping should classify material as:

**Canonical text**

**Traditional commentary**

**Historical scholarship**

**Devotional tradition**

**Modern teacher interpretation**

**Psychological or scientific reference**

**Editorial analogy**

This classification prevents confusion between sacred narrative, history, and reflection.

### Stage 4: Structure Design

Before drafting, the writer should create a structure. The structure should include:

**Opening movement**

**Context**

**Teaching development**

**Story or example**

**Modern application**

**Reflection**

**Closing movement**

The structure should fit the series type. Sutra Pathway episodes require more textual context. Healing episodes require more emotional spaciousness. Buddhist Questions episodes require clear answer architecture.

### Stage 5: Drafting

Drafting should follow the channel voice: calm, reverent, clear, emotionally intelligent, and restrained. Drafts must avoid clickbait, fear, miracle guarantees, and doctrinal overconfidence. Scripts should teach in layers and include transitions that help viewers follow the path of thought.

Drafts must not include unsupported claims. If a point is uncertain, the draft should either remove it, qualify it, or flag it for review.

### Stage 6: Editorial Review

Review should evaluate:

**Accuracy**

**Clarity**

**Tone**

**Viewer emotional safety**

**Series fit**

**Production feasibility**

**Pacing**

**Alignment with this PRD**

The editor should identify any language that sounds manipulative, vague, overly academic, too casual, too dramatic, or spiritually inflated.

### Stage 7: Fact-check and Sensitivity Review

The script must pass the fact-check level assigned during topic intake. Sensitive topics require additional review. Where possible, high-risk scripts should be reviewed by a qualified human.

### Stage 8: Production Adaptation

Once the script is approved, production notes may be added:

**Voice direction**

**Music direction**

**Visual motifs**

**On-screen text**

**Citation display if needed**

**Thumbnail guidance**

**Pacing notes**

Production adaptation must not change the meaning of the script without editorial approval.

### Stage 9: Final Quality Control

Before publication, check:

**No forbidden claims**

**No factual errors introduced during revision**

**No insensitive visuals**

**No misleading title or thumbnail**

**No mismatch between promise and content**

**Captions and terminology accuracy**

**Appropriate content warnings or support language where needed**

### Stage 10: Post-publication Review

After publishing, review audience response for:

**Confusion**

**Misinterpretation**

**Requests for clarification**

**Unexpected harm**

**Strong viewer needs for follow-up**

**Retention patterns**

**Comment sentiment**

The post-publication review should inform future videos without letting comments alone dictate doctrine or editorial standards.

## 27. Publishing Workflow

The publishing workflow ensures consistency from calendar planning to release.

### Step 1: Quarterly Editorial Planning

Every quarter should balance:

**Sutra education**

**Modern life reflection**

**Practice guidance**

**Cinematic stories**

**Healing or grief support**

**Seasonal Buddhist themes**

The calendar should avoid clustering too many heavy topics such as death, hell realms, grief, and karmic consequences without lighter or more hopeful teachings.

### Step 2: Monthly Series Allocation

Each month should include a mix of content types. A sample allocation might include:

**One sutra or doctrine episode**

**One modern life Dharma reflection**

**One story or figure episode**

**One healing, family, or practice episode**

Actual frequency may change, but the principle is balance.

### Step 3: Topic Approval

Topics should be approved based on:

**Fit with content pillars**

**Viewer need**

**Evergreen value**

**Risk level**

**Research feasibility**

**Series balance**

**Production capacity**

### Step 4: Research and Briefing

No script should be assigned without a clear brief. Research must precede drafting for medium-risk and high-risk topics.

### Step 5: Script Development

Script drafting should follow the lifecycle in Section 26. AI-generated drafts must be reviewed for hallucinations, tone drift, and unsupported claims.

### Step 6: Editorial Approval

The editor approves scripts only after confirming PRD alignment. Approval should not be based solely on emotional power or expected performance.

### Step 7: Production

Production teams create voice, visuals, music, captions, and packaging. Production must preserve the script's meaning and tone. Any major interpretive change returns to editorial review.

### Step 8: Packaging Review

Titles, thumbnails, descriptions, chapters, and pinned comments must align with the same ethical standards as scripts. Packaging must not exaggerate, frighten, or promise outcomes the video does not support.

Packaging rules:

**Titles should be clear and meaningful.**

**Thumbnails should be visually calm and compelling.**

**Descriptions should include context and disclaimers where appropriate.**

**Chapters should help navigation.**

**Pinned comments may invite reflection but should not manipulate engagement.**

### Step 9: Release

Release timing may consider audience habits, Buddhist holidays, and seasonal emotional needs. However, quality is more important than rigid schedule.

### Step 10: Analytics and Reflection

After release, review both quantitative and qualitative performance. A video may be successful even if it is not the highest-performing upload, especially if it deepens trust or serves a sensitive viewer need.

### Step 11: Library Maintenance

Older videos should be revisited periodically for:

**Outdated descriptions**

**Terminology inconsistency**

**Broken links**

**Viewer confusion**

**Need for follow-up episodes**

**Potential playlist repositioning**

The channel is a living library. Maintenance is part of publishing.

## 28. Success Metrics

Success must be measured in a way that reflects both YouTube realities and the channel's spiritual purpose.

### Quantitative Metrics

**Watch time:** Indicates sustained viewer attention. Important for long-form contemplative content.

**Average view duration:** Helps evaluate pacing and structure.

**Retention curve:** Identifies where viewers lose clarity, interest, or emotional connection.

**Returning viewers:** A key indicator of trust and long-term library value.

**Subscriber conversion:** Shows whether viewers want an ongoing relationship with the channel.

**Playlist progression:** Measures whether viewers move through learning pathways.

**Search traffic:** Indicates evergreen educational value.

**Suggested traffic:** Indicates topic and packaging resonance.

**Comment volume and quality:** Useful when interpreted carefully.

**Shares:** Strong signal for content that viewers find meaningful or healing.

**Saves and watch later behavior:** Indicates perceived depth and future value.

**Completion rate by format:** Helps refine series-specific pacing.

### Qualitative Metrics

**Viewer trust:** Comments and messages suggesting the channel feels reliable, respectful, and clear.

**Emotional impact:** Viewers report feeling calmer, less alone, more reflective, or more compassionate.

**Learning impact:** Viewers say they finally understand a concept, sutra, or practice.

**Practice impact:** Viewers report chanting, meditating, apologizing, caring for parents, reflecting on speech, or returning to study.

**Community tone:** Comment sections remain respectful, reflective, and non-combative.

**Expert confidence:** Knowledgeable reviewers do not identify serious distortions.

**Library coherence:** New videos feel connected to older ones without repetition fatigue.

### Negative Metrics and Warning Signals

The channel must monitor warning signs:

**High clicks but low trust:** Sensational packaging may produce clicks while weakening brand integrity.

**Comments driven by fear:** If viewers express panic about karma, death, or rituals, framing may need correction.

**Sectarian arguments:** Content may need clearer tradition-aware language.

**Misinterpretation of medical content:** Add stronger disclaimers and reframe future episodes.

**Overdependence on one topic:** If only death, hell, or miracle-related videos perform, resist letting the channel narrow around fear.

**Tone drift:** If scripts become generic self-help or dramatic religious warning content, return to this PRD.

### Strategic Success Definition

The channel succeeds when it builds a growing body of videos that viewers trust, revisit, share in meaningful moments, and use as a bridge into deeper Buddhist reflection and practice.

It fails if it gains attention by weakening the Dharma, exploiting suffering, or confusing viewers about what Buddhism teaches.

## 30. Future AI Collaboration Rules

Future AI agents must treat this PRD as binding project context. AI systems may help with research organization, topic ideation, outlines, drafts, revisions, metadata, visual briefs, and quality control, but they must follow the rules below.

### Rule 1: Do Not Invent Authority

AI writers must not claim personal spiritual realization, monastic authority, lineage authorization, supernatural knowledge, or exclusive interpretation. The channel voice may be wise and reflective, but it must not pretend to be a guru.

### Rule 2: Do Not Fabricate Sources

AI must never invent sutra passages, citations, teachers, historical dates, scholarly claims, or traditional stories. If uncertain, AI must flag uncertainty and request source verification.

### Rule 3: Classify Claims

AI must distinguish:

**Canonical claim**

**Traditional interpretation**

**Historical claim**

**Devotional belief**

**Modern psychological framing**

**Editorial reflection**

This classification may appear in internal notes even when not spoken directly in the final video.

### Rule 4: Preserve the Channel Voice

The voice must be calm, reverent, clear, emotionally intelligent, and restrained. AI must avoid melodrama, clickbait, fear language, exaggerated certainty, generic self-help clichés, and overly academic density.

### Rule 5: Protect Vulnerable Viewers

AI must use extra care with death, grief, suicide, illness, abuse, trauma, depression, anxiety, guilt, and family conflict. It must not diagnose, shame, frighten, or promise cure. It should encourage qualified support when appropriate.

### Rule 6: Respect Buddhist Diversity

AI must not collapse all Buddhist schools into one undifferentiated voice. It should identify tradition-specific teachings when relevant and avoid sectarian superiority.

### Rule 7: Avoid Superstition and Transactional Claims

AI must not promise worldly outcomes from chants, rituals, offerings, donations, or viewing content. It may discuss devotional practices respectfully while emphasizing intention, ethics, and transformation.

### Rule 8: Keep Karma Compassionate and Responsible

AI must present karma as moral causality involving intention and conditions. It must not blame victims or imply suffering people deserve their pain. It should emphasize agency, repentance, compassion, and wise action.

### Rule 9: Maintain Scientific Integrity

AI must not overstate the scientific evidence for meditation, chanting, mindfulness, or Buddhist practice. It must not offer medical advice. It must not use neuroscience or psychology as decorative authority.

### Rule 10: Maintain Historical Integrity

AI must distinguish historical scholarship from sacred narrative. It must avoid false precision and invented drama. It should use careful language when evidence is uncertain.

### Rule 11: Build the Library, Not Isolated Content

AI should always consider how a video fits into content pillars, series architecture, playlists, and long-term viewer journeys. New ideas should strengthen the channel ecosystem.

### Rule 12: Avoid Repetition Fatigue

AI should preserve consistent principles while varying structure, examples, emotional angle, and narrative form. Consistency does not mean repeating the same phrases in every video.

### Rule 13: Use Human Review for High-risk Topics

AI-generated content on high-risk topics must be reviewed by humans with relevant knowledge whenever possible. AI should identify risk level early.

### Rule 14: Do Not Produce Scripts When Asked for Strategy

If the task is to create strategy, PRD, outline, or editorial architecture, AI must not drift into full script generation. It should produce the requested strategic artifact only.

### Rule 15: Check Against Forbidden Topics

Before any output, AI must scan for forbidden claims: fortune-telling, miracle guarantees, fear threats, victim blaming, sectarian attacks, medical misinformation, fabricated scripture, and manipulative guilt.

### Rule 16: Maintain Terminology Discipline

AI should keep a glossary for recurring terms and use translations consistently. When multiple terms are possible, define them.

### Rule 17: Use Emotional Power Responsibly

AI may write moving content, but must not manipulate. Tears are not a success metric by themselves. Emotional resonance must serve clarity, compassion, and practice.

### Rule 18: Prioritize Evergreen Value

AI should prefer topics and framings that remain meaningful over years. Trend-based hooks should be rare and carefully adapted.

### Rule 19: State Limitations

When a topic requires deeper source verification, monastic input, translation review, or medical expertise, AI must say so in internal notes and not overstep.

### Rule 20: Final Alignment Check

Before delivering any future artifact, AI must verify:

**Does it serve the vision?**

**Does it fit a content pillar and series?**

**Does it preserve voice and tone?**

**Are claims properly bounded?**

**Does it avoid forbidden topics?**

**Does it support viewer transformation?**

**Will it still feel appropriate years from now?**
