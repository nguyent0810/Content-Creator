# 02 Buddhist Guide: Doctrinal and Editorial Handbook for AI-Assisted Buddhist Content

## Document Control

**Document Type:** Permanent Buddhist Editorial Handbook  
**Governing Specifications:** `PROJECT_PRD.md` and `01_BRAND_BIBLE.md`  
**Primary Function:** Doctrinal, research, interpretation, and safety reference for all future Buddhist content work  
**Users:** AI writers, researchers, editors, fact-checkers, reviewers, producers, translators, and human supervisors  
**Scope:** Buddhist education, spiritual reflection, doctrinal explanation, historical context, devotional framing, ethical interpretation, Buddhist psychology, meditation, grief, family, and modern application  
**Excluded Outputs:** Scripts, episode ideas, thumbnails, prompts, finished titles, marketing campaigns, and direct viewer counseling  
**Permanent Standard:** Accuracy with humility, clarity with compassion, reverence without superstition, and modern relevance without doctrinal dilution

## 1. Scope of the Guide

This guide defines how Buddhist teachings may be researched, interpreted, explained, adapted, and reviewed for the channel. It is a production handbook, not a doctrinal constitution for Buddhism as a whole. Its role is to prevent drift, hallucination, superstition, historical carelessness, exploitative language, and inconsistent editorial behavior across a long-term channel that may produce hundreds of videos over many years.

The guide applies to every Buddhist topic handled by the channel, including Kinh Địa Tạng, Kinh Pháp Hoa, Kinh Kim Cang, Kinh A Di Đà, Kinh Dược Sư, Thiền, Bát Chánh Đạo, Tứ Diệu Đế, nhân quả, hiếu đạo, Đức Phật, Bồ Tát, A-la-hán, Buddhist history, Buddhist psychology, mindfulness, ethics, compassion, healing, grief, devotional practice, ritual, and modern Buddhist reflections.

The guide governs internal production standards. It does not authorize the channel to speak as a temple, lineage, monastic authority, academic institution, therapist, medical provider, fortune-teller, or final arbiter of Buddhist truth. Future writers must treat the channel as a careful guide into Buddhist learning, not as a replacement for teachers, temples, communities, texts, qualified scholars, or professional care.

This guide should be used before research, during outlining, during drafting, during fact-checking, and during final review. It is especially important whenever a topic involves scripture, karma, rebirth, hell realms, ritual efficacy, death, suicide, mental health, trauma, abuse, family duty, supernatural claims, historical uncertainty, or differences among Buddhist traditions.

When uncertainty exists, this guide requires future collaborators to name uncertainty, classify the claim, seek better sources, narrow the statement, or escalate to human review. It is never acceptable to hide uncertainty behind confident narration.

## 2. Canonical Sources

Canonical sources are texts recognized within Buddhist traditions as authoritative scripture or sacred teaching. Because Buddhism is diverse, there is no single canon used identically by all traditions. The channel must therefore identify the relevant canonical family when using scriptural material.

Accepted canonical source families include the Pāli Canon, Chinese Āgamas, Mahāyāna sūtras preserved in Chinese, Sanskrit, Tibetan, or other transmission lines, Pure Land scriptures, major Vajrayāna canonical materials when handled with appropriate care, and regionally transmitted Buddhist scriptures used in Vietnamese, Chinese, Korean, Japanese, Tibetan, and Southeast Asian traditions. The channel may also use widely respected anthologies such as the Dhammapada when properly contextualized.

For this channel's expected scope, the most commonly relevant sources include Kinh Địa Tạng, Kinh Pháp Hoa, Kinh Kim Cang, Kinh A Di Đà, Kinh Dược Sư, the Heart Sutra, selected Nikāya or Āgama teachings, selected Jātaka stories, and narratives concerning the Buddha, Bodhisattvas, Arahants, disciples, and devotional figures. These sources must not be treated as interchangeable. A Mahāyāna sūtra should not be described as if all Buddhist schools use it in the same way. A Pāli discourse should not be framed as the complete voice of all Buddhism.

AI systems must never fabricate canonical passages. If exact wording is not verified, the content must paraphrase cautiously and identify the text generally, or request source verification. Direct quotation requires reliable source text, translation review, and citation notes. Paraphrase still requires source discipline.

Canonical status must be separated from historical authorship. A text may be canonical within a tradition without being historically attributed by scholars to the earliest strata of Buddhist teaching. The channel may respect canonical authority while remaining historically honest.

## 3. Source Priority Hierarchy

The source priority hierarchy determines how future writers should resolve conflicts, select evidence, and classify claims. It does not mean only top-level sources matter. It means stronger claims require stronger sources.

**Level 1: Canonical texts in recognized Buddhist traditions.** These are the highest priority for explaining what a scripture or core teaching says. Use reliable translations, editions, or established recensions. Do not quote from memory or unsourced online fragments.

**Level 2: Traditional commentaries and respected lineage explanations.** These help interpret canonical material. They must be identified as commentary or tradition, not as the original scripture unless they are directly quoting it.

**Level 3: Scholarly historical research.** Academic scholarship is essential for historical context, dating, transmission, textual history, and comparative analysis. Scholarly claims must be distinguished from devotional claims.

**Level 4: Teachings by respected contemporary monastics, teachers, translators, and practitioners.** These may clarify practice and modern application but should not override canonical or historical accuracy without explanation.

**Level 5: Cultural practice and local devotional tradition.** These sources are valuable for Vietnamese and regional Buddhist life, ritual meaning, filial piety, chanting, holidays, and ancestor remembrance. They must not be universalized as all Buddhist doctrine.

**Level 6: Editorial reflection and analogy.** This includes modern examples, psychological bridges, cinematic narration, and contemplative interpretation. These are useful but must be labeled internally as editorial, not scriptural or historical.

When sources conflict, the writer must not flatten them into a false consensus. The correct response is to identify the tradition, narrow the claim, or say that different traditions interpret the topic differently. The channel's authority comes from clarity and humility, not from pretending complexity does not exist.

## 4. Accepted Buddhist Traditions

The channel may respectfully draw from major Buddhist traditions when the topic calls for it. Accepted traditions include Theravāda, Mahāyāna, Vajrayāna when handled carefully, Pure Land, Chan, Zen, Thiền, Tiantai, Huayan, Yogācāra, Madhyamaka, Vietnamese Buddhist traditions, Chinese Buddhist traditions, East Asian devotional traditions, and relevant regional cultural practices.

The channel's default editorial orientation is broadly Buddhist with strong Mahāyāna compatibility, because the planned scripture set includes Kinh Địa Tạng, Kinh Pháp Hoa, Kinh Kim Cang, Kinh A Di Đà, and Kinh Dược Sư. However, this orientation does not authorize sectarian dismissal of Theravāda, Vajrayāna, or other traditions. When content is Mahāyāna-specific, it should be described as such. When a teaching is broadly shared, it may be presented more generally.

Accepted traditions must be represented on their own terms. Pure Land should not be reduced to passive belief. Thiền should not be reduced to relaxation. Theravāda should not be reduced to individual liberation without compassion. Vajrayāna should not be reduced to exotic ritual. Mahāyāna should not be reduced to cosmic mythology. Vietnamese Buddhist practice should not be reduced to ancestor ritual or superstition.

The channel may compare traditions only for clarification, never for superiority. Comparative content must avoid implying that one legitimate Buddhist tradition is primitive, magical, corrupted, incomplete, or spiritually inferior. If a tradition-specific practice requires initiation, teacher supervision, or lineage context, the channel must say so and avoid instructional overreach.

The accepted tradition standard is: explain respectfully, distinguish carefully, and never collapse Buddhist diversity into one simplified voice.

## 5. How to Handle Different Interpretations

Different Buddhist traditions often interpret the same concept, text, figure, or practice differently. The channel must treat interpretive plurality as normal rather than as a problem to hide.

When interpretations differ, writers must first identify the type of difference. A difference may be textual, doctrinal, historical, devotional, ritual, cultural, linguistic, or pedagogical. For example, emptiness may be explained differently in Madhyamaka, Yogācāra, and popular teaching contexts. Pure Land practice may be interpreted devotionally, psychologically, metaphysically, or as skillful means depending on tradition. Karma may be explained through intention, habit, rebirth, social consequence, or moral cultivation depending on context.

The channel should use a canonical framing method:

**State the shared ground when it exists.** Begin with what most Buddhist traditions broadly affirm.

**Name the tradition-specific frame.** Identify Mahāyāna, Theravāda, Pure Land, Thiền, or other context when relevant.

**Avoid forcing resolution.** Do not declare one interpretation final unless the video is explicitly framed within that tradition and sources support it.

**Explain why the interpretation matters for practice.** The viewer should understand the practical or ethical significance, not only the difference itself.

**Maintain humility.** Use phrasing such as "many traditions understand this as," "within this Mahāyāna context," or "historically, scholars often distinguish."

AI writers must never invent a harmonized doctrine that no tradition actually teaches. If a difference is beyond the production team's expertise, the content must narrow its scope or escalate for review.

## 6. Scripture Citation Standards

Scripture citation must be careful, transparent, and proportionate to the format. The channel may not always display academic citations on screen, but internal research notes must preserve the source trail.

Every scriptural claim should identify at least four things internally: the text name, the tradition or canon family, the translation or edition used, and whether the language is a direct quotation or paraphrase. If the final video uses a direct quote, the quote must be checked word for word against a reliable translation. If the final video paraphrases, the paraphrase must not add claims absent from the source.

Do not quote scripture from unsourced social posts, unattributed websites, memory, or AI output. Do not attribute modern inspirational lines to the Buddha unless verified by a reliable source. Do not treat a poetic paraphrase as scripture. Do not combine fragments from different texts into a single quotation.

When citing Mahāyāna sutras in Vietnamese contexts, use the Vietnamese title when appropriate and include the Sanskrit, Chinese, or English title in research notes if useful. For example, Kinh Kim Cang may be mapped to the Vajracchedikā Prajñāpāramitā Sūtra. Kinh A Di Đà may be mapped to the Shorter Sukhāvatīvyūha Sūtra. These mappings help researchers avoid confusion.

If a passage has multiple translations with meaningful differences, the writer must not build a major argument on one wording without review. Use general meaning unless the exact wording is important. When exact wording is important, compare translations and note the choice.

Scripture citation is not decoration. It exists to preserve trust, prevent hallucination, and protect sacred material from careless use.

## 7. Historical Claims Standards

Historical claims must be handled according to evidence, not devotional confidence or cinematic convenience. Buddhism contains historical persons, canonical narratives, legendary expansions, regional traditions, and devotional symbolism. The channel must not collapse them into one category.

A historical claim is any statement about dates, places, authorship, events, transmission, social context, textual development, institutional history, or biographical detail. Such claims require source support. The stronger and more specific the claim, the stronger the source requirement.

Preferred historical language includes "historically attested," "traditionally understood," "according to later tradition," "scholars often date," "the text is preserved in," "the tradition presents," and "the historical record is limited." Avoid false precision such as exact dates, locations, motives, or conversations where evidence is uncertain.

The life of the Buddha must be handled with special care. The channel may tell canonical and traditional life narratives reverently, but it should not claim every narrative detail is confirmed by modern historical scholarship. Similarly, Bodhisattva narratives may be sacred and meaningful without being historical biographies. Arahant and disciple narratives may come from canonical sources, commentaries, or later tradition and should be labeled accordingly.

Historical uncertainty should not be treated as a threat to faith. It can be framed as part of honest learning. The channel can say that a story has been preserved because it teaches a virtue, even when historical verification is limited.

AI writers must never invent historical drama to make a story more cinematic. Emotional power must come from the teaching, not fabricated certainty.

## 8. Legendary Narratives vs Historical Narratives

Legendary narratives and historical narratives serve different editorial functions. Both may appear in Buddhist content, but they must not be confused.

A historical narrative presents events or persons as part of the recoverable historical record. It requires evidence and careful sourcing. A legendary narrative presents a story preserved in scripture, commentary, folklore, devotional tradition, or cultural memory. It may contain miraculous events, cosmic settings, symbolic figures, or moral patterns that are not treated as modern historical reportage.

Legendary narratives are not automatically false, trivial, or disposable. In Buddhist traditions, legends often carry ethical, devotional, symbolic, and contemplative meaning. They may teach compassion, vow, sacrifice, repentance, impermanence, filial piety, or the consequences of intention. The channel should respect legendary material while naming it appropriately.

Operational rules:

**Label narrative type internally before writing.**

**Do not present legendary detail as confirmed history.**

**Do not strip legendary stories of meaning by reducing them to fantasy.**

**Do not add invented details that change the teaching.**

**Use symbolic interpretation when it clarifies, not when it dismisses.**

**Avoid horror, spectacle, or sensational framing of miraculous material.**

When a story includes supernatural elements, the writer may say, "In the traditional narrative," "the sutra presents," or "the story expresses." This allows reverent storytelling without false historical claims. The viewer should be able to understand both the story's sacred meaning and the limits of historical certainty.

## 9. Symbolic Interpretation Framework

Symbolic interpretation helps the channel explain Buddhist imagery, ritual, cosmology, and stories without reducing them to superstition or forcing literalism. It is especially useful for hell realms, Bodhisattva vows, Pure Land imagery, offerings, light, lotus symbols, jewels, robes, incense, dreams, and cosmic scenes.

The symbolic framework has four steps.

**Identify the literal traditional frame.** Explain how the tradition presents the image or story. Do not skip directly to modern symbolism as if traditional belief is an embarrassment.

**Identify the ethical or contemplative function.** Ask what the symbol invites the practitioner to understand, cultivate, fear wisely, release, remember, or vow.

**Connect to Buddhist doctrine.** Link symbolism to teachings such as impermanence, dependent arising, karma, compassion, wisdom, vows, mindfulness, non-attachment, or liberation.

**Apply without overclaiming.** Show how the symbol can illuminate modern experience without claiming that the modern interpretation is the only meaning.

Symbolic interpretation must never become disguised dismissal. Do not say or imply that devotional practitioners are naive because they approach a symbol literally. Also do not force every symbol into a psychological metaphor. Some traditions understand certain images cosmologically, devotionally, ritually, and symbolically at the same time.

Preferred language includes "can also be understood as," "one way to contemplate this image is," and "beyond its devotional meaning, it points toward." Avoid "this really only means" unless a source supports that reduction.

Symbolic interpretation is successful when it increases reverence, clarity, and ethical application.

## 10. Modern Reflection Framework

Modern reflection connects Buddhist teachings to contemporary life while preserving Buddhist specificity. It is not permission to turn Buddhism into generic self-help, productivity coaching, or lifestyle aesthetics.

The framework begins with a modern human condition: stress, grief, family tension, anger, digital distraction, loneliness, ambition, consumption, comparison, illness, caregiving, speech online, or fear of death. The writer must then identify the Buddhist teaching that genuinely illuminates the condition. The teaching should not be pasted onto the topic as decoration. It must shape the interpretation.

A modern reflection should follow this discipline:

**Name the modern condition without exaggeration.**

**Identify the Buddhist lens clearly.**

**Explain the teaching in context.**

**Show how the teaching reveals causes and conditions.**

**Offer a modest, practical, ethical direction.**

**Avoid promising emotional resolution.**

Modern reflections should be evergreen. Avoid dependence on temporary platform trends, political outrage, celebrity events, or slang. If a contemporary example is used, it should illuminate a durable human pattern rather than date the content.

Modern language may include psychology, neuroscience, stress, attention, burnout, or emotional regulation only when bounded carefully. The channel must not claim that ancient teachings are scientifically proven simply because they feel compatible with modern experience.

The canonical modern reflection standard is: translate the teaching into life, but do not translate the Buddhism out of the teaching.

## 11. Psychological Interpretation Framework

Buddhist psychology may be used to explain suffering, perception, craving, aversion, habit, shame, anger, fear, grief, attention, and transformation. It must be distinguished from clinical psychology, psychotherapy, psychiatry, and medical treatment.

The channel may use Buddhist psychological frameworks such as craving, clinging, ignorance, intention, perception, feeling tone, mental formations, mindfulness, compassion, non-attachment, and dependent arising. It may also reference modern psychological language when helpful, but the language must remain modest and non-diagnostic.

Operational rules:

**Do not diagnose viewers.**

**Do not claim that a video treats a disorder.**

**Do not promise that meditation or chanting cures trauma, depression, anxiety, addiction, or illness.**

**Do not replace professional care with Buddhist practice.**

**Do not use clinical labels casually for ordinary emotions.**

**Do not use neuroscience as decorative authority.**

Preferred framing includes "Buddhist psychology invites us to observe," "many people may notice," "this practice can support awareness," and "this reflection may help some viewers." Avoid universal claims such as "this will heal your trauma" or "this eliminates anxiety."

Psychological interpretation should return to ethics and practice. The goal is not self-analysis as entertainment. The goal is to understand the mind in ways that support compassion, restraint, wisdom, and liberation from harmful patterns.

When content addresses severe distress, self-harm, abuse, trauma, or mental illness, the writer must include appropriate escalation language in internal review and recommend qualified support in the final content when relevant.

## 12. Ethical Interpretation Framework

Ethical interpretation is central to Buddhist content. The channel must connect teachings to conduct, intention, speech, responsibility, compassion, and the reduction of harm.

The ethical framework begins with intention. In Buddhist teaching, actions are not evaluated only by external appearance but by intention, conditions, and consequences. A video should help viewers examine what they cultivate through body, speech, and mind.

Key ethical anchors include non-harming, generosity, truthful speech, right livelihood, sexual responsibility, sobriety, patience, compassion, gratitude, repentance, mindfulness, and care for vulnerable beings. When using the Noble Eightfold Path, precepts, karma, or Bodhisattva vows, the channel should explain how they shape daily life rather than treating them as abstract ideals.

Ethical interpretation must avoid moral cruelty. Do not use karma to blame victims. Do not use filial piety to excuse abuse. Do not use non-attachment to justify neglect. Do not use compassion to demand self-erasure. Do not use patience to silence legitimate harm. Do not use renunciation to shame people for ordinary needs.

The canonical ethical sequence is:

**See clearly.**

**Recognize intention.**

**Understand causes and conditions.**

**Take responsibility where responsibility exists.**

**Repair harm where possible.**

**Cultivate wholesome action.**

**Release hatred toward self and others.**

Ethics should feel serious but hopeful. The viewer should leave with increased moral agency, not despair.

## 13. Compassion-first Principle

The compassion-first principle requires that all Buddhist explanations be delivered in a way that reduces harm and preserves human dignity. Compassion is not softness without truth. It is truth offered with care.

This principle is especially important when discussing karma, rebirth, hell realms, illness, poverty, family conflict, grief, regret, death, addiction, abuse, suicide, and mental health. In these topics, a technically plausible doctrine can become harmful if framed without compassion.

Compassion-first means:

**Do not blame suffering people for suffering.**

**Do not intensify fear to create attention.**

**Do not use shame as a shortcut to moral seriousness.**

**Do not simplify complex pain into one cause.**

**Do not offer certainty where uncertainty exists.**

**Do not promise rescue through a ritual, video, chant, donation, or subscription.**

**Do invite reflection, support, repair, and practice.**

Compassion-first also requires respect for agency. The channel should not make viewers passive recipients of comfort. It should help them see possibilities for wise action, ethical restraint, repentance, gratitude, mindfulness, and care.

When a line is doctrinally accurate but emotionally dangerous, revise the line. When a teaching can be understood in a fear-based way, add context. When a viewer might hear blame, clarify responsibility without cruelty. When a topic is beyond the channel's role, point toward qualified support.

The permanent rule: no Buddhist teaching may be weaponized against a vulnerable viewer.

## 14. Filial Piety Framework

Filial piety, or hiếu đạo, is a major channel theme and must be handled with tenderness and nuance. It should be framed as gratitude, interdependence, responsibility, remembrance, and compassionate care, not as coercive obedience or guilt.

Buddhist filial piety may include gratitude toward parents, honoring ancestors, caring for elders, dedicating merit, reflecting on the difficulty of birth and upbringing, and recognizing the web of conditions that made one's life possible. It may be connected to Kinh Địa Tạng, Ullambana, Vu Lan, merit dedication, repentance, and family ethics.

The channel must also recognize that families are not always safe or loving. Some viewers have experienced neglect, abuse, manipulation, abandonment, or severe conflict. Content must not tell viewers to endure harm in the name of filial virtue. It must not imply that filial piety requires self-destruction, silence, or enabling abuse.

Operational rules:

**Honor parents without idealizing every parent.**

**Teach gratitude without coercive guilt.**

**Encourage care with boundaries where needed.**

**Recognize unresolved grief and complicated love.**

**Support repentance and repair where possible.**

**Do not weaponize parental debt.**

**Do not shame viewers whose families are painful.**

Filial piety content should guide viewers toward maturity: remembering kindness where it existed, grieving what was missing, acting with compassion where possible, setting wise boundaries where necessary, and dedicating wholesome intention without hatred.

## 15. Karma Interpretation Rules

Karma must be one of the most carefully governed topics on the channel. It should be explained as intentional action and moral causality shaped by causes and conditions, not as fate, punishment, revenge, blame, or a tool for predicting destiny.

Canonical rules:

**Karma is not simple punishment.**

**Karma is not fatalism.**

**Karma is not a complete explanation for every event.**

**Karma must not be used to blame victims.**

**Karma should be connected to intention, habit, speech, action, and consequences.**

**Karma should be explained within dependent arising and multiple conditions.**

**Karma should invite responsibility, not terror.**

When discussing suffering, never imply that the person deserves pain because of past karma. Buddhist traditions may include karmic explanations across lifetimes, but the channel must avoid simplistic moral accounting. Many causes and conditions shape experience: bodily conditions, social conditions, natural events, other people's actions, systems, environment, and personal choices.

When discussing wrongdoing, emphasize the possibility of recognition, remorse, restraint, confession where appropriate, repair, compassion, and renewed practice. Karma should not trap the viewer in despair. It should reveal that actions matter.

Forbidden karma language includes "this happened because you deserved it," "your illness proves past-life karma," "bad karma will punish you if you do not watch," and "this ritual erases all karma guaranteed." Such statements violate the channel's doctrine and ethics.

The core karma message is responsibility without cruelty.

## 16. Merit and Dedication Rules

Merit should be explained as wholesome spiritual benefit associated with ethical action, generosity, devotion, practice, study, repentance, compassion, and dedication. It must not be framed as spiritual currency, transactional purchase, or guaranteed worldly reward.

Merit dedication is meaningful in many Buddhist traditions, especially around chanting, offerings, ancestor remembrance, memorial practice, filial piety, Pure Land aspiration, and compassion for beings in suffering. The channel may explain these practices reverently. It must also avoid claims that merit dedication mechanically guarantees a specific outcome for the dead, the sick, the poor, or the practitioner.

Operational rules:

**Connect merit to intention and wholesome action.**

**Explain dedication as an act of compassion and aspiration.**

**Respect devotional belief without making guarantees.**

**Avoid financial transaction framing.**

**Avoid implying that watching or sharing a video creates guaranteed merit.**

**Avoid ranking viewers by merit.**

**Avoid claims that one ritual cancels all consequences.**

Acceptable language: "Many Buddhist traditions encourage dedicating merit with compassion and sincere intention." Unacceptable language: "Do this and your loved one will definitely be saved." The first honors practice; the second exploits grief and overclaims.

Merit content should encourage humility. Wholesome action is not a spiritual investment strategy. It is part of transforming intention, softening self-centeredness, and directing goodness toward the welfare of beings.

## 17. Rebirth Language Rules

Rebirth must be handled with doctrinal respect and epistemic humility. It is a significant Buddhist teaching across many traditions, though interpreted differently in philosophical, devotional, cosmological, and modern contexts.

The channel may discuss rebirth as part of Buddhist doctrine, karma, samsara, Pure Land aspiration, hell realms, heavenly realms, human life, animal realms, and liberation. It must not use rebirth to frighten viewers, predict individual destiny, or claim knowledge of a person's past or future lives.

Operational rules:

**Do not tell viewers where they or their loved ones will be reborn.**

**Do not claim to identify past-life karma.**

**Do not use rebirth as a threat.**

**Do not present speculative modern theories as Buddhist doctrine.**

**Do not claim scientific proof of rebirth unless citing properly and with great caution.**

**Do identify tradition-specific teachings when relevant.**

**Do connect rebirth to ethical urgency, compassion, and liberation.**

Rebirth language should be careful around death and grief. Bereaved viewers may be vulnerable to promises or fear. The channel may speak of aspiration, dedication of merit, remembrance, chanting, and compassionate hope, but must not guarantee the deceased person's state.

When appropriate, acknowledge that viewers may approach rebirth through faith, tradition, philosophical reflection, or uncertainty. The channel can explain the teaching without forcing belief. The permanent standard is reverence without manipulation.

## 18. Hell Realms Interpretation Rules

Hell realms are present in many Buddhist traditions and are especially relevant to Kinh Địa Tạng and karmic narratives. They must be handled with exceptional care because they can easily become fear-based content.

The channel may explain hell realms as part of Buddhist cosmology, moral imagination, karmic teaching, devotional narrative, and symbolic reflection. It must not present hell imagery as horror entertainment, threat marketing, or a tool to control viewer behavior.

Operational rules:

**Do not use graphic horror.**

**Do not imply viewers are in immediate danger if they do not perform an action.**

**Do not use hell realms to shame grieving families.**

**Do not claim knowledge of who is in a hell realm.**

**Do not dramatize punishment for spectacle.**

**Do connect hell realm teaching to compassion, moral seriousness, repentance, and vows to relieve suffering.**

**Do explain symbolic and psychological dimensions where helpful.**

For Kinh Địa Tạng content, hell realms should be framed through the Bodhisattva's compassion and vow, not through fascination with torment. The point is not to make viewers afraid of punishment; it is to awaken responsibility and compassion for beings trapped in suffering.

Preferred language includes "the sutra presents," "in traditional Buddhist cosmology," and "this imagery can also be contemplated as." Avoid "you will go there if," "this proves," or "watch to avoid."

Hell content must always include compassion as the governing emotional frame.

## 19. Pure Land Interpretation Rules

Pure Land content may include Amitābha Buddha, Kinh A Di Đà, Sukhāvatī, faith, vows, recitation, mindfulness of the Buddha, aspiration for rebirth, and hope at the threshold of death. It must be handled respectfully and without reduction.

Pure Land traditions are not merely "easy Buddhism" or passive wishful thinking. They involve faith, vow, practice, remembrance, humility, ethical orientation, and trust in Amitābha's vows according to tradition. The channel must not dismiss Pure Land devotion as superstition. It also must not promise guaranteed rebirth for viewers based on watching content or performing a simplified action.

Operational rules:

**Explain Pure Land within its devotional and doctrinal context.**

**Respect recitation and faith practices.**

**Do not guarantee the viewer's rebirth.**

**Do not turn nianfo or niệm Phật into a magical formula for worldly success.**

**Do not pit Pure Land against Thiền as inferior or superior.**

**Acknowledge that different schools interpret faith, practice, and rebirth differently.**

**Connect Pure Land aspiration to ethics, humility, remembrance, and compassion.**

Pure Land content should be especially gentle in death and grief contexts. It can offer hope, but not certainty beyond the channel's authority. It can encourage sincere practice, but not transactional thinking.

The canonical Pure Land standard is: honor faith, clarify practice, avoid guarantees.

## 20. Bodhisattva Interpretation Rules

Bodhisattvas should be represented with reverence, doctrinal care, and emotional depth. The channel may discuss Kṣitigarbha, Avalokiteśvara, Mañjuśrī, Samantabhadra, Bhaiṣajyaguru-related Bodhisattva contexts, and other figures where appropriate.

Bodhisattvas are not fictional superheroes, psychological archetypes only, or decorative sacred characters. In Mahāyāna traditions, they represent vows, compassion, wisdom, skillful means, and dedication to the liberation of beings. Some Bodhisattvas are approached devotionally, cosmologically, symbolically, and ritually. The channel may explain multiple layers but must not erase devotional meaning.

Operational rules:

**Identify the Bodhisattva's traditional role.**

**Explain the vow or virtue central to the figure.**

**Respect devotional practice.**

**Avoid spectacle and power fantasy.**

**Do not claim the channel can invoke or guarantee a Bodhisattva's intervention.**

**Do not invent new biographies or speeches.**

**Use symbolism to deepen understanding, not reduce the figure.**

For Kṣitigarbha, emphasize vow, filial piety, compassion for beings in suffering, and moral seriousness. For Avalokiteśvara, emphasize compassion and responsiveness to suffering. For Mañjuśrī, emphasize wisdom. For Samantabhadra, emphasize practice and vows. These are broad traditional associations and should be refined by sources for specific content.

Bodhisattva content should inspire compassionate action, not passive dependence on miracle rescue.

## 21. Buddha Representation Rules

The Buddha must be represented with the highest level of reverence, accuracy, and humility. The channel may discuss the historical Buddha, the Buddha of scripture, Buddhas in Mahāyāna cosmology, Amitābha Buddha, Medicine Buddha, and Buddha nature teachings when properly contextualized.

Operational rules:

**Do not speak as the Buddha.**

**Do not fabricate Buddha quotations.**

**Do not attribute modern motivational lines to the Buddha without verification.**

**Do not portray the Buddha as a modern self-help coach.**

**Do not reduce awakening to stress relief or success.**

**Do not use Buddha images as clickbait or fear packaging.**

**Distinguish historical discussion from canonical and devotional narratives.**

When discussing Siddhārtha Gautama, the channel may draw from canonical and traditional life narratives, but must be clear when details are historically uncertain or drawn from later tradition. When discussing Mahāyāna Buddhas such as Amitābha or Bhaiṣajyaguru, represent them within devotional and scriptural context rather than historical biography.

The Buddha's teaching should be connected to suffering, its causes, cessation, the path, wisdom, compassion, impermanence, non-self, dependent arising, and liberation. Avoid flattening the Buddha into a symbol of calmness alone.

The visual and narrative standard is dignity. The Buddha is never a prop, mascot, marketing hook, or dramatic device.

## 22. Monastic Representation Rules

Monastics should be represented with respect, realism, and restraint. The channel may discuss monks, nuns, novices, sangha, discipline, teachers, robes, alms, temple life, chanting, meditation, and monastic service. It must avoid romanticizing, stereotyping, or using monastics as generic symbols of holiness.

Operational rules:

**Respect monastic discipline and diversity.**

**Do not imply all monastics live identically across traditions.**

**Do not use robes as mere visual atmosphere.**

**Do not fabricate teachings by named monastics.**

**Do not use monastic imagery for sensational thumbnails or commercial pressure.**

**Do not present the channel as speaking on behalf of monastics.**

**Do not turn isolated misconduct into generalized anti-Sangha content.**

When discussing monastic practice, identify tradition where relevant. Theravāda Vinaya, Mahāyāna monastic culture, Vietnamese temple life, Zen monasteries, and Tibetan institutions may differ. Avoid universal claims about diet, celibacy, ritual, hierarchy, schedule, clothing, or practice unless supported.

The channel may encourage viewers to learn from qualified teachers and monastic communities, but must not pressure viewers into dependence or idealization. Monastics are human practitioners within traditions. Respect includes accuracy, not fantasy.

## 23. Meditation Representation Rules

Meditation must be represented as Buddhist practice connected to ethics, wisdom, compassion, attention, and liberation. It must not be reduced to relaxation, productivity, brain optimization, or emotional escape.

The channel may discuss mindfulness of breathing, awareness of body and mind, loving-kindness, compassion, contemplation of impermanence, repentance practice, Pure Land recitation as meditative remembrance, Thiền, concentration, insight, and daily-life mindfulness. It must avoid advanced or tradition-specific instruction that requires a teacher unless clearly framed as general education.

Operational rules:

**Connect meditation to ethical life.**

**Avoid promising cure or instant peace.**

**Avoid presenting meditation as suitable in the same way for everyone.**

**Avoid advanced techniques without context.**

**Avoid claims of attainment.**

**Avoid telling viewers to push through severe distress alone.**

**Encourage qualified support when meditation brings up trauma or crisis.**

Meditation content should be simple, grounded, and non-performative. The channel may describe practices and guide gentle reflection, but it should not claim that viewers will achieve realization through a video. It should also avoid spiritual competition, such as measuring depth by visions, bliss, or unusual experiences.

The canonical meditation standard is: practice supports seeing clearly, living ethically, and cultivating compassion; it is not a guaranteed mood-management tool.

## 24. Ritual Representation Rules

Ritual should be treated respectfully as embodied practice, communal memory, ethical intention, and devotional expression. It must not be dismissed as superstition or promoted as magical transaction.

The channel may discuss chanting, bowing, incense, offerings, repentance ceremonies, merit dedication, ancestor remembrance, memorial rituals, Pure Land recitation, Medicine Buddha practice, Vu Lan observances, and temple ceremonies. It should explain what these practices cultivate: gratitude, humility, mindfulness, repentance, generosity, remembrance, aspiration, compassion, and connection to community.

Operational rules:

**Explain ritual meaning before evaluating it.**

**Avoid guaranteed outcomes.**

**Avoid implying payment creates merit automatically.**

**Avoid mocking ritual forms.**

**Avoid inventing ritual instructions.**

**Respect regional variation.**

**Distinguish general education from formal liturgical guidance.**

Ritual content should not tell viewers that a specific number of chants, offerings, bows, or days will guarantee wealth, healing, protection, debt removal, spiritual purity, or rebirth. If numerical practice appears in a tradition, present it as part of that tradition and avoid universalizing it.

Ritual is meaningful when connected to intention and transformation. The channel's role is to clarify and honor, not to sell spiritual mechanics.

## 25. Devotional Language Rules

Devotional language can be used when discussing Buddhas, Bodhisattvas, Pure Land, chanting, vows, offerings, refuge, protection, blessings, and sacred aspiration. It must be sincere, tradition-aware, and non-exploitative.

Devotional language may include reverence, gratitude, aspiration, refuge, compassion, vows, light, blessing, and remembrance. It should sound grounded rather than inflated. Avoid language that performs holiness or creates emotional pressure.

Operational rules:

**Use devotional language only when appropriate to the topic.**

**Do not guarantee divine or Bodhisattva intervention.**

**Do not claim the channel transmits blessings.**

**Do not imply viewers are spiritually inferior if they doubt.**

**Do not use devotion to pressure engagement.**

**Do not turn faith into spectacle.**

**Do distinguish devotional belief from empirical claim.**

Preferred phrasing includes "many practitioners take refuge in," "this tradition expresses trust in," "devotees may chant with the aspiration that," and "the practice can cultivate." Avoid "this will make the Bodhisattva save you," "watching this brings blessings," or "only those with faith will be protected."

Devotional content should make viewers feel invited into reverence, not coerced into belief.

## 26. Miracles and Supernatural Claims

Miracles and supernatural claims must be handled with restraint and classification. Buddhist texts and traditions include miraculous births, visions, cosmic realms, protective powers, manifestations, karmic signs, dreams, healing stories, and divine beings. These may be discussed, but not exploited.

Operational rules:

**Identify the source of the claim.**

**Label it as scriptural, legendary, devotional, traditional, or testimonial where appropriate.**

**Do not present supernatural claims as empirically proven unless strong evidence exists and expert review approves.**

**Do not promise replication.**

**Do not use miracles as proof that viewers should obey the channel.**

**Do not build titles or packaging around shock.**

**Do not encourage viewers to ignore practical, medical, or safety measures.**

Miraculous stories should be interpreted through their ethical and spiritual function. Ask: what virtue does this story illuminate? What vow does it express? What fear does it transform? What practice does it encourage? What compassion does it reveal?

Personal miracle testimonies, if ever used, require extreme caution and human review. They should not be presented as general doctrine. The channel should avoid collecting miracle claims as engagement content because this can quickly become superstition, competition, and exploitation.

The permanent miracle standard is: reverence for tradition, humility about proof, no guarantees.

## 27. Medical and Mental Health Boundaries

The channel is not a medical, psychiatric, psychological, or crisis care provider. Buddhist teachings may support meaning, resilience, reflection, compassion, attention, and ethical living, but they must not be presented as treatment for medical or mental health conditions.

Operational rules:

**Do not diagnose viewers.**

**Do not tell viewers to stop medication, therapy, or medical treatment.**

**Do not claim that chanting, meditation, repentance, merit, or faith cures illness.**

**Do not claim that suffering is only mental or karmic.**

**Do not imply that medical care shows weak faith.**

**Do encourage qualified professional support where appropriate.**

**Do treat crisis disclosures seriously.**

When discussing illness, the channel may speak of compassion, patience, impermanence, community support, Medicine Buddha devotion, chanting, mindfulness, and meaning. It must clearly avoid promising cure. When discussing grief, anxiety, trauma, or depression, the channel may speak of Buddhist reflection and supportive practice, but must not present content as therapy.

For severe distress, suicidal thoughts, abuse, psychosis, addiction, or urgent medical symptoms, the correct editorial response is escalation language and qualified support, not spiritual instruction alone.

The boundary standard is: Buddhist practice can accompany care; it must not replace care.

## 28. Scientific Claims Policy

Scientific claims must be used only when accurate, relevant, and sourced. Science should clarify, not decorate. The channel must not use scientific language to make Buddhist teachings appear more credible through overstatement.

Operational rules:

**Do not say science proves karma, rebirth, enlightenment, merit, or Bodhisattva intervention.**

**Do not claim meditation rewires the brain permanently without specific evidence and context.**

**Do not overgeneralize from small or preliminary studies.**

**Do not use neuroscience metaphors as proof.**

**Do not cite studies that have not been verified.**

**Do not imply all meditation benefits all people equally.**

**Do not turn correlation into causation.**

Acceptable scientific framing may include cautious statements about mindfulness supporting attention, stress awareness, emotional regulation, or compassion practices in some contexts. Even then, avoid exaggerated certainty.

When research is mentioned, internal notes must include the study, author, publication, date, and claim being supported. If the production cannot verify those details, remove the scientific claim or frame it as ordinary observation rather than research.

The channel should never use science to flatten Buddhism into wellness technology. Buddhist practice includes ethical, contemplative, devotional, philosophical, and liberative aims that are not reducible to measurable outcomes.

The scientific standard is modesty, precision, and relevance.

## 29. What AI Must Never Say

AI systems must never produce or approve statements that violate doctrine, safety, or trust.

AI must never say:

**"The Buddha said" unless the source is verified.**

**"This chant will cure you."**

**"This ritual guarantees protection, wealth, love, or rebirth."**

**"Your suffering proves your bad karma."**

**"Your loved one is definitely in a specific realm."**

**"If you do not do this, disaster will happen."**

**"Only this channel or method can save you."**

**"Science proves karma or rebirth."**

**"Meditation replaces therapy or medicine."**

**"A good Buddhist must tolerate abuse."**

**"Filial piety requires obedience in all circumstances."**

**"A Bodhisattva will certainly intervene if you watch or chant."**

**"You are spiritually advanced because you saw signs."**

**"This secret teaching was hidden from ordinary people" unless historically verified and contextually appropriate.**

**"All Buddhist traditions teach the same thing" when differences matter.**

AI must also never invent citations, monastic endorsements, lineage claims, historical conversations, or doctrinal consensus. If pressure exists to make content more dramatic, AI must preserve the governing standards and flag the risk.

The simplest AI rule is: if a statement would exploit fear, grief, illness, guilt, devotion, or uncertainty, do not say it.

## 30. Hallucination Prevention Rules

Hallucination prevention is mandatory for AI-assisted Buddhist content. Buddhist material includes many languages, traditions, texts, and devotional layers, which makes it especially vulnerable to confident fabrication.

Operational rules:

**Use claim classification before drafting.**

**Never quote from memory.**

**Never invent scripture passages.**

**Never invent textual locations, chapter numbers, translators, or dates.**

**Never merge stories from different traditions without disclosure.**

**Never treat a modern online saying as canonical without verification.**

**Never fabricate Sanskrit, Pāli, Chinese, or Vietnamese terms.**

**Never assume a term has the same meaning across traditions.**

**Never use AI output as a source.**

Before drafting, AI must identify what sources are needed. During drafting, any unsupported detail must be marked for verification or removed. During review, all named texts, figures, dates, translations, doctrinal claims, and historical claims must be checked.

If reliable sources are unavailable, AI should use broad, cautious language or request research support. It is better to say less accurately than to say more falsely.

AI hallucination prevention should be treated as spiritual ethics, not only technical quality control. Fabricating Buddhist teaching misleads viewers and disrespects the tradition.

## 31. Dangerous Language Checklist

Dangerous language is language that can harm viewers, distort Buddhism, or damage trust even when it appears engaging.

Check every draft for:

**Fear coercion:** Does the wording make viewers afraid not to watch, chant, share, donate, or obey?

**Karmic blame:** Does it imply suffering people deserve pain?

**Ritual guarantee:** Does it promise a specific result from practice?

**Medical overreach:** Does it claim cure, treatment, or replacement of care?

**Spiritual superiority:** Does it rank viewers or traditions?

**False certainty:** Does it state uncertain history, cosmology, or interpretation as fact?

**Fabricated authority:** Does it attribute claims to scripture or teachers without verification?

**Family coercion:** Does it weaponize parents, ancestors, or filial debt?

**Graphic sensationalism:** Does it dwell on torment, death, or supernatural fear?

**Dependency language:** Does it imply the channel is necessary for safety or salvation?

Dangerous language must be revised, not softened cosmetically. Replace fear with responsibility, shame with reflection, certainty with humility, miracle promise with aspiration, and blame with causes and conditions.

If a line is powerful because it frightens the viewer, it is not aligned with the channel. If a line is powerful because it clarifies suffering and invites compassion, it may be aligned.

## 32. Sensitive Topics Guide

Sensitive topics require elevated care because viewers may be vulnerable, grieving, frightened, ashamed, or seeking certainty.

Sensitive topics include death, grief, suicide, self-harm, trauma, abuse, illness, mental health, addiction, family estrangement, filial guilt, abortion, violence, poverty, disability, disasters, war, karmic punishment, hell realms, rebirth, and ritual efficacy.

Operational rules:

**Assign high-risk status early.**

**Use compassionate framing from the beginning.**

**Avoid graphic detail unless essential and approved.**

**Avoid second-person accusation.**

**Avoid guaranteed spiritual outcomes.**

**Avoid moral simplification.**

**Include support language where appropriate.**

**Escalate for human review when harm risk exists.**

Sensitive topics should be structured around steadiness. The content should help viewers breathe, understand, and orient toward wise action. It should not intensify panic or make viewers feel spiritually trapped.

When in doubt, narrow the scope. A video does not need to solve trauma, explain all karma, or answer the mystery of death. It can responsibly illuminate one teaching and guide viewers toward compassion and support.

## 33. Death and Grief Guide

Death and grief content must be among the channel's most tender and disciplined work. Viewers may arrive immediately after loss, during illness, before a funeral, during memorial periods, or while fearing their own death.

The channel may discuss impermanence, mourning, compassion, remembrance, merit dedication, chanting, Pure Land aspiration, Kinh Địa Tạng, ancestor practice, death awareness, and care for the dying. It must not make claims about the exact state of the deceased or guarantee post-death outcomes.

Operational rules:

**Speak gently and slowly.**

**Do not exploit grief for retention.**

**Do not promise certainty about the deceased.**

**Do not frighten families with karmic threats.**

**Do not imply grief shows weak practice.**

**Do not rush viewers toward acceptance.**

**Do connect grief to love, impermanence, compassion, remembrance, and wholesome dedication.**

The channel should distinguish between Buddhist teachings on death and the human process of mourning. A viewer can understand impermanence and still grieve deeply. Buddhist practice does not require emotional numbness.

Death content should offer meaningful action without guarantee: sitting quietly, chanting sincerely, dedicating merit, caring for the living, speaking words of gratitude, and practicing compassion. It should help viewers remain human, not become spiritually frozen.

## 34. Suicide and Crisis Handling Principles

Suicide and crisis topics require the highest safety standard. The channel must not provide detailed methods, romanticize death, frame suicide as karmic destiny, or imply spiritual failure. It must not suggest that chanting or meditation alone is sufficient for acute crisis.

If content references suicide, self-harm, or acute crisis, it must:

**Encourage immediate support from trusted people and qualified professionals.**

**Encourage emergency services or crisis resources where relevant.**

**Avoid graphic detail.**

**Avoid blame.**

**Avoid karmic threats.**

**Avoid statements that increase shame.**

**Avoid implying the deceased is definitely in a specific realm.**

**Treat survivors with compassion.**

The channel may discuss Buddhist compassion for unbearable suffering, the importance of staying connected, the value of seeking help, and the possibility of taking one breath, one moment, and one act of reaching out. It must not present itself as crisis intervention.

When AI encounters a viewer disclosure of imminent self-harm in comments or community contexts, the response should be brief, compassionate, and direct: encourage immediate contact with local emergency services, a crisis hotline, or a trusted person nearby. Do not enter doctrinal debate. Do not offer karmic analysis.

Crisis content must always prioritize human safety over doctrinal exposition.

## 35. Family Conflict Guidelines

Family conflict content must balance filial piety, compassion, truthfulness, boundaries, and non-harm. It should not idealize family or treat all reconciliation as simple.

The channel may discuss anger at parents, guilt toward parents, sibling conflict, caregiving pressure, intergenerational misunderstanding, ancestor memory, apology, forgiveness, and gratitude. It must not tell viewers that Buddhist virtue requires enduring manipulation, violence, or abuse.

Operational rules:

**Recognize complexity.**

**Avoid blaming adult children automatically.**

**Avoid idealizing parents automatically.**

**Encourage wise speech and compassionate boundaries.**

**Distinguish forgiveness from unsafe access.**

**Distinguish gratitude from obedience.**

**Distinguish compassion from enabling harm.**

Family content should use the framework of causes and conditions. A family conflict may involve habit, trauma, poverty, migration, cultural expectation, personality, illness, addiction, attachment, shame, and love. Avoid one-cause explanations.

The Buddhist direction is not always reunion. Sometimes it is less hatred, clearer boundaries, safer distance, honest care, or merit dedication from afar. The channel should support moral maturity, not social pressure.

## 36. Abuse and Trauma Guidelines

Abuse and trauma require careful boundaries. The channel may discuss Buddhist compassion, non-harming, safety, boundaries, healing, and the misuse of spiritual ideas, but must not offer therapy or instruct viewers to remain in danger.

Operational rules:

**Do not tell abuse survivors to forgive quickly.**

**Do not tell viewers abuse is their karma.**

**Do not use patience or compassion to silence harm.**

**Do not imply reporting abuse is un-Buddhist.**

**Do not diagnose trauma.**

**Do not offer treatment plans.**

**Do encourage safety, trusted support, and qualified help.**

**Do affirm that non-harming includes protecting oneself and others.**

When discussing forgiveness, clarify that forgiveness does not require denial, reconciliation, unsafe contact, or absence of consequences. Buddhist compassion can coexist with boundaries and accountability.

When discussing trauma, use cautious language. The channel may say that painful experiences can shape perception and reactivity, and that mindfulness or compassion practices may support some people. It must not claim that Buddhist practice alone heals trauma.

The permanent abuse and trauma rule: no teaching may be used to keep a person in harm.

## 37. Children's Content Considerations

The channel is primarily for general and adult audiences, but some content may be seen by families or younger viewers. Any child-facing or family-friendly Buddhist content must be especially careful with fear, death, hell realms, karma, obedience, and supernatural claims.

**Avoid obedience-based filial piety.**

Children's content should not use guilt to shape behavior. It should help children recognize emotions, care for others, tell the truth, calm the body, and understand that actions have consequences in age-appropriate ways.

If a video is not designed for children, do not accidentally make its packaging child-targeted while including heavy themes. Family-viewable does not mean child-directed.

For younger viewers, the canonical Buddhist teaching mode is gentle virtue education, not cosmological fear.

## 38. Respectful Interfaith References

Operational rules:

The channel's posture should be respectful confidence. It can explain Buddhism deeply without denigrating others. It can acknowledge shared human concerns without erasing doctrinal differences.

## 39. Editorial Fact-check Workflow

Fact-checking must be embedded throughout production, not added at the end as a cosmetic step.

Workflow:

**Topic classification:** Assign low, medium, or high risk before research. High-risk topics include scripture commentary, history, karma, rebirth, hell realms, death, suicide, trauma, abuse, mental health, ritual efficacy, medical language, or tradition comparison.

**Source mapping:** Identify the source type for every major claim: canonical, commentary, scholarship, teacher interpretation, cultural practice, psychological research, or editorial reflection.

**Claim extraction:** List factual, doctrinal, historical, scientific, and sensitive claims separately.

**Verification:** Check each claim against appropriate sources. Do not use AI output as evidence.

**Language review:** Check for dangerous language, forbidden claims, overcertainty, and emotional harm.

**Tradition review:** Confirm that tradition-specific claims are not universalized.

**Revision:** Narrow, qualify, remove, or escalate unsupported claims.

**Final approval:** No high-risk content proceeds without documented review.

Fact-checking should not make content cold. It should make compassion reliable. A clear, careful video can still be cinematic and moving.

For public-facing descriptions, citations may be concise and viewer-friendly. For internal production, citations must be robust enough that a future reviewer can retrace the claim.

Citation is continuity infrastructure. It allows the channel to maintain integrity across years, teams, and AI systems.

## 41. AI Review Workflow

AI review must be structured, skeptical, and aligned with governing documents. AI can help identify risks, inconsistencies, unsupported claims, and tone drift, but it cannot be the final authority on high-risk Buddhist content.

AI review workflow:

**Step 1: Governing context check.** Confirm alignment with `PROJECT_PRD.md`, `01_BRAND_BIBLE.md`, and this guide.

**Step 2: Claim classification.** Mark claims as canonical, traditional, historical, devotional, psychological, scientific, or editorial.

**Step 3: Risk scan.** Identify karma, rebirth, hell, death, medical, mental health, abuse, trauma, family coercion, and supernatural risk.

**Step 4: Source gap scan.** Flag claims that require citations or human verification.

**Step 5: Language safety scan.** Identify fear bait, miracle bait, guilt bait, victim blaming, overcertainty, and pseudo-science.

**Step 6: Tradition scan.** Check whether Mahāyāna, Theravāda, Pure Land, Thiền, or cultural claims are being universalized improperly.

**Step 7: Recommendation.** Approve for human review, revise, narrow, or escalate.

AI must not self-certify high-risk material as final. It must identify uncertainty and preserve humility.

## 46. Translation Standards

Translation must preserve meaning, tone, and doctrinal accuracy. Buddhist translation is interpretive work, not mechanical substitution.

Operational rules:

**Do not translate sacred terms casually.**

**Check whether a term is Sanskrit, Pāli, Chinese, Vietnamese, Tibetan, Japanese, or English convention.**

**Use one primary translation per project context unless variation is needed.**

**Explain alternate translations when they affect meaning.**

**Do not build major arguments on one ambiguous translated word.**

**Respect Vietnamese diacritics and names.**

**Avoid mixing transliteration systems inconsistently.**

When translating into modern language, avoid flattening technical terms. For example, dukkha may be explained as suffering, dissatisfaction, stress, or unsatisfactoriness depending on context, but the chosen term must fit the teaching. Śūnyatā may be translated as emptiness, but must not be explained as nothingness.

If a translation choice is contested or central, escalate to translator review. If no review is available, use cautious phrasing and avoid making the translation carry too much doctrinal weight.

The translation standard is faithful clarity.
