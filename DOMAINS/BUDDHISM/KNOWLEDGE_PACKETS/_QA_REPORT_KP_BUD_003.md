## Detailed findings

### Check 1 — Tự lực/tha lực

Read Topic 2 (China/Japan/Vietnam history), Topic 3 (dedicated tự lực/tha lực section), and the Common Misconceptions list. The packet:
- traces the tự lực/tha lực pairing to Đàm Loan without asserting either pole is doctrinally superior
- explicitly separates three positions (Chinese/Vietnamese Tín-Nguyện-Hạnh integration; Japanese Hōnen's tha-lực emphasis; Japanese Shinran's more radical tha-lực) and states plainly "**the tự lực/tha lực question has no single correct answer that applies to the whole tradition**" (Topic 3, Production cautions)
- Common Misconceptions item 3 explicitly flags "Tự lực and tha lực have one correct balance across all of Pure Land Buddhism" as incorrect

One phrase was checked for accidental bias: Topic 3 Narrative detail calls Shinran's position "a position genuinely more radical than either the Chinese/Vietnamese combined model or Hōnen's own teaching." This is a factual description of degree of emphasis (matching the research draft's own framing), not a value judgment about which is doctrinally correct — it does not violate the unresolved-debate requirement.

### Check 2 — Literal vs. symbolic Cực Lạc

Read Topic 4 in full. Both readings (literal/traditional and Duy Tâm Tịnh Độ/Tự Tánh Di Đà) are presented side by side, each sourced, with explicit production cautions: "**The literal/symbolic Cực Lạc question must never be resolved on screen**" and instructions to present the traditional frame first (not skipped as if naive) and the mind-only frame as "a real, sourced, coexisting tradition — never as a 'more scientific' or 'more modern' upgrade." This matches `BUDDHIST_GUIDE.md` §9's Symbolic Interpretation Framework and the research draft's own explicit conclusion (research draft §4.2). Common Misconceptions item 2 also flags both overstated positions ("definitely just a metaphor" / "definitely a literal place") as incorrect. No winner is picked anywhere in the packet.

### Check 3 — Quotation length

Searched the entire file for every quoted string. The longest quotation drawn from sutra content is: "with only a small amount of good roots, blessings, merit, causes and conditions" (Topic 1, Primary concepts) — **13 words**, under the ~15-word ceiling, and already trimmed shorter than the fuller sentence given in the research draft ("cannot be born in that Land with only a small amount of good roots, blessings, merit, causes and conditions," ~19 words). All other quoted strings are short doctrinal terms, section titles, or reusable narration-line templates, none of which are continuous sutra text near the limit.

### Check 4 — Consistency with CB_BUD_004 and CB_BUD_005

Read both character bibles in full and checked every packet claim that touches them:

- **Three Pure Land Sutras and their roles** — packet's "principle / contemplation / recitation" framing does not contradict CB_BUD_004's Canonical Appearance list; packet explicitly labels the sequence as a teaching arrangement, not composition order, consistent with CB_BUD_004's own historical-confidence caution.
- **Vow-count variation (24/36/48)** — packet defers to CB_BUD_004 rather than restating a number, per its own Topic 5 rule ("Do not restate vow-count detail here beyond a cross-reference"). Consistent.
- **Tín-Nguyện-Hạnh framework** — packet's description (faith/aspiration/practice, sincerity + conduct + recitation "resonating" with A Di Đà's vow-power) matches CB_BUD_004's Tín-Nguyện-Hạnh section term-for-term in substance.
- **Japan/Vietnam tự lực-tha lực boundary rule** — CB_BUD_004 states "Do not import this specific Japanese doctrinal framing into general Vietnamese-context narration unless the episode is explicitly comparative and sourced." Consistent, not a re-litigation.
- **Cực Lạc interpretive humility (CB_BUD_004 Misconception 7)** — packet's Topic 4 explicitly extends this by explaining *why* humility is required (two legitimate coexisting readings), without contradicting CB_BUD_004's own wording that Cực Lạc's description "should be treated as canonical scriptural imagery with doctrinal and symbolic function." - **Đại Thế Chí's Niệm Phật Tam Muội (Kinh Lăng Nghiêm, mother-and-child simile)** — packet's Topic 3 cross-reference explicitly defers to CB_BUD_005 rather than restating the teaching, and does not introduce any conflicting detail (e.g., does not misstate the succession of Buddhas or the simile).
- **A Di Đà/Đại Thế Chí figure boundaries** (not a Buddha yet, Kinh Bi Hoa prophecy is separately sourced, must not be confused with Quán Thế Âm) — the packet never touches these claims directly; it only references the character bibles, so no drift risk was found.

No contradiction was found in dates, biographical claims, or doctrinal framing between KP_BUD_003 and either character bible.

### Check 5 — Traceability of historical/textual claims

Cross-checked every date, patriarch name, and dynasty/era reference against the research draft: Kumārajīva's dates (344–413 CE), Kucha origin, capture in 383 CE, ~17 years in Liangzhou, arrival at Chang'an in 401 CE, the 402 CE translation, and the "8,000 disciples" figure (correctly flagged as traditional/unverified in both Known Gaps and Production cautions) all trace cleanly. Huệ Viễn (334–416), Đàm Loan (476–542), Đạo Xước (562–645), Thiện Đạo (613–681), Hōnen (1133–1212), and Shinran (1173–1263) all match the research draft exactly. The Vietnam timeline (5th century, Lý Thánh Tông 1023–1072, Trần Thái Tông 1218–1277, Khóa Hư Lục/Niệm Phật Luận) matches, and the packet correctly preserves the research draft's "lowest-confidence" caution for the 5th/11th-century milestones rather than upgrading them to flat assertions. No Taishō-style catalog numbers or other unsupported specific identifiers were introduced (checked separately; none found, unlike a similar issue found and fixed in the sibling packet KP_BUD_002).
