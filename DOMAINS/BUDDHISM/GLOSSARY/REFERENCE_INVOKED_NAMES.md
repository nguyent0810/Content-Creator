# Reference: Commonly Invoked Buddha and Bodhisattva Names (Vietnamese Mahayana Practice)

## Document Role

This reference lists the Buddha and Bodhisattva names most commonly recited/invoked ("niệm danh hiệu") in Vietnamese Mahayana practice, for use whenever a script needs to explain, list, or briefly reference an invoked name - most directly for an anticipated episode on "Vì sao niệm danh hiệu Phật, Bồ Tát?" (why recite the names of Buddhas and Bodhisattvas), and as a general disambiguation aid whenever two commonly-confused figures appear near each other in a script.

This is a reference glossary, not a devotional guide and not a ranking of the figures listed. Order below follows general frequency of invocation in popular Vietnamese practice, not doctrinal hierarchy - no bodhisattva or Buddha listed here should be presented as superior to another (consistent with CB_BUD_001's existing rule against ranking bodhisattvas).

Source lineage: `DOMAINS/BUDDHISM/SOURCES/RESEARCH_DRAFT_ENTITIES.md`, Section C, cross-referenced against phatgiao.org.vn (Vietnam Buddhist Sangha portal), Vietnamese Wikipedia, and Đại Bảo Tháp Mandala Tây Thiên (Vajrayana-tradition source, labeled where used). Confidence: high for the core list of names; medium for detailed iconographic distinctions (mudras, mounts, ritual implements), which should be checked against an academic or museum source before being treated as fixed visual doctrine.

## How to Use This Reference

Each entry gives: the full invocation form as commonly said aloud, the figure's short meaning/identity, and any documented confusion risk. When a script references an invoked name, prefer the full canonical invocation form on first use, then a short form afterward. Do not invent new invocation phrases.

---

## 1. Nam Mô A Di Đà Phật

Amitabha Buddha, teacher and guiding figure of the Western Pure Land (Cực Lạc). The name is traditionally explained as meaning "Immeasurable Life, Immeasurable Light." This is the most frequently invoked name in Vietnamese Pure Land practice, associated with forty-eight vows to receive beings into that Pure Land.

Full character bible: **CB_BUD_004_A_Di_Da_Phat.md**.

**Confusion risk:** Frequently but incorrectly merged with Shakyamuni Buddha (see entry 2) by newer viewers, since both are commonly enshrined together. They are not the same figure: Shakyamuni is the historical Buddha of this world-era; Amitabha is the teacher of a separate Pure Land. Never say or imply "A Di Đà is Thích Ca in old age" or any similar collapsing of the two.

## 2. Nam Mô Bổn Sư Thích Ca Mâu Ni Phật

Shakyamuni Buddha, the historical founder of Buddhism in this world-era. "Bổn Sư" means "root/founding teacher" - the teacher from whom this tradition originates.

Full character bible: **CB_BUD_007_Thich_Ca_Mau_Ni_Phat.md**.

**Confusion risk:** See entry 1. Also should not be presented as a still-living, currently-teaching figure in the way a Pure Land Buddha's ongoing teaching activity is sometimes described; Shakyamuni's earthly teaching career is presented as historically completed.

## 3. Nam Mô Dược Sư Lưu Ly Quang Vương Phật

Medicine Buddha, teacher of the Eastern Pure Land of Lapis Lazuli Light, associated with twelve vows connected to healing suffering and stabilizing body and mind.

**Confusion risk:** Do not present invocation of this name as a medical treatment or a substitute for professional healthcare - this is a firm boundary already established elsewhere in this domain's guidance (see KP_BUD_001-adjacent healing-content boundaries) and must be respected whenever this name is used.

Full character bible: **CB_BUD_008_Duoc_Su_Phat.md** (contains the mandatory no-medical-substitution editorial rule in full).

## 4. Nam Mô Đương Lai Hạ Sanh Di Lặc Tôn Phật

Maitreya, the bodhisattva/future Buddha expected to appear in a future era. Associated with patience, generosity, and hope oriented toward the future.

**Confusion risk:** The popular "laughing fat monk" image widely seen in decorative and folk contexts originates from a separate figure, the Chinese monk Bố Đại Hòa Thượng ("Budai"), popularly believed to be a manifestation of Maitreya. This is a documented folk-devotional layer, not the canonical bodhisattva form itself - label explicitly as folk tradition if the "laughing monk" image is used or discussed.

Full character bible: **CB_BUD_009_Di_Lac.md** (contains the full canonical/folk distinction).

## 5. Nam Mô Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát

Avalokiteshvara, the bodhisattva of great compassion who "hears the cries of the world" and responds. One of the most widely invoked figures in Vietnamese Buddhist practice generally.

**Confusion risk:** See entries 6 and 7 below - this is the figure most frequently confused with two others (Đại Thế Chí, and Địa Tạng Bồ Tát).

Full character bible: **CB_BUD_006_Quan_The_Am_Bo_Tat.md**.

## 6. Nam Mô Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát

Mahasthamaprapta, who stands alongside Amitabha and Avalokiteshvara as one of the "Tây Phương Tam Thánh" (Three Sages of the West). Associated with the light of wisdom that guides beings toward the Pure Land.

**Confusion risk:** The single most commonly confused pair in this list is Quán Thế Âm and Đại Thế Chí, since both are shown flanking Amitabha in near-identical postures. Traditional distinguishing markers cited in the sources reviewed: Đại Thế Chí's crown ornament is typically a vase, while Quán Thế Âm's crown typically bears a small seated Buddha image. Treat these iconographic markers as medium confidence pending confirmation from an academic or museum source; the doctrinal distinction (Quán Thế Âm = compassion that hears and responds; Đại Thế Chí = wisdom-light that guides) is the more load-bearing and higher-confidence distinction for narration.

Full character bible: **CB_BUD_005_Dai_The_Chi_Bo_Tat.md**.

## 7. Nam Mô Địa Tạng Vương Bồ Tát

Ksitigarbha, this channel's central bodhisattva figure. Full documentation lives in **CB_BUD_001_Dia_Tang_Bo_Tat.md**; this entry exists only to place him correctly within this comparative reference list, not to duplicate his full character bible.

**Confusion risk:** See CB_BUD_001 in full. In brief: not the same as Diêm La Vương (CB_BUD_002); not a death god or judge of the dead; the honorific "Vương" must not imply worldly kingship.

## 8. Nam Mô Văn Thù Sư Lợi Bồ Tát

Manjushri, bodhisattva of great wisdom, traditionally depicted with a flaming sword (cutting through ignorance) and the Prajnaparamita (Perfection of Wisdom) scripture, riding a blue lion.

**Confusion risk:** Frequently paired and sometimes blurred with Phổ Hiền (entry 9) since the two are commonly shown flanking Shakyamuni together. Distinguish by mount (lion for Văn Thù, elephant for Phổ Hiền) and by domain (wisdom for Văn Thù, practice/conduct for Phổ Hiền).

Full character bible: **CB_BUD_010_Van_Thu_Su_Loi_Bo_Tat.md**.

## 9. Nam Mô Phổ Hiền Bồ Tát

Samantabhadra, bodhisattva of great conduct/practice, traditionally depicted riding a white six-tusked elephant, holding a lotus or jewel. Frequently paired with Văn Thù (wisdom) as two complementary expressions of the Mahayana path - practice and wisdom together, neither superior to the other.

**Confusion risk:** See entry 8.

Full character bible: **CB_BUD_011_Pho_Hien_Bo_Tat.md**.

## 10. Nam Mô Thiên Thủ Thiên Nhãn Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát

"Thousand-Handed, Thousand-Eyed" Avalokiteshvara - a specific manifested form of Quán Thế Âm (entry 5), symbolizing the capacity to see and respond to suffering everywhere at once. This is a form/manifestation, not a separate bodhisattva.

**Confusion risk:** Should never be introduced as if it were a distinct bodhisattva independent from entry 5; always frame as "a manifested form of Quán Thế Âm."

No separate character bible needed - see CB_BUD_006_Quan_The_Am_Bo_Tat.md.

## 11. Nam Mô Chuẩn Đề Vương Bồ Tát

Cundi, a bodhisattva figure most associated with Vajrayana/Mật Tông (Esoteric/Tantric) practice in Vietnamese reception, commonly invoked for protection and the reduction of karmic obstacles, often depicted with many arms (commonly described as eighteen).

**Confusion risk:** Sometimes loosely described in popular sources as "a many-armed form of Quán Thế Âm," but the sources reviewed for this document do not confirm this as a settled identification across all traditions; present Cundi as a distinct figure within the Esoteric/Tantric devotional stream rather than asserting a confirmed identity with Quán Thế Âm. Label Vajrayana/Esoteric-tradition sourcing explicitly when discussing this figure, consistent with this domain's general rule to attribute school-specific claims to their school.

No character bible built yet (lowest priority of the 11 entries - more tangential to this channel's current Mahayana-devotional/Kinh Địa Tạng focus). Build one before any episode centers on this figure specifically.

## Cross-Cutting Notes for Scripting

- **"Nam Mô" (Namo)** itself is a term of reverence/taking-refuge, roughly "I pay homage to" / "I take refuge in" - it is not part of the figure's name and can be explained once, early, rather than re-glossed at every use.
- **Regional reception varies.** Quán Thế Âm is very commonly depicted and worshipped in a feminine form ("Phật Bà Nam Hải") in Vietnamese and Chinese popular reception, while earlier Indian and Tibetan sources present a more gender-neutral or masculine-leaning form. Present this as a difference in regional reception across history, not a claim that one form is the single "correct" one.
- **Do not rank these figures.** Every entry above represents compassion or wisdom expressed through a different symbolic emphasis, consistent with CB_BUD_001's existing rule against creating sectarian superiority or ranking bodhisattvas.
- **Confidence tiering for iconographic detail.** The core identity, meaning, and confusion-risk notes above are high confidence, cross-checked across multiple mainstream Vietnamese Buddhist sources. Fine iconographic detail (exact mudras, exact crown ornaments, exact arm counts) is medium confidence and should be verified against an academic or museum-quality source before being treated as fixed visual doctrine for a character bible or shot plan.
