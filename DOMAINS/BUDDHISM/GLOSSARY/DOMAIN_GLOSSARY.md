# Buddhism Domain Glossary

Status: active, expanded 2026-07-17. This glossary defines the terms `BUDDHIST_GUIDE.md` §45 ("Glossary Standards") and §47 ("Vietnamese Terminology Standards") require to be under glossary control. Each entry gives the term, its origin/register, a working definition, and the editorial rule that governs how the term may be used in a script — pulled directly from the numbered rule section in `BUDDHIST_GUIDE.md` so a script writer does not have to re-derive it. This file does not replace `BUDDHIST_GUIDE.md`, `KP_BUD_001_Kinh_Dia_Tang.md`, or `CONTINUITY_REGISTRY.md` — it is the fast-lookup layer; the full doctrinal reasoning lives in those documents.

Every entry marked **[Character/Entity]** has a full Character Bible under `CHARACTER_BIBLES/` — this glossary gives only a one-line pointer for those, not the full profile.

## A. Core Doctrinal Concepts

**Nghiệp (karma; Sanskrit: karman)** — Intentional action and moral causality shaped by causes and conditions. Not fate, punishment, revenge, or a tool for predicting destiny. *Rule (BUDDHIST_GUIDE §15):* never imply a person deserves suffering because of past karma; never use karma to blame victims; always connect it to intention, habit, speech, and multiple conditions, not a single cause. Forbidden: "this happened because you deserved it," "your illness proves past-life karma."

**Nhân quả (cause and effect)** — The broader causal framework karma operates within; often paired with nghiệp in Vietnamese usage ("nhân quả nghiệp báo"). Same editorial rule as Nghiệp above (§15).

**Nghiệp duyên (karmic connection/bond)** — Why particular beings meet, owe, and part; a relational subset of nghiệp, not a synonym for it. *Rule (established in production, see EP005):* must not be used to romanticize or justify staying in a harmful relationship; not deterministic ("số phận đã viết sẵn"); does not license claiming to know which specific past-life debt explains a specific relationship. See `CONTINUITY_REGISTRY.md` for the full EP005 precedent.

**Vô thường (impermanence)** — All conditioned things arise and pass; nothing fixed or permanent. Used to soften attachment and grief, not to minimize real loss. Pair with compassion (§13) when used in grief contexts — impermanence teaching must not read as "your loss doesn't matter."

**Vô ngã (non-self; Sanskrit: anātman)** — The teaching that there is no fixed, independent, permanent self. A philosophically advanced concept — do not flatten into pop-psychology "ego death" language; distinguish from vô thường (impermanence applies to phenomena generally; vô ngã specifically to the absence of a fixed self).

**Duyên khởi (dependent origination; Sanskrit: pratītyasamutpāda)** — Phenomena arise in dependence on multiple causes and conditions, never from a single cause. This is the doctrinal backbone that keeps karma explanations from collapsing into simplistic single-cause blame (§15) — invoke it explicitly whenever a script risks implying "X happened because of exactly one past action."

**Luân hồi (samsara; the cycle of rebirth)** — The cycle of birth, death, and rebirth across the six realms, driven by karma and craving. See Tái sinh below for the specific rebirth-language rule.

**Tái sinh (rebirth)** — A being's continuation into a new existence after death, within luân hồi. *Rule (BUDDHIST_GUIDE §17):* never tell a viewer where they or a loved one will be reborn; never claim to identify someone's past-life karma; never use rebirth as a threat; acknowledge viewers may hold faith, doubt, or uncertainty about it without being forced toward one position.

**Niết Bàn (Nirvana)** — The cessation of suffering and the cycle of rebirth; liberation from craving, aversion, and delusion. Do not present as "eternal happy afterlife" in the theistic-heaven sense — it is the extinguishing of the causes of suffering, not a location.

**Giải thoát (liberation)** — Freedom from the causes of suffering (craving, aversion, delusion) and from the compulsion of samsara. The Buddha's teaching (§21) should connect to giải thoát as an ongoing aspiration, not a single dramatic event a viewer completes by watching a video.

**Tánh Không (emptiness; Sanskrit: śūnyatā)** — The teaching that phenomena lack fixed, independent existence; central to Mahāyāna philosophy (Bát Nhã / Prajñāpāramitā literature, Văn Thù's domain). Advanced doctrinal territory — do not introduce casually in beginner-level content without the Symbolic/Modern Reflection framing `BUDDHIST_GUIDE.md` §9-10 require.

## B. Practice & Ritual Terms

**Hồi hướng (dedication of merit)** — The act of directing the wholesome benefit of an action toward the welfare of others (the deceased, family, all beings). *Rule (BUDDHIST_GUIDE §16):* explain as an act of compassion and aspiration, never as a transaction or a mechanism that guarantees a specific outcome for the dead, sick, or poor. Acceptable: "many Buddhist traditions encourage dedicating merit with compassion and sincere intention." Forbidden: "do this and your loved one will definitely be saved."

**Công đức (merit)** — Wholesome spiritual benefit associated with ethical action, generosity, devotion, practice, study, repentance, and compassion. *Rule (§16):* not spiritual currency; do not rank viewers by merit; do not imply watching/sharing a video creates merit.

**Phước đức (blessedness/merit-from-goodness)** — Closely related to công đức and often used interchangeably in popular speech; traditional distinction (not fully standardized across schools) leans toward công đức being merit tied to spiritual practice/liberation-oriented action, while phước đức leans toward worldly blessing/good fortune from virtuous conduct. Treat this distinction as a nuance to explain, not a hard rule to enforce rigidly — cross-check against KP_BUD_001's Chapter 10 material (giving/merit comparison) before drawing a sharp line in a script.

**Sám hối (repentance)** — Acknowledging wrongdoing, feeling genuine remorse, and committing to not repeat it. Connected to karma's "responsibility without cruelty" standard (§15) — repentance content should lead to repair and renewed practice, never to prolonged self-punishment or shame (§13's "do not use shame as a shortcut to moral seriousness").

**Quy y (taking refuge)** — Formally committing to the Tam Bảo (Triple Gem) as one's spiritual foundation. A specific devotional/practice act — do not use loosely as a synonym for "believing in Buddhism" generally.

**Giới (precepts)** — Ethical training rules a practitioner voluntarily undertakes (e.g., the five lay precepts). Present as a voluntary ethical commitment, not an external legal code imposed on the viewer.

**Nguyện (vow)** — A committed spiritual aspiration, most centrally Địa Tạng's vow but relevant to any Bodhisattva. *Rule (§20):* do not invent new vow wording not attested in the sutra/tradition; the traditional 16-character vow formulation must always be presented as "a traditional formulation long associated with," never as a verified verbatim scripture quotation (per `CONTINUITY_REGISTRY.md` standardized terminology table).

**Chánh niệm (mindfulness)** — Sustained, clear awareness of present experience. Popular in modern secular contexts — when used in this channel's Buddhist context, keep it connected to ethical and liberative purpose, not reduced to a pure productivity/wellness technique (a risk this channel's `PROJECT_PRD.md` explicitly names: "resist the pressure to reduce Buddhism to productivity advice, wellness hacks").

**Thiền (Zen / meditation)** — Both a specific Mahāyāna school/tradition (Zen/Chan/Thiền tông) and, more generally, meditative practice. Disambiguate which sense is meant on first use in a script; do not pit Thiền against Tịnh Độ as superior/inferior (§19).

**Tịnh Độ (Pure Land)** — The devotional/practice tradition centered on Amitābha Buddha (A Di Đà) and aspiration for rebirth in Sukhāvatī (Cực Lạc). *Rule (§19):* honor faith, clarify practice, avoid guarantees — do not dismiss as "easy Buddhism" or passive wishful thinking; do not promise a viewer's rebirth there.

## C. Ethics & Family

**Hiếu đạo (filial piety)** — A major channel theme; gratitude, interdependence, responsibility, and remembrance toward parents — not coercive obedience or guilt. *Rule (§14):* honor parents without idealizing every parent; do not tell viewers to endure abuse in the name of filial virtue; do not weaponize "parental debt"; recognize some families are not safe, and support boundaries where needed alongside gratitude where warranted.

**Vu Lan (Ullambana)** — The filial-piety/ancestor-remembrance festival distinct from Kinh Địa Tạng's own narrative content, though thematically adjacent (both connect to a mother's suffering and a child's vow to help). Do not conflate the Ullambana narrative (Mục Kiền Liên / Maudgalyāyana saving his mother) with any Kinh Địa Tạng past-life story — this exact conflation produced the "Mục Kiền Liên Thánh" naming error already corrected in `CONTINUITY_REGISTRY.md` (Error 1).

## D. Roles & Titles

**Bồ Tát (Bodhisattva)** — A being who has vowed to attain enlightenment for the sake of all beings; not a fictional superhero, a psychological archetype only, or a decorative sacred character. *Rule (§20):* identify the traditional role and central vow/virtue of the specific Bodhisattva named; do not invent new biography or speech for any Bodhisattva; do not claim the channel can invoke or guarantee a Bodhisattva's intervention.

**A-la-hán (Arhat)** — A being who has attained liberation by following the Buddha's teaching; a distinct attainment-category from a Bodhisattva (who delays/redirects toward helping others attain liberation too, in Mahāyāna framing). Do not use interchangeably with Bồ Tát.

**Đức Phật (the Buddha, general title)** — See **[Character/Entity]** full profile: `CB_BUD_007_Thich_Ca_Mau_Ni_Phat.md` for Thích Ca Mâu Ni specifically. *Rule (§21):* never speak as the Buddha or fabricate Buddha quotations; distinguish historical discussion (Siddhārtha Gautama) from devotional/cosmological Buddhas (Amitābha, Bhaiṣajyaguru) — the latter are represented within scriptural/devotional context, not historical biography. The Buddha is never a prop, mascot, or marketing hook.

**Tam Bảo (Triple Gem)** — Phật (Buddha), Pháp (Dharma/teaching), Tăng (Sangha/community) — the three things a practitioner takes refuge in (see Quy y above).

## E. Core Values

**Từ bi (compassion)** — The governing emotional frame for the entire channel, per §13's Compassion-first Principle: do not blame suffering people, do not intensify fear for attention, do not use shame as a moral shortcut, do not promise rescue through a ritual/video/chant/donation/subscription.

**Trí tuệ (wisdom)** — Direct insight into the nature of reality (impermanence, non-self, dependent origination), traditionally paired with từ bi — wisdom without compassion risks cold abstraction; compassion without wisdom risks sentimentality (see `PROJECT_PRD.md` Core Philosophy).

## F. Sutra Titles

**Kinh Địa Tạng Bồ Tát Bổn Nguyện** — This series' primary and only currently-researched sutra (13 chapters, see `KP_BUD_001_Kinh_Dia_Tang.md`). Short form "Kinh Địa Tạng" accepted after first full-title reference in an episode.

**Kinh Pháp Hoa, Kinh Kim Cang, Kinh A Di Đà, Kinh Dược Sư** — Named in `PROJECT_PRD.md`'s long-term vision and `BUDDHIST_GUIDE.md` §47 as future expansion sutras. **Not yet researched at KP depth as of this glossary's last update** — do not draw specific chapter-level or narrative claims about these sutras into a Kinh Địa Tạng-series script; a character connected to one of these sutras (e.g., A Di Đà → Kinh A Di Đà) may be discussed at the Character Bible level (see CB_BUD_004 etc.) without implying deep sutra-specific research has been done.
