## Document Role

This Character Bible is the source of truth for **Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát** wherever this figure is referenced across the channel - primarily as the third member of the Tây Phương Tam Thánh (Three Sages of the West) alongside A Di Đà Phật (see CB_BUD_004) and Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát, and in any future content drawing on his own teaching in Kinh Lăng Nghiêm (the Surangama Sutra). It exists in large part as a **disambiguation document**: Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát is, by a wide margin, the figure most commonly confused with Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát in Vietnamese popular reception, because both are shown flanking A Di Đà Phật in near-identical postures. This document exists to prevent that error, and the secondary error of collapsing his "great power/great strength" name into unrelated "great vow" language associated with Địa Tạng Bồ Tát, before either reaches a script.

This document is not a script, not a devotional recitation guide, and not an invitation to build an episode centered on Đại Thế Chí as a standalone protagonist without a dedicated, source-reviewed episode plan. Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát is a significant bodhisattva of wisdom and diligence, but within this channel's practical scope he functions primarily as a supporting figure in the Tây Phương Tam Thánh grouping and as a comparison/disambiguation figure.

# Character Identity

## Canonical Name

**Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát**

This is the canonical Vietnamese working name for channel use. It should be used in public-facing Vietnamese content unless an episode is specifically explaining Sanskrit derivation, Chinese transliteration, or the Surangama Sutra source.

## Alternative Names

- Đại Thế Chí (short form)
- Đắc Đại Thế Bồ Tát ("the bodhisattva who has attained great strength/power," an alternate rendering found in some translation traditions)
- Đại Thế Chí Pháp Vương Tử ("Dharma Prince Mahāsthāmaprāpta," an honorific form used in some Surangama Sutra translations, paralleling how Văn Thù is sometimes styled "Pháp Vương Tử")
- Mahāsthāmaprāpta
- Mahasthamaprapta Bodhisattva
- Seishi Bosatsu (Japanese reception)

Use alternative names only when clarity requires it. Default to **Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát** in narration.

## Chinese

**大勢至菩薩**

Common romanization: **Dàshìzhì Púsà**.

Confidence note: high confidence for the received Chinese canonical identity and for the name's consistent appearance across Pure Land sutra translations and the Surangama Sutra.

## Sanskrit

**Mahāsthāmaprāpta**, commonly parsed as "the one who has attained (prāpta) great (mahā) power/strength (sthāma)" - "Arrival of Great Strength" or "Attainment of Great Power." Vietnamese sources consistently gloss this as referring to the completed power of wisdom, merit, and spiritual diligence, not physical or military force.

Confidence note: high confidence for the Sanskrit name as the standard form behind the Chinese and Vietnamese renderings; the specific semantic emphasis (wisdom-power versus generic "strength") should be presented as the doctrinally load-bearing meaning, consistent with how this bodhisattva is described in Vietnamese commentary sources.

## Pali

No standard Pali Canon equivalent should be asserted for Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát. Editorial rule: do not present this figure as canonical across all Buddhist traditions equally; he belongs to the Mahayana Pure Land and Surangama textual streams.

## Vietnamese

Canonical form:

**Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát**

Common devotional invocation form:

**Nam Mô Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát**

Editorial note: "Đại Thế Chí" translates the name's "great power/strength attained" meaning; Vietnamese devotional sources consistently interpret this power as the power of wisdom-light and diligent cultivation, not worldly or physical power, and this document requires that framing be preserved whenever the name is explained.

## English

Preferred working form:

**Mahasthamaprapta Bodhisattva**

Acceptable educational renderings:

- Bodhisattva of Great Power/Great Strength (with a gloss clarifying this means spiritual, not physical, power)
- Bodhisattva of Wisdom-Light and Diligence

Avoid:

- "The muscle of the Pure Land" or any framing implying physical/martial power;
- presenting him as interchangeable with Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát in English-language material;
- "Buddha Đại Thế Chí" (he is a bodhisattva in this channel's primary devotional framing, not currently a Buddha; see Future Buddhahood section below for the specific, separately sourced prophecy claim).

## Honorific Titles

Approved honorific descriptions:

- attendant bodhisattva of A Di Đà Phật, representing wisdom-light within the Tây Phương Tam Thánh;
- bodhisattva of diligence and the light of wisdom;
- teacher of the Niệm Phật Tam Muội (Buddha-recollection samadhi) method described in his own chapter of Kinh Lăng Nghiêm.

## Common Nicknames

- Đại Thế Chí (widely used short form). Formal narration should default back to the canonical full name **Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát**.

## Common Misidentifications

Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát must not be misidentified as:

- **Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát.** This is the single most common and most damaging error for this figure, since both bodhisattvas are shown flanking A Di Đà Phật in the Tây Phương Tam Thánh in visually similar standing postures with jeweled crowns and flowing robes. They are two distinct bodhisattvas representing two distinct qualities: Quán Thế Âm represents compassion that hears and responds to suffering (associated with a willow branch and vase of pure water, and a small seated Buddha image on the crown as a traditional distinguishing marker); Đại Thế Chí represents the light of wisdom and diligent cultivation that guides beings toward the Pure Land (associated with a lotus flower, and a precious vase said to hold wisdom-light on the crown as a traditional distinguishing marker). Per `REFERENCE_INVOKED_NAMES.md` entry 6, these iconographic crown markers should be treated as medium-confidence pending confirmation from an academic or museum source; the doctrinal distinction (compassion versus wisdom-light) is the higher-confidence, more load-bearing distinction for narration.
- **Địa Tạng Bồ Tát.** There is no scriptural relationship of equivalence between the two; any surface-level echo between "Đại Thế Chí" (great power attained) and Địa Tạng's "great vow" language is coincidental in English paraphrase and must not be used to imply a shared identity or role.
- **A Buddha.** Within the Pure Land sutras and the Tây Phương Tam Thánh framing central to this channel, Đại Thế Chí is a bodhisattva, not a currently-attained Buddha. A separate, specifically sourced prophecy tradition (see Historical & Canonical Status below) describes a future Buddhahood; this must always be labeled as a distinct, later-stage prophecy claim, not his current status.
- **A generic "wisdom bodhisattva" identical to Văn Thù Sư Lợi Bồ Tát.** Văn Thù represents wisdom in the context of cutting through ignorance (traditionally shown with a flaming sword and riding a lion); Đại Thế Chí represents wisdom-light specifically in the context of guiding beings toward the Pure Land and empowering diligent practice. Do not merge the two wisdom figures.
- **A martial or physically powerful protector figure.** "Great power/strength" in his name refers to spiritual and wisdom power, not combat capability; do not depict him with weapons or in guardian-warrior iconography.

# Historical & Canonical Status

## Historical Confidence

- **High:** Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát/Mahāsthāmaprāpta is a well-attested Mahayana bodhisattva figure, consistently named across the Pure Land sutras, the Surangama Sutra, and the Lotus Sutra's assembly listings.
- **High:** his role as the third figure of the Tây Phương Tam Thánh, standing at A Di Đà Phật's side opposite Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát, is consistently attested in Vietnamese and Chinese Pure Land devotional tradition and in Kinh Quán Vô Lượng Thọ's description of the trio's forms.
- **High:** his own account of attaining Niệm Phật Tam Muội (Buddha-recollection samadhi), presented in the chapter of Kinh Lăng Nghiêm known in Vietnamese as **Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát Niệm Phật Viên Thông Chương**, is a consistently attested and frequently studied passage within Vietnamese Pure Land teaching.
- **Medium:** the specific iconographic detail that his crown bears a precious vase containing wisdom-light (as distinguished from Quán Thế Âm's crown bearing a small seated Buddha image), along with more elaborate descriptions of "500 jeweled flowers" on the crown found in some devotional sources, should be treated as traditional iconographic description requiring confirmation from an academic or museum source before being presented as fixed visual doctrine.
- **Low unless sourced and explicitly labeled:** the specific prophecy naming his eventual future Buddhahood (see below), which derives from a different textual stream than the core Pure Land sutras and must be labeled as such whenever used.

## Canonical Appearance

Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát's primary canonical basis for this channel spans two source families:

- **The Pure Land sutras**, especially Kinh Quán Vô Lượng Thọ, which describes his form as one of the two great bodhisattvas attending A Di Đà Phật, and Kinh A Di Đà, which lists him among the assembly of great bodhisattvas.
- **Kinh Lăng Nghiêm (the Surangama Sutra)**, specifically the chapter presenting his own first-person account of attaining Niệm Phật Tam Muội - one of twenty-five methods of "perfect penetration" (viên thông) described in that sutra by different enlightened figures.

He is also listed among the assembly of great bodhisattvas at the opening of Kinh Pháp Hoa (the Lotus Sutra). Do not import doctrine specific to the Lotus Sutra's own teaching content onto Đại Thế Chí without explicit sourcing; his presence there is an assembly listing, not a teaching role.

## The Niệm Phật Viên Thông Chương (Buddha-Recollection Perfect Penetration Chapter)

In this chapter of Kinh Lăng Nghiêm, Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát recounts that, in a past age, a Buddha named **Vô Lượng Quang** (Infinite Light) appeared, followed in succession by eleven further Tathāgatas, the last of whom was named **Siêu Nhật Nguyệt Quang Như Lai** ("Tathāgata of Light Surpassing Sun and Moon"). That final Buddha in the succession taught him the method of **Niệm Phật Tam Muội** (Buddha-recollection samadhi) - continuous, unbroken mindfulness of the Buddha, described through the image of a mother and child who remember each other so constantly that they are never truly separated across lifetimes, "like a form and its shadow." Editorial rule: any retelling of this simile must be a paraphrase in the channel's own words, not a reproduction of any single translator's exact wording, and must cite the source chapter.

Through sustained practice of this method across many lifetimes, Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát describes having attained a state described as "beyond mental effort" and states that he now, in this world, gathers beings who recollect the Buddha and draws them toward the Pure Land - the doctrinal basis for his devotional role as a guide who empowers recitation practice.

Editorial rule: this chapter is a first-person teaching account within Kinh Lăng Nghiêm's frame of twenty-five perfect-penetration testimonies, not an independent free-standing sutra; always cite it as "the Đại Thế Chí chapter of Kinh Lăng Nghiêm."

## Legend vs Canon

- **Canonical scripture:** his appearance and described form in Kinh Quán Vô Lượng Thọ and Kinh A Di Đà; his first-person teaching account in the Niệm Phật Viên Thông chapter of Kinh Lăng Nghiêm; his assembly listing in Kinh Pháp Hoa.
- **Traditional devotion:** Tây Phương Tam Thánh altar imagery, associated recitation practice, temple iconography.
- **Legend/prophecy tradition:** a distinct narrative describing Đại Thế Chí's origin alongside Quán Thế Âm as two sons/attendants connected to a king figure in a prior age, and a prophecy naming his eventual future Buddhahood after Quán Thế Âm's own eventual Buddhahood, most associated with the **Kinh Bi Hoa (Karuṇāpuṇḍarīka Sūtra)** textual tradition. This is a separate, specifically sourced narrative stream from the core Pure Land sutras and must always be labeled explicitly as such - "according to the Kinh Bi Hoa tradition..." - rather than presented as part of the core Tây Phương Tam Thánh material.
- **Modern interpretation:** wisdom-power and diligence read as psychological or ethical reflection - permissible as clearly labeled modern reflection, not literal doctrine.

## Future Buddhahood (Source-Labeled)

According to the prophecy narrative associated with the Kinh Bi Hoa tradition, Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát is foretold to succeed Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát as the teaching Buddha of the Pure Land realm after Quán Thế Âm's own eventual attainment of Buddhahood there, with the future Buddha-title most commonly rendered in Vietnamese sources as **Thiện Trụ Trân Bảo Sơn Vương Như Lai**. Editorial rule: this must always be presented with its specific source label (Kinh Bi Hoa tradition) and framed as a distant-future prophecy narrative, never as his current status, and never as a claim that displaces A Di Đà Phật's present teaching role in Cực Lạc.

## Tradition Differences

### Chinese and Vietnamese Traditions

Đại Thế Chí is consistently presented as the wisdom-power counterpart to Quán Thế Âm's compassion within the Tây Phương Tam Thánh, and as the teacher-source (via his own account) of the Niệm Phật Tam Muội method central to Pure Land recitation practice.

### Japanese Traditions

Seishi Bosatsu is recognized in Japanese Buddhist reception, sometimes with distinct regional devotional emphases (including associations with specific calendar-year protective figures in some folk-Buddhist frameworks). Do not import Japanese-specific associations into Vietnamese content without an explicit comparative label.

### Theravada Contexts

Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát and his source sutras are not part of the Pali Canon. Avoid claiming pan-Buddhist canonical status.

## Scholarly Notes

Scholarly humility is mandatory. The channel should not overstate:

- the precise historical dating or textual origin of the Surangama Sutra's Đại Thế Chí chapter, whose canonical status and translation history carry more scholarly debate than the core Pure Land sutras and should be handled with appropriate caution if this becomes relevant;
- fine iconographic detail (exact crown ornamentation, exact flower/pedestal counts) as fixed visual doctrine without museum or academic confirmation;
- the Kinh Bi Hoa prophecy material as equivalent in devotional weight to the core Tây Phương Tam Thánh material.

# Role in the Tây Phương Tam Thánh

## Narrative Function

Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát's primary narrative function across the sources reviewed for this channel is as the **wisdom-power attendant** of A Di Đà Phật, standing opposite Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát's compassion-attendant role. Where Quán Thế Âm is described as "hearing the cries of the world" and responding with compassion, Đại Thế Chí is described as extending the light of wisdom that illuminates the ten directions and empowers beings' own diligent practice, helping them free themselves from difficult states through spiritual strength rather than direct rescue.

This positions him, within this channel's framing, as a **supporting bodhisattva who empowers practice**, not a central protagonist independent of A Di Đà Phật's teaching role, and not a combat or rescue figure in any physical sense.

## Relationship to A Di Đà Phật

Đại Thế Chí stands at A Di Đà Phật's side (conventionally to the Buddha's right, opposite Quán Thế Âm to the left, though regional artistic convention can vary) as one of the two great attendant bodhisattvas of the Tây Phương Tam Thánh. This relationship is one of **devoted attendance and complementary function**, not equivalence with A Di Đà Phật's status as a fully attained Buddha. See CB_BUD_004 for the full documentation of A Di Đà Phật.

## Relationship to Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát

The two bodhisattvas are presented as a complementary pair - compassion (Quán Thế Âm) and wisdom-power/diligence (Đại Thế Chí) - flanking A Di Đà Phật together. This channel must never rank one as superior to the other; each expresses a different, equally necessary quality of the bodhisattva path, consistent with the existing rule against ranking bodhisattvas established in CB_BUD_001 and `REFERENCE_INVOKED_NAMES.md`.

## The Niệm Phật Tam Muội Teaching

Đại Thế Chí's most doctrinally distinctive contribution, drawn from his own account in Kinh Lăng Nghiêm, is the teaching that sustained, sincere mindfulness of the Buddha - likened to the mutual remembrance between a mother and child - naturally draws the practitioner toward realization and toward the Pure Land, without requiring elaborate technique. Editorial rule: this teaching must be presented as describing sustained, sincere practice over time, never as an instant or effortless shortcut, consistent with the Tín-Nguyện-Hạnh framework already established in CB_BUD_004.

# Character Psychology (Narrative Role, Not Invented Personality)

Because Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát is a scriptural figure known primarily through a first-person teaching account and iconographic tradition rather than an extended narrative biography, do not invent an elaborate personal psychology, dramatic backstory, or inner conflict for him. What the sources support:

- **Posture:** dignified, radiant, standing in devoted attendance beside A Di Đà Phật.
- **Motivation (as shown):** to extend the light of wisdom and to draw beings toward diligent, sustained recollection of the Buddha, having himself attained realization through exactly this practice across countless past lives.
- **Tone:** calm, steady, and instructive when presenting his own teaching account; otherwise largely non-verbal within the core Pure Land sutras' visual/devotional framing.

Do not write fictional dialogue for Đại Thế Chí beyond a careful paraphrase of his documented teaching account. Do not give him a villain arc, a rivalry with Quán Thế Âm, or an invented redemption arc - none of this is supported by the source material.

## Core Values

- diligence (tinh tấn);
- wisdom-light freely extended;
- steady, sustained practice over dramatic gesture;
- complementary partnership rather than competition (with Quán Thế Âm);
- humble attendance rather than independent authority.

## Compassion/Teaching Style

Đại Thế Chí's teaching style, as narratively supported, is to empower rather than to directly intervene - extending wisdom-light that strengthens beings' own capacity for sustained practice, rather than acting as a direct rescuer. This is a different register from Quán Thế Âm's responsive, hearing-and-answering compassion, and from Địa Tạng's descending, non-abandoning compassion; none should be presented as superior.

# Symbols and Visual Identity

## Appearance

Traditional East Asian and Vietnamese depictions show Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát as a bodhisattva figure - jeweled crown, flowing robes, standing (most commonly, in the Tây Phương Tam Thánh composition) on a lotus pedestal at A Di Đà Phật's side, in a dignified, calm posture closely paralleling Quán Thế Âm's posture on the opposite side.

Channel rule: use respectful East Asian/Vietnamese visual references consistent with Tây Phương Tam Thánh iconography. Avoid random fantasy-deity imagery, and avoid depicting him in isolation from this grouping unless the specific scene (e.g., illustrating his own account in Kinh Lăng Nghiêm) calls for it.

## Crown and Distinguishing Marker

Traditional Vietnamese and Chinese devotional sources describe Đại Thế Chí's crown as bearing a **precious vase (báu bình)** said to hold the light of wisdom - the traditional visual marker distinguishing him from Quán Thế Âm, whose crown traditionally bears a small seated Buddha image.

Confidence note: treat this crown-marker distinction as medium confidence, consistent with `REFERENCE_INVOKED_NAMES.md` entry 6, pending confirmation from an academic or museum source. Some devotional sources add further elaborate description (a crown of five hundred jeweled flowers, each displaying a pure land) - present this only as traditional devotional description, not as settled iconographic doctrine, if used at all.

## Lotus Flower

Đại Thế Chí is commonly depicted holding a **green or blue lotus flower**, distinguished from Quán Thế Âm's willow branch and vase of pure water. The lotus is a general Buddhist symbol of purity arising from difficult conditions; in this context it is specifically associated with wisdom and the removal of afflictive, defiling thoughts.

## Light

Light is Đại Thế Chí's central symbolic association - the "wisdom-light" said to illuminate the ten directions and empower diligent practice, distinct from A Di Đà's broader "Infinite Light" (Vô Lượng Quang) as a defining Buddha-quality. Đại Thế Chí's light should be framed specifically as empowering practice and dispelling confusion, not as a generic glow effect.

Forbidden light meanings:

- laser-beam or superhero power-glow effects;
- light as literal wealth, luck, or protection-charm imagery;
- light framed as combat power.

## Clothing

Depictions typically show jeweled bodhisattva ornamentation (crown, necklace, flowing robes) rather than the simpler monastic robes used for A Di Đà Phật's Buddha-form, paralleling Quán Thế Âm's bodhisattva presentation. Default visual palette should follow the series' restrained palette: warm gold, muted temple red, soft white, and gentle blue-green (for the lotus).

Avoid armor, battle clothing, or elaborate fantasy-warrior ornamentation.

## Objects

Approved objects:

- green/blue lotus flower;
- jeweled crown with vase motif (medium-confidence detail, may be simplified in production art);
- lotus pedestal;
- position beside A Di Đà Phật and opposite Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát in Tây Phương Tam Thánh compositions.

Avoid inventing objects (weapons, scales, mirrors) not supported by the sutras or documented iconography for this figure.

## Composition

Preferred compositions:

- the Tây Phương Tam Thánh trio, with correct relative positioning (A Di Đà Phật centered, Quán Thế Âm and Đại Thế Chí flanking);
- centered, stable, reverent framing when shown individually (e.g., illustrating his Kinh Lăng Nghiêm teaching account);
- low visual noise, negative space for calm.

## Forbidden Depictions

- Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát depicted identically to, or unlabeled and indistinguishable from, Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát in any composition meant to show them as distinct figures;
- Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát in combat, armor, or action-hero poses invoking "great power" as physical/martial strength;
- Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát presented as a currently-attained Buddha without the future-Buddhahood prophecy being explicitly source-labeled as a distinct, separate tradition;
- sexualized, comedic, or disrespectful depictions;
- AI-generated distorted sacred face or body;
- using the sacred image as mere background decoration disconnected from the episode's actual content.

## Facial Expression

Facial expression should be:

- calm;
- dignified;
- attentive;
- radiant without being theatrical;
- neither smiling broadly nor stern.

## Lighting

Lighting should use warm, restrained radiance consistent with the series' Tây Phương Tam Thánh palette (see CB_BUD_004) - soft golden-white wisdom-light rather than harsh or garish effects.

# Common Misconceptions

## Misconception 1: Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát is the same figure as Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát.

Correct understanding: they are two distinct bodhisattvas representing two distinct qualities - compassion (Quán Thế Âm) and wisdom-power/diligence (Đại Thế Chí) - who together flank A Di Đà Phật in the Tây Phương Tam Thánh.

## Misconception 2: "Great power/strength" in his name refers to physical or martial power.

Correct understanding: Vietnamese devotional sources consistently interpret this as spiritual power - the completed power of wisdom, merit, and diligent cultivation - not combat capability.

## Misconception 3: Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát is currently a Buddha.

Correct understanding: within the core Tây Phương Tam Thánh framing, he is a bodhisattva; a separate prophecy tradition (Kinh Bi Hoa) describes a distant future Buddhahood and must always be labeled as such.

## Misconception 4: Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát is the same as Văn Thù Sư Lợi Bồ Tát because both represent "wisdom."

Correct understanding: they are distinct wisdom-associated bodhisattvas with distinct iconography, sources, and symbolic emphasis; do not merge them.

## Misconception 5: The crown-vase versus crown-Buddha-image distinction from Quán Thế Âm is settled, precise iconographic doctrine.

Correct understanding: this is a traditional devotional marker that should be treated as medium confidence pending academic or museum confirmation, per `REFERENCE_INVOKED_NAMES.md`.

## Misconception 6: Reciting his name or invoking "Niệm Phật Tam Muội" guarantees instant enlightenment.

Correct understanding: his own account in Kinh Lăng Nghiêm describes attainment through sustained, sincere practice across many lifetimes, not an instant or effortless shortcut.

## Misconception 7: Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát is related to Địa Tạng Bồ Tát through shared "great" (Đại) honorific language.

Correct understanding: there is no scriptural relationship of equivalence; the shared honorific is coincidental and must not imply a shared identity or role.

## Misconception 8: Every Buddhist tradition venerates Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát identically.

Correct understanding: Chinese, Vietnamese, and Japanese receptions differ in devotional emphasis; Theravada does not include this figure canonically.

# FAQ

2. **Is Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát the same as Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát?** No. They are two distinct bodhisattvas representing distinct qualities - compassion and wisdom-power - who together flank A Di Đà Phật.
3. **What does "Đại Thế Chí" mean?** Traditionally rendered as "attainment of great power/strength," understood in Vietnamese devotional sources as spiritual power - the power of wisdom, merit, and diligent cultivation.
4. **What is the Sanskrit name?** Mahāsthāmaprāpta.
5. **What is the Chinese name?** 大勢至菩薩, Dàshìzhì Púsà.
6. **Where does he stand in the Tây Phương Tam Thánh?** Conventionally at A Di Đà Phật's side opposite Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát, though exact left/right placement can vary by regional artistic convention.
7. **What does he hold?** Commonly a green or blue lotus flower, distinguishing him from Quán Thế Âm's willow branch and vase of pure water.
8. **What is on his crown?** Traditional sources describe a precious vase said to hold wisdom-light, as distinguished from Quán Thế Âm's crown bearing a small seated Buddha image; treat this as medium-confidence iconographic detail.
9. **Is Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát currently a Buddha?** No, he is presented as a bodhisattva in the core Tây Phương Tam Thánh framing; a separate prophecy tradition describes a distant future Buddhahood.
10. **What is the Kinh Lăng Nghiêm chapter associated with him?** The Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát Niệm Phật Viên Thông Chương, his own first-person account of attaining Niệm Phật Tam Muội (Buddha-recollection samadhi).
11. **What is Niệm Phật Tam Muội?** A state of realization Đại Thế Chí describes attaining through sustained, unbroken mindfulness of the Buddha across many lifetimes.
12. **What is the mother-and-child teaching?** A simile from his Kinh Lăng Nghiêm account illustrating how mutual, sustained remembrance between practitioner and Buddha leads to lasting connection; must always be paraphrased, not quoted verbatim, and cited to its source chapter.
13. **Does his teaching promise instant enlightenment?** No; his own account describes cultivation across many lifetimes, not an instant shortcut.
14. **Is he related to Địa Tạng Bồ Tát?** No meaningful scriptural relationship exists beyond both being Mahayana bodhisattvas; do not imply shared identity from the "great" honorific.
15. **Can he be shown holding weapons or in combat poses?** No; "great power" refers to spiritual power, not martial capability.
16. **Is he the same as Văn Thù Sư Lợi Bồ Tát?** No; both are associated with wisdom, but they are distinct figures with distinct iconography and sources.
17. **What sutras document him?** Kinh Quán Vô Lượng Thọ, Kinh A Di Đà, Kinh Lăng Nghiêm, and the assembly listing in Kinh Pháp Hoa.
18. **Is the "future Buddha" prophecy part of the core Pure Land sutras?** No; it derives from the separate Kinh Bi Hoa tradition and must be explicitly labeled as such whenever referenced.
19. **What should a script do if it needs to mention both Đại Thế Chí and Quán Thế Âm?** Explicitly name them as two different bodhisattvas and state their distinct symbolic roles (wisdom-power versus compassion).
20. **Can fictional dialogue be written for him?** Only if clearly illustrative and not presented as canonical scripture, and grounded in a paraphrase of his documented Kinh Lăng Nghiêm account.
21. **What visual palette fits?** The series' restrained Tây Phương Tam Thánh palette: warm gold, muted temple red, soft white, gentle blue-green for the lotus.
22. **Can he be depicted alone, without A Di Đà Phật and Quán Thế Âm?** Yes, when the scene specifically illustrates his own Kinh Lăng Nghiêm teaching account, but his relationship to the Tây Phương Tam Thánh should still be accurately described in accompanying narration.
23. **Where should a writer check before using this character?** This document, CB_BUD_004 (for the A Di Đà Phật relationship), `REFERENCE_INVOKED_NAMES.md` entry 6, and any relevant knowledge packet on Kinh Lăng Nghiêm if produced.
24. **What is the greatest risk in depicting Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát?** Conflating him visually or doctrinally with Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát.
25. **What is the second greatest risk?** Presenting the Kinh Bi Hoa future-Buddhahood prophecy as his current status rather than a distinct, distant-future, separately sourced tradition.

## Reusable QA Checklist

- Does the asset clearly distinguish Đại Thế Chí Bồ Tát from Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát, stating both figures' distinct symbolic roles?

- Does the asset preserve the correct Tây Phương Tam Thánh grouping and relative roles when depicted together with A Di Đà Phật and Quán Thế Âm Bồ Tát?
