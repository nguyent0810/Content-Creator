## Document Role

This Character Bible is the source of truth for **Kiên Lao Địa Thần**, the guardian earth-spirit who appears in Chapter 11 of Kinh Địa Tạng Bồ Tát Bổn Nguyện ("Địa Thần Hộ Pháp" / "Dharma Protection by the Earth Spirit"). It exists to give this minor but visually and thematically valuable figure a stable, correctly sourced identity ahead of any future episode centered on Chapter 11 (anticipated Episode Catalog item on "Địa thần hộ pháp"), and to resolve a naming ambiguity: the chapter's title names the chapter's topic, not the character's own name.

This document is not a script and not an invitation to elevate Kiên Lao Địa Thần to bodhisattva status or to a level of devotional importance equal to Địa Tạng Bồ Tát.

# Character Identity

## Canonical Name

**Kiên Lao Địa Thần** (堅牢地神). "Kiên Lao" means firm, solid, or unshakeable; "Địa Thần" means earth spirit/earth deity. The name itself is a description of character: steadfastness.

## Alternative Names

- "Firm and Stable Earth Spirit" / "Firm and Stable Earth Deity" / "Firm and Stable Earth God" (English renderings used across translations)
- 堅牢地神 (Chinese/Han source form)

## Name NOT to use as an alias

- **"Địa Thần Hộ Pháp"** is the title of Chapter 11 itself ("Dharma Protection by the Earth Spirit"), not this character's personal name. Many secondary web sources use the two interchangeably; this document requires they be kept distinct. When referring to the chapter, say "Chapter 11, Địa Thần Hộ Pháp." When referring to the character, say "Kiên Lao Địa Thần."

## Chinese

堅牢地神. Confidence: high, directly sourced from the chapter text across multiple translation traditions.

## Sanskrit / Pali

No standard Sanskrit or Pali form is established for this specific figure in the sources reviewed for this document. Do not assert one without further research.

## Vietnamese

Canonical form: **Kiên Lao Địa Thần**. No common alternate Vietnamese devotional name was found in the sources reviewed; if one surfaces in future research, add it here with a source citation.

## English

Preferred working form: **the Firm and Stable Earth Spirit**, or simply **Kiên Lao Địa Thần** with a brief gloss on first use ("a guardian earth-spirit described in Chapter 11").

## Honorific Titles

- Guardian earth-spirit and Dharma-protector (hộ pháp) of those who keep, read, recite, and make offerings in connection with this sutra and Ksitigarbha's image.

## Common Nicknames

None documented with source confidence. Avoid inventing a nickname.

## Common Misidentifications

Kiên Lao Địa Thần must not be misidentified as:

- **Địa Tạng Bồ Tát.** The shared character "Địa" (earth) in both names is a frequent source of confusion for new viewers, but the two figures occupy entirely different tiers: Địa Tạng Bồ Tát is a bodhisattva of vast vow; Kiên Lao Địa Thần is a guardian spirit who testifies to and protects those devoted to that vow. Never present them as equivalent or interchangeable.
- **The chapter title "Địa Thần Hộ Pháp."** As above - the chapter title names the topic, not this character's personal name. Do not write "Địa Thần Hộ Pháp said..." when the sutra text is describing Kiên Lao Địa Thần's speech.
- **Thổ Địa / Thổ Công**, the household earth god of Vietnamese and Chinese folk religion, commonly worshipped at home or land shrines. Thổ Địa/Thổ Công belongs to a separate, older, independent folk-religious tradition that predates and exists apart from this sutra. If a comparison between the two is made for educational purposes, it must be explicitly labeled as a comparison between a scriptural figure (Kiên Lao Địa Thần, specific to this sutra) and a separate folk-devotional tradition (Thổ Địa/Thổ Công) - the same layering discipline CB_BUD_001 applies when distinguishing Vietnamese Địa Tạng devotion from Japanese Jizo devotion.
- A wealth god or fortune deity. Some low-quality commercial/SEO websites conflate earth-spirit figures with folk wealth deities; this is not supported by the sutra and must not be imported into channel content.

# Historical & Canonical Status

## Historical Confidence

- **High:** Kiên Lao Địa Thần is a named figure appearing in Chapter 11 of Kinh Địa Tạng Bồ Tát Bổn Nguyện, confirmed across at least two to three independent Vietnamese-language sources plus the chapter's consistent presence across English translations.
- **Medium:** the specific practical details attributed to this chapter in some translations/commentaries - building a small shrine of earth, stone, bamboo, or wood, or casting an image of Ksitigarbha in gold, silver, copper, or iron - have not been confirmed against a primary source edition as of the 2026 research pass. Label this detail explicitly as "in some translations/commentaries" if used; do not present it as a required or universally attested practice.
- **Low unless sourced:** any claim linking Kiên Lao Địa Thần directly and formally to the folk Thổ Địa/Thổ Công tradition as the "same figure" rather than a loose thematic parallel.

## Canonical Appearance

Primary canonical appearance: **Kinh Địa Tạng Bồ Tát Bổn Nguyện, Chapter 11 ("Địa Thần Hộ Pháp")**.

## Legend vs Canon

- **Canonical scripture (this sutra):** Kiên Lao Địa Thần's testimony about Ksitigarbha's vow, and his vow to protect practitioners, as recorded in Chapter 11.
- **Commentarial tradition:** elaborations on the earth-spirit's role and on shrine/practice details found in some translations and Dharma talks (e.g., Hsuan Hua's commentary tradition); label as commentary.
- **Folk tradition (separate):** Thổ Địa/Thổ Công household earth-god worship. Keep clearly distinct from this scriptural figure.
- **Modern interpretation:** the earth as a metaphor for quiet, foundational, unglamorous support - a legitimate and encouraged modern reflective reading, provided it is labeled as modern reflection and does not replace the doctrinal content.

# Role in Kinh Địa Tạng Bồ Tát Bổn Nguyện

## Narrative Function in Chapter 11

Kiên Lao Địa Thần addresses the Buddha, explaining that across countless ages he has venerated many bodhisattvas of great spiritual power, but has never encountered a vow as deep and enduring as Ksitigarbha's toward beings across countless lifetimes. He then vows: in future ages, wherever a good man or woman keeps this sutra and an image of Ksitigarbha in their home, reads and recites the sutra, and makes offerings before the image, he will use his own spiritual power to protect that person and household day and night - so that flood, fire, theft, and calamities both major and minor do not befall them at all, not merely that they recover from such things. The Buddha confirms and praises his power, explaining that virtually everything that grows in Jambudvipa - grass, trees, sand, stone, rice, hemp, bamboo, reeds, grain, and gems - exists and endures because of the earth spirit's power, and extends the same protective charge to the retinues of Sakra (Indra) and Brahma alongside him.

## Relationship to Ksitigarbha Bodhisattva

The relationship is one of **testimony and voluntary service, not partnership of equal status**. Kiên Lao Địa Thần explicitly states that his own vow, though vast among earth spirits, does not compare to Ksitigarbha's - he is a witness to and supporter of that vow, not a co-equal figure. His devotion functions structurally like the ghost kings' devotion in Chapter 8 and the Dharma-protector role played by devas elsewhere in the sutra: protective service offered in response to Ksitigarbha's example, not independent spiritual authority.

## Symbolic Relationship to Ksitigarbha's Own Name

The chapter's placement of an "earth spirit" alongside "Earth Store/Earth Treasury" (Địa Tạng) Bodhisattva is thematically resonant: the earth is the silent, load-bearing foundation that allows all growth, exactly paralleling the packet's existing "earth as patience, endurance, support" reading of Ksitigarbha's own name (see KP_BUD_001, "Ksitigarbha as Earth Treasury"). This parallel may be used as an interpretive bridge but should be labeled as an interpretive/symbolic reading, not asserted as an explicit textual claim that the sutra draws this comparison itself.

# Character Psychology (Narrative Role, Not Invented Personality)

Kiên Lao Địa Thần is a minor scriptural figure with a single documented speech and vow. Do not invent extensive personality, backstory, or inner conflict. What the text supports:

- **Character concept:** steady, humble, always underfoot, never seeking credit - a spirit whose entire documented statement is in service of praising another (Ksitigarbha) and offering protection to others (future practitioners), not advancing his own status.
- **Tone:** solemn, sincere, unpretentious.
- **Motivation (as shown):** reverence for Ksitigarbha's vow, and a wish to extend practical protection to those who follow the sutra's teaching.

Do not write extended fictional dialogue for Kiên Lao Địa Thần beyond a paraphrase of his documented testimony and vow. Do not give him a personal quest, a rivalry, or a redemption arc - none of this is supported by source material.

# Symbols and Visual Identity

## Visual Rules

- Kiên Lao Địa Thần may be visualized through earth-associated imagery: soil, stone, roots, growing plants, a grounded and stable figure rather than a floating or radiant one - visually distinct from Ksitigarbha's own staff-and-jewel iconography, to avoid the two figures blending together on screen.
- Facial expression and posture: calm, humble, steady - not commanding, not flashy, not seeking attention. This is a character whose entire narrative purpose is quiet, foundational service.
- Palette: earth brown, umber, stone gray, moss green as accent - consistent with the series' existing earth-toned palette, but visually distinguishable from Ksitigarbha's own charcoal/muted-gold/temple-red palette so the two are not confused at a glance.
- If shown alongside Ksitigarbha, use scale and composition (Kiên Lao Địa Thần lower, smaller, or in a supporting position) to visually reinforce the hierarchical relationship documented in the text, without making the earth spirit appear diminished or disrespected.

## Forbidden Depictions

- Kiên Lao Địa Thần depicted as equal in stature or devotional importance to Địa Tạng Bồ Tát.
- Kiên Lao Địa Thần depicted as a wealth god, fortune deity, or household luck charm.
- Kiên Lao Địa Thần merged visually or narratively with folk Thổ Địa/Thổ Công altar statues without an explicit "separate folk tradition" label.
- Kiên Lao Địa Thần used as a decorative background element stripped of narrative or doctrinal context.
- Any depiction implying his protection is an unconditional insurance guarantee against all harm regardless of the practitioner's own conduct or circumstances (consistent with KP_BUD_001's Devotional Benefit Rule).

## Objects and Setting

Approved: soil, stone, roots, grain, bamboo, a modest home shrine or a simple potted plant (as a humble, non-acquisitive stand-in for "keeping the sutra and an image at home" - avoiding any implication that an expensive statue is required), a grounded, low-to-the-earth visual register.

Use with explicit "in some translations/commentaries" labeling if depicted: a small shrine built of earth, stone, bamboo, or wood, or an image of Ksitigarbha cast in gold, silver, copper, or iron - since this specific practice detail is not yet confirmed against a primary source edition.

Avoid: thrones, crowns, or any visual marker of worldly kingship or wealth.

# Common Misconceptions

## Misconception 1: Kiên Lao Địa Thần is another name for Địa Tạng Bồ Tát.

Correct understanding: they are different figures at different tiers. Địa Tạng Bồ Tát is the sutra's central bodhisattva; Kiên Lao Địa Thần is a guardian earth-spirit who testifies to and supports that bodhisattva's vow.

## Misconception 2: "Địa Thần Hộ Pháp" is this character's name.

Correct understanding: "Địa Thần Hộ Pháp" is the title of Chapter 11. The character's name is Kiên Lao Địa Thần.

## Misconception 3: Kiên Lao Địa Thần is the same figure as the household Thổ Địa/Thổ Công worshipped in Vietnamese folk religion.

Correct understanding: these are separate traditions - a specific scriptural figure in this sutra versus an independent folk-religious household deity. A comparison may be drawn for educational purposes but must be explicitly labeled as such.

## Misconception 4: Keeping this sutra and Ksitigarbha's image at home is a guaranteed insurance policy against flood, fire, and theft.

Correct understanding: this is traditional devotional teaching describing the vow of a guardian spirit, not a verifiable or unconditional guarantee; present per KP_BUD_001's Devotional Benefit Rule.

## Misconception 5: Kiên Lao Địa Thần is a wealth or fortune deity.

Correct understanding: no source reviewed supports this; his documented role is protection from calamity (flood, fire, theft) in connection with Dharma practice, not the granting of wealth.

# FAQ

1. **Who is Kiên Lao Địa Thần?** A guardian earth-spirit who appears in Chapter 11, testifies to the depth of Ksitigarbha's vow, and vows to protect practitioners of this sutra.
2. **Is Kiên Lao Địa Thần the same as Địa Tạng Bồ Tát?** No. Different figure, different tier - a supporting guardian spirit, not a bodhisattva.
3. **Is "Địa Thần Hộ Pháp" his name?** No, that is the chapter's title. His name is Kiên Lao Địa Thần.
4. **Is he the same as the household Thổ Địa/Thổ Công?** No. Related in theme (both are earth deities) but from separate traditions; label any comparison explicitly.
5. **What does he promise to practitioners?** Protection from flood, fire, theft, and calamity for those who keep, read, recite, and make offerings in connection with this sutra and Ksitigarbha's image - framed as traditional devotional teaching, not a guarantee.
6. **Can he be shown as a wealth god?** No. This is not supported by the source material and is a forbidden depiction.
7. **How should he look, visually?** Grounded, humble, earth-toned - visually distinct from Ksitigarbha's own staff-and-jewel iconography.
8. **Is the "small home shrine" detail confirmed scripture?** It appears in some translations/commentaries but is not yet confirmed against a primary source edition - label accordingly if used.
9. **Does he have his own devotional cult independent of Ksitigarbha?** Not documented in the sources reviewed; his role in this sutra is specifically in service of and testimony to Ksitigarbha's vow.
10. **Where should a writer check before using this character?** This document, KP_BUD_001 Chapter 11 knowledge map, and the Continuity Registry.
