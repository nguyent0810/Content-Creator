# Domain Guide: Hình Sự — Luật Hình Sự, Án Có Thật & Tội Phạm Học — Editorial Handbook for AI-Assisted Content

Status: **active foundation** (upgraded from `planned` 2026-07-22). This guide unblocks the domain per `DOMAIN_SPECIFICATION/NEW_DOMAIN_PLAYBOOK.md`'s Activation Gate. Written in the same spirit as `DOMAINS/BUDDHISM/BUDDHIST_GUIDE.md` and `DOMAINS/FENG_SHUI/DOMAIN_GUIDE.md` — a working editorial constitution for AI agents, not a finished legal treatise. This is the highest-risk domain built for this channel so far (`risk_level: critical` in `DOMAIN_MANIFEST.md`, one tier above Tử Vi/Phong Thủy's `high`): unlike FS, this domain routinely touches **real, identifiable, sometimes-living people** — victims, the accused, the convicted, and organized-crime figures — where getting a boundary wrong can mean defamation, contempt of an active case, re-traumatizing a real victim's family, or providing an operational template for real harm. Read this entire guide before producing any asset in this domain, not just the section that seems relevant.

## 1. Scope of the Guide

This domain (`CL` — domain code kept as originally registered; the separate `TC` — True Crime — skeleton is **folded into this domain** per the channel owner's explicit 2026-07-22 decision to combine both, the same way `FS` combines Tử Vi and Phong Thủy. `DOMAINS/TRUE_CRIME/` remains on disk as an inactive, superseded skeleton — do not activate it separately or create parallel content there; see "Related Documents" for the formal deprecation note) covers five content pillars, explicitly locked in by the channel owner:

1. **Luật Hình Sự (Criminal Law Explainer)** — how Vietnamese criminal law works: crime categories, procedure, rights of the accused, how a case moves from investigation to verdict. Educational/explainer register, not case-narrative.
2. **Án Đã Xử / Phá Án (Solved True Crime)** — narrating real, already-adjudicated criminal cases: the investigation, the trial, the outcome.
3. **Vụ Án Chưa Có Lời Giải (Unsolved Cases)** — real cases with no final legal resolution; the highest-precision-required pillar for claim hedging (§5).
4. **Chân Dung Sát Nhân / Tội Phạm Nổi Tiếng (Criminal Profiles)** — biographical/psychological profiles of notorious convicted criminals.
5. **Tổ Chức Tội Phạm (Organized Crime — mafia, hội Tam Hoàng/Triads, and comparable syndicates)** — the history, structure, and downfall of criminal organizations.

This is not a legal-advice service, not a vigilante-justice platform, not a venue for accusing anyone of a crime the courts have not confirmed, and not a source of operational instruction for criminal method. It is educational and narrative content about how crime, law, and justice actually work — produced with the same non-exploitation standard as the rest of this channel, extended with the additional real-person protections this subject matter requires.

## 2. Jurisdiction & Canonical Legal Framework

- **Primary jurisdiction for Pillar 1 (Luật Hình Sự):** the Socialist Republic of Vietnam. Canonical legal text: **Bộ luật Hình sự 2015** (sửa đổi, bổ sung 2017), cross-referenced with **Bộ luật Tố tụng Hình sự 2015** for procedure. Do not describe Vietnamese law using foreign legal concepts that do not exist in the Vietnamese system (e.g., do not say "pleading the fifth," "grand jury," or "Miranda rights" as if they apply in Vietnam — note the analogous Vietnamese right if one exists, or state plainly that the concept is foreign).
- **Pillars 2-5 may draw on any jurisdiction** (Vietnamese and international cases both), but **every case must state which country's legal system it occurred under**, in the episode itself, before any procedural claim is made — a "trial," a "verdict," a "life sentence," and an "appeal" mean structurally different things in Vietnam, the U.S., the U.K., Hong Kong, Japan, etc. Never imply a foreign case's procedure generalizes to Vietnamese law, or vice versa.
- Common-law vs. civil-law distinctions (e.g., jury trial vs. judge panel, precedent vs. codified statute) must be named explicitly when a case's procedure depends on which system it happened under.

## 3. Source Priority Hierarchy

1. **Official primary records** — court verdicts (bản án), indictments (cáo trạng), government statutes, official law-enforcement statements/press releases.
2. **Established investigative journalism** — named, reputable outlets with documented editorial/fact-checking standards (domestic and international), and named journalists/authors with a documented track record on the specific case.
3. **Academic criminology, legal scholarship, and peer-reviewed forensic/psychological literature** — used for procedural, systemic, and psychological-pattern claims (never for asserting a specific unconvicted individual's guilt).
4. **Documented true-crime books/long-form documentaries with citations** — usable once cross-checked against tiers 1-3; a true-crime book's narrative flourishes are not themselves a source for a factual claim.
5. **Popular secondary compilations (Wikipedia-tier, wiki-crime sites, aggregator articles)** — usable only for narrative color and cross-checking, never as the sole source for an identity claim, a guilt claim, or a victim-identifying detail.

**Explicitly forbidden as sources, regardless of how confident they sound:** anonymous forums (Reddit-tier speculation, imageboards), unverified social-media claims, doxxing/stalking sites, "conspiracy" true-crime channels with no citation discipline, and any source whose primary content is unmasking a private individual's identity or location. Do not invent a case detail, a name, a date, or a quote. If a claim cannot be cross-checked across at least two independent tier-1-to-3 sources, mark it explicitly as single-sourced/lower-confidence — same confidence-tiering discipline as the Buddhism and FS domains.

## 4. The Core Boundary: Presumption of Innocence & Legal-Status Precision

This is the single most important structural rule in this guide — the direct analogue of FS's §4 "explaining a tradition is not performing a reading," except the failure mode here is worse: **stating or implying a living person committed a crime before a final conviction is defamation, not just an editorial lapse.**

**Operational rule, no exceptions:**

- A living person may be described as having committed a crime **only** after a **final, non-appealable conviction** (bản án đã có hiệu lực pháp luật — meaning the appeal window has passed or the highest appeal has been decided). Before that point, use precise legal-status language: **bị tình nghi** (suspected), **bị can** (charged, under investigation), **bị cáo** (charged, on trial) — never "kẻ giết người," "hung thủ," or any guilt-asserting noun.
- After a final conviction, the person may be named and described factually as convicted — but if the case is still under a live appeal (kháng cáo) or extraordinary review (giám đốc thẩm, tái thẩm) at time of publication, that status must be disclosed in the same piece.
- For **historical or deceased perpetrators with a well-documented, uncontested final conviction**, direct factual statement is permitted (mirrors FS §4a Format 2's retrospective allowance) — but "well-documented" still requires tier-1-3 sourcing, not popular repetition of a rumor.
- **The "net impression" test governs the whole piece, not just individual sentences:** a script that technically hedges every sentence ("bị cáo được cho là...") while a sensational title, thumbnail-text, tone, or narrative structure leads an ordinary viewer to conclude guilt is established fact **fails this rule** regardless of the hedged wording used inside. QA must evaluate the piece's overall impression, not just search-and-replace for hedge phrases.
- This rule applies with equal force to organizations (§4a Format 3) — an organization may be named as documented "tổ chức tội phạm" only where that designation itself comes from a tier-1/tier-2 source (law-enforcement designation, court finding, or well-established investigative journalism), not from popular reputation alone.

### 4a. Three Permitted Formats (mirrors FS §4a's structure)

**Format 1 — Finally-convicted persons.** May be named and described factually as having committed the crime, per §4's rule above. This is the normal case for Pillar 2 (Án Đã Xử) content.

**Format 2 — Unsolved cases with publicly-named persons of interest.** A person may be named as a suspect/person-of-interest **only if** tier-1-to-3 sources already publicly connect them to the case. The content must:
- Present this as **one theory among the theories the sourced record actually contains** — never as the channel's own conclusion, and never as the only theory if credible sources offer others.
- Use theory-labeling language throughout ("một giả thuyết được đưa ra bởi [nguồn] là...", "cảnh sát từng coi X là nghi phạm nhưng chưa từng truy tố"), never converge the episode's ending on "and that's who really did it."
- Never invent a suspect or a piece of evidence the cited sources do not contain, and never imply guilt through structural placement (e.g., saving one name for a dramatic reveal beat implies the channel has concluded something the sources have not).

**Format 3 — Documented criminal organizations.** An organization with a tier-1/tier-2-sourced criminal designation (mafia family, Tam Hoàng/Triad society, cartel, gang) may be named and its documented history discussed — but naming the organization does not license naming an *individual, unconvicted* member as guilty of a specific act; §4's per-person rule still applies to every individual mentioned.

None of these formats license identifying a **living private victim** beyond what §6 permits, or providing operational detail per §8.

## 5. Certainty, Theory-Labeling & Anti-Speculation Rules

- Every claim about an unsolved case's likely perpetrator, motive, or mechanism must be sourced and explicitly labeled as a theory, a hypothesis, or a piece of evidence — never narrated as settled fact.
- Where the sourced record contains multiple competing theories, present the real range, not just the most dramatic one — omitting a credible competing theory to make the episode's preferred one look stronger is itself a QA failure (mirrors the multi-school-plurality discipline already established in FS §2 for Bát Trạch vs. Huyền Không).
- Never invent a theory, a piece of forensic detail, or a witness statement not present in cited sources, even for narrative smoothness.
- Procedural facts (dates, charges, verdicts, sentences) may be stated as settled once confirmed by a tier-1 source; only the *contested* elements (who did it, why, motive, disputed forensic interpretation) require hedged/theory language.

## 6. Victim Dignity & Privacy Boundary (hard rule, non-negotiable)

- **Never** publish a victim's current home address, a surviving family member's personal contact information, or private/unpublished photographs.
- Use only names and details already in the public court record or that the victim/family has themselves made public; do not use investigative techniques to surface a victim's identity beyond what sourcing already establishes.
- **Minor victims:** never name, never show an identifiable image, and never describe identifying biographical detail — regardless of whether that detail is technically present in a public court record. This exception-free rule applies even when adult-victim naming would otherwise be permitted for the same case.
- **Sexual-violence cases:** apply the same minor-level anonymization discipline to adult victims unless the victim has publicly self-identified; never speculate about or narrate assault detail beyond what is necessary for factual/legal understanding.
- Do not dramatize or reenact a victim's final moments, suffering, or death in exploitative or graphic sensory detail. Facts and consequence carry the narrative weight, not manufactured suffering.
- Do not use real crime-scene or autopsy photography; if visual reference to a real scene is needed for the video layer (out of this Guide's direct scope but noted for downstream Visual Engine use), it must be handled outside script content and is never assumed default-permitted.

## 7. No Legal Advice Boundary (hard rule, non-negotiable)

- **Never** tell a viewer what to do about their own specific legal situation, charge, or dispute — no "nếu bạn đang bị điều tra vì tội X, hãy làm Y."
- **Do** explain law and procedure in general, educational terms — what a legal concept means, how a process typically works, what rights the law grants generally.
- This rule exists independently of, and in addition to, my own platform-level constraint against providing advice that functions as licensed legal counsel for a specific person's situation; it does not get relaxed for stylistic or engagement reasons, exactly as FS §6 states for financial advice.

## 8. Organized Crime Non-Glorification & No-Operational-Detail Boundary (hard rule, non-negotiable, highest sensitivity)

This is this domain's most sensitive rule — the analogue of FS §8's death/lifespan rule — because the specific failure mode (functioning as a how-to guide or a recruitment advertisement) causes real-world harm beyond the channel.

- **Never** provide operational detail that could function as instruction: specific recruitment scripts/targeting methods, money-laundering mechanics, weapon- or drug-sourcing logistics, specific law-enforcement-evasion techniques, or coded communication systems presented usably rather than historically.
- **Never** frame a criminal figure or organization heroically, aspirationally, or as "cool" — the narrative center of gravity must always be consequence (arrests, deaths, imprisonment, harm done to victims and society), never lifestyle glamorization, wealth-flaunting, or a "rise" narrative without a proportionate "fall."
- **Never** reproduce gang/triad insignia, recruitment materials, or symbols in a form usable for real-world identification or recruitment (naming and historically describing a symbol is fine; providing a usable rendering or "how to recognize a member near you" framing is not).
- **Do** present organized crime historically and sociologically: why it emerged, what social/economic conditions enabled it, how law enforcement or internal collapse dismantled it — an analytical lens, not an adventure narrative.

## 9. Anti-Sensationalism / Anti-Exploitation Standard

Commercial true-crime media has a well-documented pattern of manufacturing shock for engagement at the expense of victims' dignity and factual accuracy. This channel rejects that pattern at the same level of firmness as `CORE_OS/SEO_ENGINE.md`'s anti-fear-bait rule and FS §9's Anti-Fear-Sales Standard.

**Forbidden patterns:** "SỰ THẬT RÙNG RỢN," "KINH HOÀNG," "KHÔNG AI DÁM KỂ," countdown-of-horror framing, thumbnail/title implying guilt not yet legally established (§4), or treating a real victim's death as entertainment shock value rather than a fact to be handled with gravity.

**Required instead:** the same calm, reverent, non-manipulative voice already established channel-wide (`CORE_OS/BRAND_BIBLE.md`); genuine curiosity and factual gravity as the hook mechanism, never manufactured dread; treatment of real victims and their families with the same compassion-first standard `BUDDHIST_GUIDE.md` §13 establishes for grief content generally.

## 10. Layered Interpretation Framework

Following the same layered approach proven in the Buddhism and FS domains:

- **Legal/procedural layer:** what actually happened procedurally — charges, trial, verdict, sentence, appeal status; what the relevant law says.
- **Narrative/case layer:** the human story — timeline, investigation, key turns — told with factual discipline, not embellishment.
- **Societal-reflective layer:** what the case reveals about systemic issues (investigative gaps, social conditions, legal-reform debates, institutional failure) — presented analytically, never as victim-blaming or as a verdict on a legal system's legitimacy the channel is not positioned to render.

No single layer should erase the others: do not present a case as "just" a procedural timeline stripped of its human weight, and do not present it as "just" a dramatic story stripped of legal precision.

## 12. Terminology and Claims Never to Use

- Never state or imply a living person is guilty of a crime before a final, non-appealable conviction (§4).
- Never provide advice that functions as legal counsel for a viewer's own situation (§7).
- Never provide operational detail usable for committing, evading, or recruiting into crime (§8).
- Never identify a minor victim, or an adult victim beyond what public record/self-disclosure permits, or publish a private individual's current location/contact information (§6).
- Never present an unsolved case's leading theory as a settled conclusion (§5).
- Never use manufactured shock, gore, or countdown-of-horror framing to drive engagement (§9).
- Never glamorize, romanticize, or present as aspirational any criminal figure or organized-crime lifestyle (§8).

## 13. Domain QA Checklist (used by `DOMAIN_QA/DOMAIN_QA_POLICY.md`)

1. Does every claim about a living, not-finally-convicted person use correct hedged legal-status language, and does the *piece as a whole* (title, hook, structure — the "net impression" test) avoid implying guilt regardless of hedged wording (§4)?
2. For unsolved cases, is every theory sourced and labeled as a theory, with competing credible theories represented rather than omitted (§5)?
3. Does any line publish a victim's private/current location/contact info, identify a minor victim, or exceed public-record detail for an adult victim (§6)?
4. Does any line function as legal advice for the viewer's own situation (§7)?
5. Does any line provide operational crime/evasion/recruitment detail, or glamorize a criminal figure/organization (§8)?
6. Does any line use manufactured shock, gore, or sensational framing (§9)?
7. Does the content hold the legal/narrative/societal-reflective layers together without erasing any one (§10)?
8. Are all terms consistent with `DOMAIN_GLOSSARY.md`, and does every jurisdiction-dependent claim name its jurisdiction (§2)?

## 14. Editorial Process: Transform First, Refuse Only When Impossible

Mirrors FS §14's posture exactly: **an editor who transforms risk, not a gatekeeper who only refuses.** Losing a locked claim type must never mean losing the hook, the mystery, or the content goal — only the specific unsafe sentence gets rewritten.

**Per-boundary transform patterns:**

- **§4 Premature guilt:** "X là hung thủ" → "X bị cáo buộc/bị coi là nghi phạm chính, theo [nguồn]" or, if unconvicted, reframe the hook around the *mystery/investigation* rather than a name ("Cảnh sát đã theo dõi một người trong nhiều năm — nhưng chưa bao giờ đủ bằng chứng để buộc tội").
- **§6 Victim privacy:** a graphic/identifying victim detail → shift the narrative weight to the investigation, the consequence, or the systemic response, naming only what public record already establishes.
- **§7 Legal advice:** "nếu bạn gặp trường hợp này, hãy làm X" → "về mặt lý thuyết, pháp luật quy định X trong trường hợp này" (general educational framing, not directed advice).
- **§8 Organized crime glamorization:** a "how they got away with it" beat → reframe as "how the pattern was eventually caught," centering the systemic/investigative response rather than the technique itself.

**Required 4-step response whenever an idea contains a locked claim type:** (1) name exactly what is invalid; (2) keep everything else — hook, structure, emotional arc; (3) transform into the nearest valid framing above; (4) continue and deliver a complete output — do not stall the pipeline to relitigate the boundary.

**When a transform still requires dropping a specific expression, offer at least three alternative directions:** (1) a mass-appeal/high-engagement direction; (2) a storytelling direction; (3) a deep-analysis direction — same structure as FS §14.

**When refusal is genuinely the only option** (no compliant transform exists — should be rare): state precisely which claim type made it impossible, and offer whatever adjacent creative territory remains open.

The purpose of every rule above is to prevent real, specific harms (defamation, victim re-traumatization, operational crime instruction) — never to make the content bland or overly hedged. A QA pass or draft that comes back so hedged it reads as meaningless has itself failed this section; re-run the transform rather than softening into nothing.
